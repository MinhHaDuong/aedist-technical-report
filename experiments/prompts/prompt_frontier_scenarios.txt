You are an energy systems modeler preparing a reproducible scenario study of Vietnam's thermal power sector evolution to 2050. Design and describe three contrasting scenarios using state-of-the-art open-source energy modeling tools and fresh open primary data.

## Deliverables

### A. Scenario Definitions

Define three internally-consistent scenarios for Vietnam's thermal power fleet:

1. **Reference / PDP8 Baseline**: Implements PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 2023) as-is — coal retained at authorized levels, LNG buildout per plan, gas fields developed on schedule
2. **Accelerated Transition**: Aggressive coal phase-down aligned with Vietnam's JETP commitments ($15.5B pledge), faster LNG ramp, early coal retirements, higher renewable penetration
3. **Gas Bridge**: Maximizes domestic gas (Block B, Cá Voi Xanh) and LNG imports as transitional fuel, moderate coal retirement, gas replaces coal baseload before renewables scale

For each scenario, specify:
- Thermal capacity additions and retirements by fuel type, by 5-year period (2025–2050)
- Fuel supply assumptions: domestic coal/gas production curves, LNG import prices, carbon price trajectory
- Demand growth assumptions: GDP elasticity, industrial load growth, EV penetration
- Policy levers: carbon tax/ETS, coal moratorium dates, renewable targets, gas price regulation
- Key uncertainties and sensitivity parameters

### B. Modeling Framework

Recommend and justify a specific open-source energy model for this study. Evaluate:

| Model | License | Granularity | Vietnam support | Active community |
|-------|---------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| PyPSA | MIT | Hourly dispatch, network | Configurable | Yes |
| OSeMOSYS | Apache-2.0 | Long-term capacity expansion | ASEAN precedents | Yes |
| Calliope | Apache-2.0 | Multi-carrier, spatial | Configurable | Moderate |
| TEMOA | AGPL | Technology-rich, LP | Academic use | Moderate |
| Switch | Apache-2.0 | Investment + dispatch | US-focused but adaptable | Moderate |

For the recommended model:
- Justify why it fits Vietnam's thermal sector analysis
- Specify model version and dependencies
- Describe the temporal and spatial resolution needed
- Identify which model features matter (unit commitment, fuel supply constraints, transmission)

### C. Data Requirements and Sources

For each data input, specify:
- Parameter name and units
- Recommended open primary data source (with URL if available, "URL not verified" if unsure)
- Vintage (most recent available year)
- Fallback source if primary is unavailable
- Any preprocessing or harmonization needed

Key data categories:
1. **Existing fleet**: Plant-level capacity, heat rate, age, remaining life (source: EVN, Global Energy Monitor)
2. **Fuel costs**: Coal (domestic TKV, imported FOB Newcastle), gas (PVN wellhead, LNG spot/contract), carbon prices
3. **Demand projections**: MOIT load forecasts, GDP scenarios from World Bank/IMF
4. **Technology costs**: CAPEX/OPEX for new gas CCGT, coal USC, solar, wind, battery (IRENA, IEA WEO, NREL ATB)
5. **Policy parameters**: JETP milestones, PDP8 targets, national climate commitments (NDC)
6. **Renewable profiles**: Hourly solar/wind capacity factors by province (renewables.ninja, Global Solar Atlas)

### D. Reproducibility Package

Describe the complete reproducibility package:
- Repository structure (model, data, scripts, results)
- Environment specification (conda/pip requirements)
- Run instructions (single command to reproduce all three scenarios)
- Output format: capacity mix, generation mix, emissions, system cost, fuel consumption — all as CSV time series
- Validation: how to cross-check results against EVN historical data

### E. Expected Results Discussion

For each scenario, provide expected qualitative outcomes:
- Total thermal capacity in 2030 and 2050
- Coal share of generation in 2030 and 2050
- Cumulative CO2 emissions 2025–2050
- System cost implications
- Key risks and tipping points

## Quality instructions
- All data sources must be open and accessible (no paywalled databases)
- Prefer government/multilateral primary sources over news articles
- Distinguish between model inputs (data) and model outputs (projections)
- Be specific about parameter values, not just "use IEA data" — specify which IEA publication, table, and year
- The goal is that a graduate student could reproduce this study from your description alone
