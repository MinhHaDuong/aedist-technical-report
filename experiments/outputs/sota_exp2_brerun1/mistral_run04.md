## **1. Sector Overview**

### **1.1 Electricity Mix and Policy Framework**
- **Thermal Power Dominance**: As of May 2026, thermal power (coal, domestic gas, LNG) accounts for **~43% of Vietnam’s total installed capacity** and **~56% of total power generation**. Coal alone provides **33.2% of total capacity (26,757 MW)**, while gas (domestic and LNG) contributes **~10% (8–9 GW)**.
- **Policy Drivers**: The **Revised Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8, 2021–2030, vision to 2050)**, approved in April 2025, sets the following targets:
  - **Coal**: Cap at **30,127 MW by 2030**, with no new coal plants after 2030. All existing coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or retire by 2050.
  - **Gas (Domestic)**: **14,930 MW by 2030**.
  - **LNG**: **22,400 MW by 2030**, with a focus on combined cycle gas turbine (CCGT) technology.
  - **Nuclear**: Reintroduced in PDP8, with Ninh Thuan 1 & 2 (4,000–6,400 MW) planned for 2030–2035.
- **Key Institutions**:
  - **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Oversees energy policy, planning, and regulation.
  - **Vietnam Electricity (EVN)**: State-owned utility, largest buyer and operator of thermal plants.
  - **PetroVietnam (PVN)**: Leads domestic gas and LNG projects.
  - **General Department of Energy**: Coordinates fuel supply and infrastructure.

### **1.2 Current Challenges**
- **Fuel Supply**: Vietnam imports **~25% of its coal** (Australia, Indonesia, China) and faces **LNG price volatility** and **infrastructure delays**.
- **Grid Congestion**: Especially in the north, limiting integration of new thermal and renewable capacity.
- **Environmental and Financial Pressures**: International commitments (JETP, COP26) and OECD tax rules are accelerating the phase-out of coal and pushing for fuel conversion (biomass, ammonia, hydrogen).
- **Project Delays**: Many LNG and gas projects face delays due to financing, land acquisition, and regulatory hurdles.

### **1.3 Technology Trends**
- **Coal**: Most new coal plants use **supercritical (SC) or ultra-supercritical (USC)** technology. Older subcritical plants are being retired or converted.
- **Gas**: CCGT is the dominant technology for new gas and LNG plants, with some open cycle gas turbines (OCGT) for peaking demand.
- **Fuel Conversion**: PDP8 mandates that coal plants convert to biomass/ammonia by 2050, with pilot projects underway.

## **2. Per-Plant Narratives**

### **2.1 Major Operational Coal Plants**
- **Vung Ang 1 & 2 (Ha Tinh)**: Vung Ang 1 (1,200 MW, operational since 2015) and Vung Ang 2 (1,320 MW, inaugurated April 2026) are critical for central Vietnam’s power supply. Vung Ang 2 is a BOT project with Japanese and Korean investors, using ultra-supercritical technology.
- **Vinh Tan 4 (Binh Thuan)**: 1,200 MW, operational since 2017, developed by EVN.
- **Duyen Hai 1 & 3 (Tra Vinh)**: 2,488 MW, operational since 2016, developed by EVN.

### **2.2 Major Gas/LNG Plants**
- **Nhon Trach 3 & 4 (Dong Nai)**: Vietnam’s first LNG-fired power plants, with a combined capacity of 1,624 MW, inaugurated in December 2025 and operational in early 2026. Developed by PV Power, these plants use GE’s HA-class gas turbines and are designed for future hydrogen co-firing.
- **O Mon 2 (Can Tho)**: Proposed 1,050 MW CCGT plant, with financing expected to close in 2026.

### **2.3 Under Construction**
- **Quang Trach 1 (Quang Binh)**: 1,403 MW coal-fired plant, Unit 1 connected to the grid in April 2026, commercial operation expected May 2026.
- **Long Phu 1 (Soc Trang)**: 1,200 MW coal-fired plant, expected COD in 2026.
- **Na Duong 2 (Lang Son)**: 110 MW subcritical plant, EPC bids submitted, target COD 2026.

### **2.4 Approved/Planned Conversions**
- **Quang Trach 2 (Quang Binh)**: 1,500 MW, approved for conversion from coal to LNG, construction to start Q4 2026.
- **Vung Ang 3 (Ha Tinh)**: Proposed conversion from coal to gas, target COD 2031–2035.

### **2.5 Suspended/Cancelled**
- **Quang Tri (Quang Tri)**: 1,200 MW coal-fired plant, suspended due to financing withdrawal.
- **Vinh Tan 3 (Binh Thuan)**: 1,500 MW, suspended as MOIT evaluates future.
- **Cong Thanh (Thanh Hoa)**: 1,200 MW, suspended due to financing issues.
- **Song Hau II (Hau Giang)**: 2,000 MW, cancelled under PDP8.

### **2.6 Retired**
- No major retirements >30 MWe reported in 2026. Older subcritical plants are being phased out or converted.
