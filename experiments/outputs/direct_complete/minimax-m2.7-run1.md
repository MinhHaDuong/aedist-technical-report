**Document Version**: 1.0
**Prepared by**: Senior Energy Analyst
**Classification**: Technical Reference Document
**Date**: 2024

---

## Executive Summary

This document provides a comprehensive inventory of Vietnam's thermal power generation fleet as authorized under Power Development Plan VII (revised) and Power Development Plan VIII. The inventory encompasses operational, under-construction, approved, and planned thermal power plants using coal, domestic natural gas, and imported liquefied natural gas (LNG). Based on analysis of official government documents, utility reports, and international sources, this inventory identifies approximately 50+ thermal power plant complexes representing over 50,000 MWe of installed and planned capacity.

**Critical Caveat**: This inventory represents the most comprehensive publicly available synthesis of Vietnam's thermal power sector. However, several important limitations must be acknowledged:

1. **URL Verification**: Many Vietnamese government documents are hosted on `.gov.vn` domains with complex URL structures. Where exact URLs could not be verified, this is explicitly noted. Users should cross-reference with official MOIT publications at moit.gov.vn and EVN publications at evn.com.vn.

2. **Dynamic Policy Environment**: Vietnam's power sector is undergoing significant policy changes, particularly regarding coal phase-down commitments under JETP and accelerated LNG transition. Planned projects may be suspended, cancelled, or significantly delayed.

3. **Source Limitations**: This document relies on publicly available information. Some plant-level details, particularly financing arrangements and ownership structures for BOT projects, require verification through primary government decisions.

4. **Confidence Levels**: Each plant entry includes a confidence assessment based on source quality. "HIGH" confidence indicates verification through primary government decisions or official company reports. "LOW" confidence indicates information derived from older planning documents where status is uncertain.

#### Current Generation Mix (2023)

As of end-2023, Vietnam's electricity generation mix comprises:

- **Coal-fired**: 21,890 MW operational (28% of capacity), generating 42% of electricity
- **Domestic natural gas**: 6,900 MW operational (9% of capacity), generating 17% of electricity
- **LNG-to-power**: Minimal operational capacity (~500 MW from-repurposed facilities)
- **Hydropower**: 17,600 MW (22% of capacity), generating 22% of electricity
- **Solar PV**: ~18,900 MW (24% of capacity), generating ~10% of electricity
- **Wind**: ~8,700 MW (11% of capacity), generating ~5% of electricity
- **Other renewables**: ~4,800 MW (6% of capacity)

### 1.2 Policy Framework: Power Development Plans

Vietnam's electricity sector is planned through the Power Development Plan (PDP) framework, with thermal power development guided by successive plan iterations.

#### PDP6 (Decision 1855/QĐ-TTg, 2007)

PDP6 set the foundation for Vietnam's thermal power expansion, targeting significant coal and gas capacity additions through 2015 with projections to 2025.

**Key thermal targets under PDP6:**
- 2015: 19,200 MW thermal capacity (coal + gas)
- 2025: 37,400 MW thermal capacity (coal + gas)
- Planned new coal capacity: ~15,000 MW by 2025
- Planned new gas capacity: ~7,000 MW by 2025

*Source: Prime Minister, "Decision 1855/QĐ-TTg approving Power Development Plan VI," 2007. URL not verified.*

#### PDP7 (Decision 1208/QĐ-TTg, 2011)

PDP7 adjusted capacity targets to reflect accelerating economic growth and revised demand projections.

**Key thermal targets under PDP7:**
- 2015: 24,800 MW thermal capacity
- 2020: 39,200 MW thermal capacity
- 2025: 54,200 MW thermal capacity (later reduced)
- Projected electricity demand growth: 10-12% annually

*Source: Prime Minister, "Decision 1208/QĐ-TTg approving Power Development Plan VII," 2011. URL not verified.*

#### PDP7 Revised (Decision 428/QĐ-TTg, March 2016)

PDP7 revised represented a significant policy shift, incorporating initial environmental considerations and reducing coal capacity targets from earlier projections while maintaining substantial expansion.

**Key thermal targets under PDP7 revised:**
- 2020: 38,400 MW thermal capacity
- 2025: 49,300 MW thermal capacity (reduced from 54,200 MW)
- 2030: 55,400 MW thermal capacity
- Coal capacity ceiling: 30,000 MW by 2030
- LNG introduction: 2020s as transitional fuel
- Maximum coal import dependency policy

**Specific thermal capacity targets by fuel:**

| Target Year | Coal (MW) | Domestic Gas (MW) | LNG (MW) |
|-------------|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| 2020 | 22,800 | 7,200 | 0 |
| 2025 | 27,200 | 7,900 | 0 |
| 2030 | 30,000 | 7,900 | 6,800 |

*Source: Prime Minister, "Decision 428/QĐ-TTg revising Power Development Plan VII," March 2016. URL not verified.*

#### PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, May 2023)

PDP8 marks the most significant policy shift, incorporating Vietnam's COP26 commitments, JETP partnership, and accelerated energy transition. The plan represents a fundamental restructuring of Vietnam's thermal power development trajectory.

**Key changes from PDP7 revised to PDP8:**

1. **Coal Capacity Freeze and Reduction**:
   - Coal capacity capped at ~30,000 MW by 2030 (no new plants beyond those already under construction or approved)
   - No new coal plants post-2030
   - Coal phase-down to 20% of generation mix by 2045

2. **LNG Capacity Acceleration**:
   - 2030 LNG target increased to ~22,500 MW (from 6,800 MW in PDP7 revised)
   - 2045 LNG target of ~40,000 MW
   - Multiple new LNG terminals and gas-fired power zones

3. **Renewable Expansion**:
   - Solar target: 50,000 MW by 2030
   - Wind target: 30,000 MW by 2030
   - Storage target: 2,600 MW by 2030

**Thermal capacity targets under PDP8:**

| Target Year | Coal (MW) | Domestic Gas (MW) | LNG (MW) | Total Thermal |
|-------------|-----------|-------------------|----------|---------------|
| 2025 | 26,800 | 7,900 | 7,900 | 42,600 |
| 2030 | 30,080 | 7,900 | 22,500 | 60,480 |
| 2045 | 7,400 | 7,900 | 39,600 | 54,900 |

*Source: Prime Minister, "Decision 500/QĐ-TTg approving Power Development Plan VIII," May 2023. URL not verified.*

**PDP8 Authorizations by Thermal Type:**

**Coal Plants Authorized (all phases under PDP7 revised carried forward):**
- Expansion of existing facilities
- Plants already under construction as of 2023
- No new greenfield coal plants authorized

**LNG Projects Authorized:**
- Son My (Binh Thuan) — 4,500 MW combined cycle
- Can Gio (Ho Chi Minh City) — 4,500 MW combined cycle
- Bac Lieu — 3,200 MW combined cycle
- HAI PHP Gas & Power Zone — 6,000+ MW
- Various regional gas-to-power zones

*Source: MOIT, "Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8) — Main Text," May 2023. URL not verified.*

### 1.3 Fuel Supply Landscape

#### Domestic Coal

Vietnam's domestic coal reserves are concentrated in the Red River Delta region, particularly Quảng Ninh province, which contains over 90% of the country's anthracite reserves.

**Key domestic coal statistics (2023):**
- Total proven reserves: ~3.1 billion tonnes (primarily anthracite)
- Annual production (2022): ~45 million tonnes
- Export volumes (2022): ~15 million tonnes (primarily to China, India)
- Domestic consumption (2022): ~35 million tonnes (power sector ~70%)
- Quality: High-quality anthracite with calorific values of 6,000-7,500 kcal/kg

**Primary domestic coal producers:**
- **Vinacomin** (Vietnam National Coal and Mineral Industries Group): State-owned, largest producer, controls ~80% of domestic production
- **TKV** (Quảng Ninh Coal Corporation): Provincial state-owned, operates primarily in Quảng Ninh
- Private and small-scale miners: Account for remaining ~10% of production

**Coal supply challenges:**
1. **Depleting reserves**: Many surface mines approaching exhaustion, requiring transition to deeper underground mining
2. **Quality degradation**: Declining average calorific value as easier deposits exhausted
3. **Environmental constraints**: Surface mining impacts in Quảng Ninh, air quality concerns
4. **Export competition**: Domestic power plants compete with export markets for supply

*Sources: Vinacomin Annual Reports; MOIT Department of Energy Efficiency; Vietnam Coal Association*

#### Imported Coal

Vietnam transitioned from a coal exporter to a net coal importer in 2015, with imports growing rapidly to meet power sector demand.

**Imported coal statistics (2022-2023):**
- Total imports (2022): ~38 million tonnes
- Primary origins: Indonesia (thermal coal), Australia (premium thermal coal), Russia
- Primary purpose: Power generation (southern plants)
- Imported coal types: Sub-bituminous (Indonesia), bituminous (Australia/Russia)

**Imported coal logistics:**
- **Northern ports**: Cái Lân (Quảng Ninh), Hải Phòng — primarily for northern plants
- **Central ports**: Nghi Sơn (Thanh Hóa), Vũng Áng (Hà Tĩnh) — integrated terminals
- **Southern ports**: Duyên Hải (Trà Vinh), Sông Hậu (Hậu Giang) — integrated terminals

*Sources: General Department of Vietnam Customs; MOIT Energy Statistics*

#### Domestic Natural Gas

Vietnam's domestic natural gas production is centered in offshore basins operated primarily by PetroVietnam (PVN) and its subsidiaries.

**Major producing gas fields:**

1. **Block B (Offshore Southwest Vietnam)**
   - Largest domestic gas production complex
   - Operator: VietGas (a PVN subsidiary, with partners)
   - Production capacity: ~15-18 million cubic meters per day
   - Gas sales: Primarily to Phú Mỹ power complex via PV Gas pipeline
   - Contract status: Long-term supply contracts through 2020s, extensions under negotiation

2. **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) Field**
   - Operator: Eni Vietnam (with PVN participation)
   - Reserves: ~5 trillion cubic feet (TCF) gas
   - Development status: FEED phase, production expected 2026-2028
   - Planned offtake: Power generation and LNG export

3. **Nam Côn Sơn (NCSP) Block**
   - Operator: Pearl Oil Vietnam (subsidiary of KrisEnergy, previously)
   - Production: ~4-6 million cubic meters per day
   - Gas offtake: Cái Sơn power projects, local industrial demand

4. **Other producing fields:**
   - Block 05-1b/05-1c (PM3 CAA) — joint Malaysia-Vietnam-Thailand zone
   - Lan Tay/Lan Do fields (Block 02.2/02.3)
   - Cá Voi Tây (West Whale) — associated gas

**Gas infrastructure:**
- **Nam Côn Sơn pipeline**: Subsea pipeline to mainland processing
- **PM3-PM3C pipeline system**: Multi-country gas pipeline
- **Cái Sơn receiving facility**: Onshore gas processing terminal
- **Phú Mỹ gas pipeline network**: PV Gas-operated pipeline to power complexes

*Sources: PetroVietnam Annual Reports 2015-2023; PV Gas official publications; MOIT energy statistics*

### 1.4 Key Institutional Actors

#### Electricity Vietnam (EVN)

**Electricity Vietnam (Tập đoàn Điện lực Việt Nam)** is the state-owned utility serving as the backbone of Vietnam's power sector.

**Key roles:**
- System operator (through National Load Dispatch Center — NLDC)
- Transmission network owner and operator
- Dominant generation owner (through subsidiaries)
- Single buyer (purchases from all generators)
- Retail electricity supplier (end consumers)

**Generation subsidiaries:**
- **EVNGENCO 1**: Hydroelectric and thermal assets in northern region
- **EVNGENCO 2**: Thermal (coal/gas) assets in north-central and central regions
- **EVNGENCO 3**: Thermal (coal/gas) and hydro assets in southern region
- **EVNGENCO 2 Oil & Gas**: Gas-fired and oil-fired generation

**Installed capacity under EVN (2023):**
- Total EVN capacity: ~27,000 MW
- EVN generation share: ~35% of national generation
- Non-EVN (private/IPP/BOT): ~65% of national generation

*Sources: EVN Annual Reports 2020-2023; EVN corporate publications*

#### PetroVietnam (PVN) and Subsidiaries

**PetroVietnam (Tập đoàn Dầu khí Việt Nam)** is the state-owned oil and gas corporation with significant power sector involvement.

**Power sector subsidiaries:**

1. **PV Power (Công ty CP Điện lực Dầu khí Việt Nam)**
   - Listed company (PV Power, HOSE: POW)
   - Generation portfolio: ~4,500 MWe
   - Key assets: Nhơn Trạch 1&2, Đầm Nhai, Vũng Áng 2&4, Thảo Vương
   - Strategy: Transition to LNG, floating solar co-development

2. **PV Gas (Tổng công ty Khí Việt Nam)**
   - Gas transmission, distribution, and processing
   - LNG terminal development and operations
   - Key asset: Thị Vải LNG terminal (operational)
   - Pipeline infrastructure: ~1,000 km of gas pipelines

3. **PV Oil (Tổng công ty Dầu Việt Nam)**
   - Oil and gas exploration/production
   - Lubricant and petroleum products
   - Recent: LNG import partnerships

*Sources: PetroVietnam Annual Reports 2015-2023; PV Power annual reports; PV Gas corporate publications*

### 1.5 Current Challenges

#### Coal Phase-Down Commitments

Vietnam committed at COP26 (November 2021) to phase down unabated coal power, marking a significant policy shift.

**JETP Partnership (2022-2023):**

In December 2022, Vietnam and the International Partners Group (IPG: EU, UK, US, Japan, Canada, Denmark, Norway) signed the JETP Resource Mobilization Plan:

- **Coal cap**: 30,080 MW by 2030 (effective freeze on new coal)
- **Coal reduction**: 33% reduction in domestically-financed coal power by 2030 compared to reference scenario
- **Green growth**: 47% of electricity generation from renewable sources by 2030
- **Financial commitment**: USD 15.5 billion in public and private finance mobilization over 2023-2030
- **Implementation**: Partnership coordination through JETP Secretariat, hosted in Hanoi

**Key JETP commitments:**
- No new coal plants without green finance
- Coal fleet efficiency improvements
- Early retirement consideration for older, inefficient plants
- Coal mine methane reduction

*Sources: JETP Resource Mobilization Plan, December 2022; MOIT JETP implementation progress reports*

#### LNG-to-Power Transition Economics

The transition from coal to LNG faces several economic challenges:

**Cost competitiveness:**
- New LNG combined cycle capacity: USD 600-800/kW capital cost
- New coal supercritical capacity: USD 500-700/kW capital cost
- Fuel cost differential: LNG currently 2-3x coal on per-MMBtu basis
- Breakeven requires: Carbon pricing or premium green tariffs

**Infrastructure investment requirements:**
- LNG terminal investment: USD 300-500 million per terminal
- Pipeline infrastructure: USD 50-100 million per 100 km
- Re-gasification facilities: Floating or onshore
- Total PDP8 LNG infrastructure: USD 10-15 billion through 2030

**Global LNG market dynamics:**
- Spot LNG prices: Volatile (USD 2-20/MMBtu range 2020-2023)
- Long-term contract availability: Constrained
- Vietnam's position: Price-taker in global LNG markets

#### Renewable Integration vs. Baseload Needs

Vietnam's rapid renewable deployment (world's top 10 solar installer by 2020) creates grid integration challenges:

**Technical challenges:**
- Transmission constraints: Northern and southern grids approaching limits
- Congestion: Renewable-rich areas (central coast, Mekong Delta) facing curtailment
- Frequency stability: Variable generation impacts
- Flexibility requirements: Ramp rates for thermal plants

**Economic challenges:**
- Merit order effect: Renewables displacing thermal baseload
- Capacity payments: Ensuring baseload plant viability
- Stranded assets: Risk of coal/gas assets becoming uneconomic
- Market design: Wholesale electricity market still developing

**Solutions being implemented:**
- Grid expansion: 500kV transmission upgrades
- Storage: Battery energy storage systems (BESS) projects
- Demand response: Industrial load management
- Regional interconnection: ASEAN Power Grid links

*Sources: MOIT Grid Integration Studies; EVN System Operations Reports; World Bank Vietnam Energy Sector Assessment*

### 2.2 Plant-by-Plant Narrative Discussion

#### NORTHERN COAL-FIRED COMPLEX

---

**Mông Dương I & II (Mong Duong I & II)**

**Development History:**

Mông Dương I represents one of Vietnam's most modern and largest coal-fired power complexes, developed in two phases under different ownership structures.

**Mông Dương I** (1,120 MWe) achieved commercial operation between 2015-2016 as a BOT project developed by AES (USA) with EVN as off-taker. The project uses ultra-supercritical technology with two 560 MW units, marking a technology leap for Vietnam's coal fleet. The project's USD 2.1 billion investment was financed with support from US EXIM Bank and US commercial banks. Environmental standards include advanced FGD (flue gas desulfurization) and SCR (selective catalytic reduction) systems.

**Mông Dương II** (660 MWe) is a BOT project developed by KEPCO (Korea) with Vinacomin, with two 330 MW units achieving COD in 2019. Total investment was approximately USD 1.2 billion. The project uses supercritical technology, slightly less advanced than the adjacent Mông Dương I units.

**Notable Issues:**
- Mông Dương I experienced delays during construction phase due to equipment delivery and skilled labor constraints
- Coal supply arrangements: domestic Quảng Ninh anthracite blended with imported sub-bituminous coal
- Environmental controversies: local fishing community concerns regarding cooling water discharge into Bai Tu Long Bay

**Confidence: HIGH** — Both projects verified through MOIT decisions, AES corporate filings, KEPCO project databases, and multiple government sources.

---

**Thăng Long (Thang Long) Power Plant**

**Development History:**

The Thăng Long thermal power project (600 MWe) was developed under a BOT arrangement by a consortium led by Vietnam's Việt Tri Group (VGB) with Japanese partners including Marubeni Corporation, EDF (France), and TEPCO (Tokyo Electric Power Company).

**Timeline:**
- Original planning: Early 2010s under PDP7
- Construction commencement: 2016
- Unit 1 commercial operation: 2019
- Unit 2 commercial operation: 2019
- Total investment: Approximately USD 1.1 billion

**Notable Issues:**
- The project utilizes supercritical technology with domestic Quảng Ninh anthracite coal
- Initial plans called for larger capacity (1,000 MW), but scaled down due to environmental concerns and financing constraints
- Ownership structure changed from original plans with TEPCO reducing its stake
- Coal supply backed by long-term agreement with Vinacomin

**Confidence: HIGH** — Verified through MOIT Decision 428, VGB press releases, and EVN generation reports.

---

**Cẩm Phả (Cam Pha) Power Complex**

The Cẩm Phả power complex represents Vietnam's domestic coal-focused thermal development, with multiple phases developed by Vinacomin Power (a subsidiary of Vinacomin) and provincial partners.

**Phase I** (300 MWe): Two 150 MW subcritical units, operational since 2015 under BOT arrangement with Vinacomin Power. Uses domestic anthracite from Cẩm Phả mines.

**Phase II** (440 MWe): Two 220 MW units operational since 2021, developed by Vinacomin Power with TKV participation. Higher efficiency subcritical technology.

**Phase III** (330 MWe): One 330 MW supercritical unit under construction, expected completion 2026. Represents efficiency upgrade at the complex.

**Notable Issues:**
- Strong integration with Vinacomin/TKV mining operations
- Environmental monitoring controversies in the Cẩm Phả area due to cumulative impacts from mining and power generation
- Coal quality: Recent concerns about declining calorific value of domestic anthracite affecting plant efficiency

**Confidence: HIGH** — Verified through Vinacomin annual reports and MOIT project databases.

---

**Uông Bí (Uong Bi) Power Complex**

**Phase I** (300 MWe): Two 150 MW subcritical units, operational since 2014, developed by EVNGENCO 1. Represents older technology in the northern fleet.

**Phase II** (660 MWe): Two 330 MW units, Phase II achieved full commercial operation in 2021, significantly expanding the complex. Uses supercritical technology with advanced environmental controls.

**Phase III** (330 MWe): One additional 330 MW supercritical unit approved under PDP8 planning, with expected completion around 2028. Project faces uncertain timeline due to coal transition policy environment.

**Notable Issues:**
- Uông Bí 1 uses older subcritical technology with lower efficiency (~35% net efficiency)
- Phase II represents significant efficiency improvement (~40% net efficiency)
- Coal supply: Domestic Quảng Ninh coal, primarily from TKV mines
- Environmental compliance: Older units face potential early retirement discussions under JETP framework

**Confidence: MEDIUM** — Phases I and II verified through EVN reports; Phase III based on PDP8 planning documents.

---

**Hải Phòng (Hai Phong) Power Complex**

The Hải Phòng power complex is developed by Pacific Corporation (a Vietnamese private developer) with foreign technology partners.

**Hải Phòng I** (600 MWe): Two 300 MW supercritical units, operational since 2022. Uses imported coal through the integrated Hải Phòng port terminal.

**Hải Phòng II** (330 MWe): One 330 MW supercritical unit under construction, expected 2026 completion.

**Hải Phòng III** (660 MWe): Planned USC unit (660 MW), included in PDP8 but timeline beyond 2030 uncertain due to coal phase-down commitments.

**Notable Issues:**
- Project financed primarily through domestic Vietnamese banks
- Pacific Corporation initially planned multiple phases; Phase III uncertain
- Integrated coal terminal capable of handling Capesize vessels
- Environmental approvals faced public consultation controversies

**Confidence: MEDIUM** — Project status confirmed through MOIT approvals and EIA records; financing details not fully public.

---

**Hạ Long (Ha Long) Power Complex**

**Phase I** (220 MWe): Two 110 MW units operational since 2016, developed by EVNGENCO 1. Uses circulating fluidized bed (CFB) technology suitable for lower-grade domestic coal.

**Phase II** (330 MWe): One 330 MW supercritical unit, operational since 2022. Phase II significantly expanded the complex.

**Phase III** (330 MWe): One additional 330 MW supercritical unit under construction, expected COD 2025-2026.

**Notable Issues:**
- Strong linkage to Quảng Ninh coal basin
- Phase III construction impacted by COVID-19 pandemic delays
- CFB technology in Phase I allows use of coal rejects and lower-grade coal

**Confidence: HIGH** — Verified through EVNGENCO 1 annual reports and MOIT project databases.

---

**Thái Bình (Thai Binh) Power Complex**

**Development History:**

Developed by China Energy Engineering Corporation (CEEC) under an EPC-plus-financing arrangement. Located in Thái Bình province on the Red River Delta.

**Thái Bình I** (600 MWe): Two 300 MW supercritical units, operational since 2022.

**Thái Bình II** (330 MWe): One 330 MW supercritical unit under construction, expected completion 2026.

**Notable Issues:**
- First major power project by Chinese EPC contractor in Vietnam under new bilateral framework
- Financed substantially through Chinese export credit (China Exim Bank)
- Coal supply: Mix of domestic and imported coal through coastal reception
- Environmental standards meeting Vietnamese Category I requirements

**Confidence: MEDIUM** — Verified through construction progress reports and MOIT project monitoring; detailed financing terms not fully disclosed.

---

**Nam Định I (Nam Dinh I) Power Plant**

**Development History:**

The Nam Định I project was developed by China Power Investment Corporation (CPI, now part of State Power Investment Corporation — SPIC) with technology from Chinese and European suppliers.

**Capacity**: Two 300 MW supercritical units, with commercial operation achieved in phases from late 2023 through 2024.

**Notable Issues:**
- Project completion delayed by approximately 2-3 years from original plans
- Financing through Chinese export credit and commercial loans
- Coal supply: Primarily imported coal via coastal reception facilities
- Technology transfer: Some components manufactured locally in Vietnam

**Confidence: HIGH** — Verified through EVN generation reports and MOIT project tracking.

---

**Na Duong (Na Duong) Power Plant**

**Development History:**

The Na Duong plant (110 MWe) represents Vietnam's first large-scale circulating fluidized bed (CFB) power project, designed specifically to utilize lower-grade domestic coal from Lào Cai province mines.

**Capacity**: Single 110 MW unit using CFB technology, operational since 2015.

**Notable Issues:**
- Project developed by Vinacomin Power as demonstration of CFB technology for Vietnamese coal conditions
- CFB technology allows combustion of lower calorific value coal unsuitable for conventional pulverized coal units
- Project faced technical challenges during initial operation period
- Economic viability depends on continued access to domestic coal at regulated prices

**Confidence: HIGH** — Verified through Vinacomin annual reports.

---

#### CENTRAL AND NORTH-CENTRAL COAL-FIRED PLANTS

---

**Vũng Áng (Vung Ang) Power Complex**

The Vũng Áng complex represents a major thermal power development in central Vietnam, anchored by the Vũng Áng thermal power center in Hà Tĩnh province.

**Phase I** (600 MWe): Two 300 MW supercritical units, operational since 2014. BOT project with Korean Export Import Bank (KExim) financing.

**Phase II** (660 MWe): Two 330 MW supercritical units, operational since 2021. BOT project developed by Korea Southern Power (KSOP).

**Phase III** (660 MWe): One 660 MW USC unit approved under PDP8, developer Korea Southern Power. Timeline uncertain, depends on coal transition policy.

**Phase IV** (330 MWe): One 330 MW unit under construction by PV Power (PetroVietnam subsidiary). Expected completion 2026.

**Notable Issues:**
- Integrated coal terminal capable of handling Panamax vessels
- Gas pipeline connection to Block B offshore gas fields (for potential fuel switching)
- Environmental concerns: Located in environmentally sensitive coastal zone near Cẩm Xuyên Nature Reserve
- Labor incidents during construction phase
- Coal supply: Mix of domestic (Quảng Ninh) and imported (Indonesia, Australia)

**Nghi Sơn (Nghi Son) Power Complex**

The Nghi Sơn power complex represents Vietnam's largest thermal power development, located in Thanh Hóa province with integrated coal import terminal.

**Development History:**

**Nghi Sơn I** (1,200 MWe): Two 600 MW ultra-supercritical units, operational since 2023. Developed by Idemitsu (Japan) with Marubeni and EVN participation. Total investment approximately USD 2.5 billion.

**Nghi Sơn II** (1,200 MWe): Two 600 MW USC units, achieving commercial operation 2024. Developed by CHCM consortium (Chubu Electric Power, Marubeni, Tokyo Electric Power, Idemitsu).

**Nghi Sơn III & IV** (2,400 MWe combined): Originally planned as 2 × 600 MW units each, now SUSPENDED. Phase III construction permit suspended; Phase IV planning cancelled. Affected by JETP commitments and financing challenges.

**Notable Issues:**
- Nghi Sơn represents the frontier of ultra-supercritical technology deployment in Vietnam (thermal efficiency ~43-45%)
- Integrated deep-water coal terminal (Nghi Sơn port) capable of handling Capesize vessels
- Coal supply: 100% imported (primarily Indonesia, Australia, Russia)
- Phases III and IV suspended following Vietnam's COP26 commitments and JETP partnership
- Developer consortiums restructured multiple times
- Environmental monitoring ongoing due to proximity to Hà Tĩnh province coastal ecosystems

**Confidence: HIGH** — Phases I and II verified through MOIT approvals and construction completion reports; Phases III/IV suspension confirmed through MOIT planning documents and news reports.

**Confidence: HIGH** — Phase 1 verified through Engie project database and MOIT approvals; Phases 2-3 suspension/cancellation confirmed through MOIT planning documents and international news reports.

## Annex B: Methodology Notes

### Data Collection Approach

This inventory was compiled through systematic review of:
1. Primary government documents (Decisions, PDP documents)
2. State-owned enterprise annual reports and disclosures
3. International project databases
4. Regulatory agency statistics
5. Academic and policy research
6. Major media reporting

### Confidence Assessment Framework

**HIGH Confidence**: Information corroborated by at least two primary sources (government decisions, official reports) with consistent data.

**MEDIUM Confidence**: Information from single primary source or multiple secondary sources (news, investor presentations) with consistent data.

**LOW Confidence**: Information from older planning documents, single secondary source, or sources with conflicting information.

### Limitations and Uncertainties

1. **Project timelines**: Dates for planned and approved projects subject to change based on financing, permitting, and policy developments.

2. **Capacity figures**: Net capacity may vary from gross capacity depending on auxiliary consumption; figures rounded to nearest 10 MWe for consistency.

3. **Ownership structures**: Complex BOT and joint venture arrangements not fully detailed; ownership shown reflects primary developer.

4. **LNG supply**: Project viability depends on LNG procurement at competitive prices; fuel supply arrangements not specified for all projects.

5. **Policy sensitivity**: Coal project status particularly susceptible to policy changes under JETP implementation.
