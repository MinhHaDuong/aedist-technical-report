# VIETNAM THERMAL POWER GENERATION INVENTORY

**Report date:** 23 May 2026 | **Analyst:** Energy-sector research specialist | **Classification:** Primary-sourced inventory

## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

Vietnam's power sector has undergone rapid expansion, growing from ~40 GW total installed capacity in 2018 to an estimated 80+ GW by 2025. Thermal generation (coal, gas, oil) remains the backbone, though renewables (solar, wind) have surged.

**Policy framework.** The PDP8 Implementation Plan (Decision 262/QĐ-TTg, 1 April 2024) prioritises domestic gas-fired thermal power (14,930 MW), LNG-fueled thermal power (initially 22,400 MW), and coal-fired thermal power (30,127 MW) as key power sources through 2030. The Revised PDP8 (Decision 768/QĐ-TTg, 2025) updated LNG projects, adding LNG Hải Phòng Phase I (1,600 MW), Hiệp Phước Phase II (1,500 MW), and others.

**Coal status.** As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction. In December 2022, Vietnam concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) agreement with US$15.5 billion to fund development of a plan to cap and then phase out unabated coal generation.

**LNG transition.** By 2030, Vietnam plans to develop 10 more gas-fired power projects using domestically exploited gas (7,900 MW) and 13 gas-fired power projects using imported LNG (22,542 MW). As of mid-2024, Vietnam had no LNG-to-power projects in operation; the first two ventures, Nhơn Trạch 3 and 4, were then under construction. By 2025, these entered commercial operation, making them Vietnam's first operational LNG plants.

**Key actors:** EVN (state utility, operates through Genco1/2/3), PVN/Petrovietnam (oil & gas group, via PV Power), Vinacomin/TKV (coal mining, operates mine-mouth plants), MOIT (Ministry of Industry and Trade, planning authority), and multiple BOT investors (AES, Marubeni, KEPCO, Sumitomo, CPIH/China).

**Challenges:** Coal phase-down under JETP commitments; LNG project delays due to PPA negotiations and financing difficulties; declining domestic gas reserves; renewable integration requiring flexible thermal backup.

## 2. PER-PLANT NARRATIVES (Major/Disputed Assets)

### Long Phú 1 (Sóc Trăng, 1,200 MW Coal)
Long Phu 1 is a 1.2 GW supercritical thermal power plant being developed in the Long Phu district of Soc Trang Province. The 1,200 MW coal-fired plant was developed by PVN with an EPC contract signed in 2014 with Power Machines (Russia)/PTSC JV worth US$1.2 billion, originally targeting COD 2018-2019. However, Power Machines was sanctioned by the US in 2018, when the plant was approximately 72% complete, and the project was suspended at 77.56% completion. In January 2025, a "launching ceremony" was held at the site. The power station was still slated for completion before 2027, but new EPC contractors were required. In April 2025, boiler installation began. Both boilers are expected to be fully installed and pressure-tested in the second and third quarters of 2026, ensuring Unit 1 begins power generation in the third quarter and Unit 2 in the fourth quarter of 2027.

### Hiệp Phước LNG Phase 1 (TP.HCM, 1,200 MW LNG)
Siemens will provide equipment to upgrade the Hiệp Phước 1 steam power plant in Ho Chi Minh City to a combined cycle plant, increasing electrical capacity by roughly 780 MW to approximately 1,200 MW, fired with regasified LNG instead of the oil used previously. The project is a key national project, invested by Hiệp Phước Power Company Limited under the BOO model, with total investment of approximately VND 18,911 billion (USD 892 million) and installed capacity of 1,200 MW. EVN signed the PPA for the project in March 2026, making it the second LNG PPA in Vietnam after Nhơn Trạch 3&4.

### Ô Môn Gas-to-Power Chain (Cần Thơ, ~3,810 MW total)
Ô Môn I (660 MW) operates on domestic gas. Ô Môn II, III, and IV (~1,050 MW each) are planned to use gas from Block B (Lô B) field, developed by PVN. Petrovietnam has been accelerating the investment process for the Lô B – Ô Môn Gas and Power chain project. All three expansion phases remain in pre-construction.

### Công Thanh (Thanh Hóa, 600 MW Coal → 1,500 MW LNG)
Công Thanh was originally a two-unit, 600 MW coal power station in the Nghi Sơn Economic Zone, Thanh Hóa. The coal project was presumed shelved. Under the Revised PDP8, LNG Công Thanh (1,500 MW) was added, converted from coal to LNG based on Government Office Letter 5473/VPCP-CN dated 31 July 2024.
