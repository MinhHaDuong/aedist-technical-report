## 1. Sector Overview

Vietnam's power system has an estimated total installed capacity of approximately 78 GW. EVNGENCO3's total installed capacity of 6,565 MW accounts for 8.4% of the entire power system. Thermal generation — coal, domestic gas, and LNG — constitutes the backbone of dispatchable supply. As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction.

**Policy framework.** The National Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8), approved by Decision 500/QĐ-TTg (15 May 2023), caps coal at 30.2 GW by 2030 and mandates full coal phase-out by 2050. Under PDP8, Vietnam will domestically generate 20% of its electricity needs with coal by 2030, increasing coal to a peak of 30 GW and replacing outstanding coal projects with LNG or renewables. Decision 262/QĐ-TTg (1 April 2024) approved the PDP8 Implementation Plan. On 15 April 2025, Decision 768 approved the Amended PDP8, raising LNG capacity to 22,524 MW by 2030. On 30 May 2025, MOIT issued Decision 1509/QĐ-BCT with the amended implementation schedule.

**Key actors.** EVN and its generation subsidiaries (EVNGENCO1, EVNGENCO2, EVNGENCO3) own most domestic coal and gas plants. EVNGENCO3 was established under Decision 3025/QĐ-BCT on 1 June 2012, reorganising the Phú Mỹ Thermal Power Company and receiving state capital ownership rights at several power generation companies. PetroVietnam (PVN) and its subsidiary PV Power operate the Cà Mau, Nhơn Trạch, Sông Hậu 1, and Thái Bình 2 plants. TKV (Vinacomin) operates mine-mouth coal plants. BOT-format plants by foreign investors (Sumitomo, Marubeni/KEPCO, AES, Janakuasa, CSEC, Jaks Resources) are progressively reverting to EVN as contracts expire — Phú Mỹ 3 was officially handed over to EVN on 1 March 2024, and EVNGENCO3 took over management, operation, repair, and maintenance of Phú Mỹ 2.2 on 4 February 2025.

**Challenges.** The LNG transition faces PPA/GSA bottlenecks; of 13 PDP8 LNG projects (~22,400 MW), only Nhơn Trạch 3&4 have reached commissioning. Coal retirements are slow. The JETP ($15.5 bn) creates political pressure for an accelerated phase-down.

## 2. Major Asset Narratives

### 2.1 Vĩnh Tân Complex (Bình Thuận)
Four coal units totalling 4,244 MW. The 1,800 MW Vĩnh Tân IV was developed by Vietnam Electricity and commissioned in 2017. Vĩnh Tân II (1,244 MW) has been operating since 2014. Vĩnh Tân I (1,200 MW BOT) is operated by China's CSEC; Vĩnh Tân IV Extension (600 MW) came online in 2019. The PDP8-listed Sơn Mỹ LNG hub (2×2,250 MW) would also be sited in Bình Thuận, making it the province with the most thermal capacity.

### 2.2 Phú Mỹ Complex (Bà Rịa – Vũng Tàu)
Vietnam's largest gas-fired cluster. The Phú Mỹ power complex has a combined capacity of up to 3,900 MW. The complex consists of Phú Mỹ 1, 2-1, 2-2, 3 and 4, all fuelled by natural gas with distilled oil as standby fuel. All Phú Mỹ plants completed commercial operation by 2004. Following expiry of BOT contracts, Phú Mỹ 3 and Phú Mỹ 2.2 have now been returned to EVNGENCO3.

### 2.3 Long Phú 1 (Sóc Trăng)
Long Phú 1 (2×600 MW) in Sóc Trăng province was assigned to PVN as investor from 2010. The project reached ~77–78% completion before US sanctions on Russian contractor Power Machines (January 2018) forced contract termination in January 2019. Under Decision 262/QĐ-TTg the completion date is 2027, but Decision 1509/QĐ-BCT (30 May 2025) advanced the target to 2026. Boiler 1 first fire is targeted for December 2026, with electricity generation from September 2027.

### 2.4 Ô Môn Complex (Cần Thơ)
Ô Môn I (660 MW, gas CCGT) has operated since ~2009. Ô Môn II (1,050 MW) is planned to use domestic gas from the Lô B–Ô Môn gas chain, while Ô Môn III and IV (~1,050 MW each) are designated for LNG. Notably, EVNGENCO3 signed an EPC contract for fuel conversion at Ô Môn 1 Thermal Power Plant in 2025, indicating LNG-readiness upgrades.

### 2.5 Sông Hậu Complex (Hậu Giang)
Sông Hậu Thermal Power Plant is an operating power station of at least 1,200 MW in Hậu Giang. Sông Hậu 2 (2,000 MW BOT, Toyo Ink/Malaysia) was terminated by MOIT in July 2024 after persistent delays. Land lease has been terminated.

### 2.6 Vân Phong 1 (Khánh Hòa)
In May 2025, Sumitomo announced intention to transfer a 50% stake in Van Phong Power Company. As of July 2025, Vân Phong-1 was reportedly not operating at full capacity. The 2×660 MW supercritical coal plant received its construction completion acceptance on 30 December 2023.
