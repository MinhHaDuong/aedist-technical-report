## **1. Sector Overview**

### **Electricity Mix**
Vietnam’s electricity mix has evolved rapidly, with thermal generation (coal, gas, oil) historically dominating. As of 2026, thermal plants account for ~60% of installed capacity, but the share of renewables (solar, wind, hydro) is rising. Coal remains the largest single source, followed by domestic gas and imported LNG.

### **Policy Framework**
- **Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8, 2021–2030, vision to 2050):** Prioritizes LNG and renewables, phases down coal, and targets net-zero by 2050.
- **Revised PDP7 (2016):** Focused on coal and large-scale thermal projects, many of which are now delayed or converted to LNG.
- **National Energy Development Strategy:** Encourages private investment, BOT/BOO models, and regional fuel diversification.

### **Key Institutional Actors**
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT):** Policy and planning.
- **Vietnam Electricity (EVN):** State-owned utility, major owner/operator.
- **PetroVietnam (PVN):** Gas and oil-fired plants.
- **Vinacomin (TKV):** Coal mining and coal-fired plants.
- **Independent Power Producers (IPPs):** Private and foreign investors, especially in LNG and BOT projects.

### **Current Challenges**
- **Coal:** Environmental opposition, financing difficulties, and supply chain risks.
- **Gas:** Domestic reserves declining; LNG infrastructure (terminals, regas) still under development.
- **Grid:** Congestion, especially in the south; need for transmission upgrades.
- **Policy:** Uncertainty over project approvals, fuel switching, and investor confidence.
