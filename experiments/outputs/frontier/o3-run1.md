1.  SECTOR OVERVIEW
=====================================================================

1.1  Evolution of the generation mix (all figures net, EVN statistics 2022 unless otherwise indicated)

•  Installed capacity 2005: 11.6 GW (Hydro 55 %, Gas 24 %, Coal/Oil 21 %) – 50.6 TWh generated  
•  Installed capacity 2010: 23.5 GW (Hydro 42 %, Gas 34 %, Coal/Oil 24 %) – 98.2 TWh  
•  Installed capacity 2015: 38.6 GW (Hydro 34 %, Gas 30 %, Coal 33 %, RE 3 %) – 159.2 TWh  
•  Installed capacity 2022: 76.6 GW  
    – Coal 26.3 GW (34 %) produced 131 TWh (46 %)  
    – Domestic gas 7.4 GW (10 %) produced 32 TWh (11 %)  
    – Oil & small diesels 0.6 GW (<1 %)  
    – Hydro 22.3 GW (29 %) produced 75 TWh (26 %)  
    – Solar 18.4 GW (24 %) produced 26 TWh ( 9 %)  
    – Wind 4.1 GW ( 5 %) produced 10 TWh ( 4 %)  
    – Biomass/others 0.9 GW ( 1 %)  
    Source:  EVN “Electricity Statistic Yearbook 2022” [S-04].

1.2  Policy framework

•  PDP6 (Decision 110/2007/QĐ-TTg, July 2007): first to signal coal import need after 2020.  
•  PDP7 (Decision 1208/QĐ-TTg, Jul 2011): 75 GW total by 2020, 146 GW by 2030; coal share 47 % by 2020.  
•  PDP7-Revised (Decision 428/QĐ-TTg, 18 Mar 2016): coal capacity target 55 GW by 2030;  gas/LNG 19 GW.  
•  PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 15 Apr 2023):  
     – Phase-down coal to max 30.1 GW in 2030, zero unabated coal after 2050.  
     –  LNG/CCGT 22.4 GW by 2030.  
     –  Renewables ≥47 % of generation by 2030.  

1.3  Fuel-supply landscape

a)  Coal  
   •  Domestic production: 38 Mt in 2022 (Vinacomin 32 Mt, Northeast Corp 6 Mt); calorific value ~4,300 kcal/kg.  
   •  Imports: 54 Mt in 2022 (mainly Indonesia, Australia, Russia).  
   •  10 coastal power plants are designed for 100 % imported coal (e.g. Duyên Hải 3, Nghi Sơn 2).  

b)  Domestic natural gas  
   •  Nam Côn Sơn Basin (Bạch Hổ, Rồng, Lan Tây/Lan Đỏ pipelines) feeds Phú Mỹ and Cà Mau complexes.  
   •  Cà Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) PSC – FID pending, earliest first gas 2028 (Quyết định 214/QĐ-TTg 2020).  
   •  Block B–Ô Môn (PetroVietnam, Mitsui, PTT) – targeted 2027 (MOIT Report 35/BC-BCT-2023).  

c)  LNG import terminals (PDP8 priority list)  
   •  Thị Vải (PV Gas) – 1 Mtpa commissioning 2023 for Nhơn Trạch 3-4.  
   •  Sơn Mỹ (AES & PV Gas) – 3.6 Mtpa FID 2024.  
   •  Long An, Hải Phòng, Bạc Liêu FSRUs – licensing under MOIT Circular 13/2021/TT-BCT.  

1.4  Key institutional actors

•  EVN (state utility, 100 % state): parent of EVNGENCO 1, 2, 3; holds majority in 18 coal plants and all gas plants at Phú Mỹ.  
•  PetroVietnam (PVN): upstream gas, PV Gas (mid-stream LNG), PV Power (7.5 GW generation).  
•  BOT investors:  
    –  Japan: JBIC, JERA, KEPCO (JV), Marubeni (Nghi Sơn 2, Ô Môn 2)  
    –  Korea: KEPCO (Vũng Áng 2), Doosan (Vân Phong 1 EPC)  
    –  Singapore: Sembcorp (Duyên Hải 2), SP Group (Long An LNG)  
    –  US: AES (Sơn Mỹ 2 gas, Mông Dương 2 coal)  
    –  France: EDF (Sơn Mỹ 1 CCGT)  
•  MOIT (Ministry of Industry and Trade) – plans & licensing; provincial People’s Committees approve land/EIA.  

1.5  Current challenges

•  Coal phase-down pledge in Glasgow Statement and JETP (Dec 2022, US$15.5 bn) – 2030 unabated coal ceiling of 30 GW versus ~27 GW already online.  
•  LNG-to-power economics: delivered LNG > US$13/MMBtu vs. EVN retail tariff < US$0.085/kWh (ERAV 12/2022) ⇒ viability gap.  
•  Renewable curtailment (solar midday), lack of ancillary services market; thermal plants still needed for ramping/spinning reserve.  
•  Grid congestion: 500 kV Bắc–Nam line fully loaded (>2,400 MW) forcing coal plants in the North to back-down at night.
