**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Prepared by:** Senior Energy Analyst

## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Evolution of the Electricity Generation Mix
Vietnam's power sector has undergone a dramatic transformation over the last two decades. Historically reliant on hydropower, the country faced severe power shortages during the droughts of 2010-2011, prompting a strategic pivot toward thermal capacity to ensure energy security.

*   **Hydropower:** Dominated the mix in the early 2000s (approx. 40-50% of generation). Large-scale dams (Son La, Hoa Binh) provided baseload but proved vulnerable to climate variability. Capacity has plateaued around 22-23 GW due to resource exhaustion.
*   **Coal Power:** The "Coal Boom" (2015-2020) shifted coal to the primary baseload source. By 2020, coal accounted for ~49% of generation. Installed capacity reached ~21 GW.
*   **Renewables:** The 2017 FiT mechanisms triggered a solar boom (rooftop and utility scale), reaching ~16 GW installed solar PV capacity by 2020. Wind power followed, reaching ~4 GW by 2021.
*   **Gas Power:** Historically supplied by associated gas from offshore fields (Cu Long, Nam Con Son, Malay-Tho Chu). The depletion of domestic reserves and the need for flexible backup for renewables has shifted focus toward Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG).

**Key Statistics (End of PDP7 Revised Period vs. PDP8 Baseline):**
*   **Total Installed Capacity (2022):** ~80 GW (excluding rooftop solar estimates).
*   **Thermal Share:** Coal ~30% of capacity, Gas/LNG ~15%.
*   **Generation Share (2022):** Coal ~45%, Hydro ~30%, Gas/LNG ~11%, Renewables ~14%.

### 1.2 Policy Framework
*   **PDP6 (2004):** Focused on hydropower exploitation and initial coal plants.
*   **PDP7 (2011) & PDP7 Revised (2016 - Decision 428/QĐ-TTg):** The blueprint for the coal boom. Targeted 42% coal and 24% gas in generation share by 2030. It authorized the bulk of current operational coal fleet and BOT projects.
*   **PDP8 (2023 - Decision 500/QĐ-TTg):** A paradigm shift. Reflects COP26 commitments.
    *   **Coal:** Caps coal capacity at 30.13 GW by 2030 (down from earlier projections of ~55 GW). No new coal plants after 2030; phase-out by 2050. Cancels roughly 15 GW of previously planned coal capacity (e.g., Long Phu 2/3, Song Hau 2).
    *   **Gas/LNG:** Prioritizes LNG-to-power to replace coal. Targets 37.33 GW gas/LNG by 2030 (approx. 24% of mix).
    *   **Renewables:** Targets 31-39 GW by 2030.

### 1.3 Fuel Supply Landscape
*   **Domestic Coal:** Produced primarily by **Vinacomin (TKV)** and Dong Bac Corporation. Domestic production (~40-45 MT/year) is prioritized for power, but quality is low (high ash, moisture). Most new plants require imported coal.
*   **Imported Coal:** Vietnam became a top global coal importer (Indonesia, Australia, Russia). Imports surged to ~40 MT/year to feed USC plants in the South (Vinh Tan, Duyen Hai).
*   **Domestic Gas:** Sourced from three main basins by PetroVietnam (PVN):
    *   *Cu Long Basin:* Supplying Ca Mau 1&2 (declining).
    *   *Nam Con Son:* Supplying Phu My, Ba Ria (declining).
    *   *Malay - Tho Chu:* Supplying O Mon, Ca Mau.
    *   *Future:* **Block B** (delayed) and **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale)** are critical for future domestic supply.
*   **LNG:** Vietnam transitioned to an LNG importer in 2023.
    *   *Thi Vai LNG Terminal (PV Gas):* Operational (1.5 MTPA capacity).
    *   *Son My LNG Terminal:* Under construction.
    *   *Long Son LNG Terminal:* Under construction.

### 1.4 Key Institutional Actors
*   **EVN (Vietnam Electricity):** State utility, monopoly transmitter, major generator.
    *   *Subsidiaries:* EVNGENCO 1, 2, 3 (G3), CP Power.
*   **PVN (PetroVietnam):** Controls gas supply and gas-power value chain.
    *   *PV Power:* Largest gas generator (Phu My, Ca Mau, Nhon Trach).
    *   *PV Gas:* Monopoly on gas processing and LNG import infrastructure.
*   **BOT Investors:** Build-Operate-Transfer developers dominate large coal/LNG projects.
    *   *Key Players:* AES (USA), Marubeni/J-POWER (Japan), KEPCO/Samsung C&T (Korea), Sumitomo Corp, EDF, Sembcorp, Tokyo Gas.

### 1.5 Current Challenges
*   **JETP (Just Energy Transition Partnership):** The $15.5bn deal with G7 nations pressures Vietnam to accelerate coal phase-down, threatening the economics of recently completed coal plants (stranded asset risk).
*   **LNG Economics:** High global LNG prices (post-Ukraine war) make LNG power expensive ($10-12/MMBtu vs domestic gas $6-7). PDP8's pricing mechanisms for LNG are still being finalized.
*   **Grid Congestion:** Rapid renewables deployment in the South/Central regions outpaces transmission, causing curtailment of thermal plants.

### Per-plant Discussion

**Operational Coal Fleet (North):**
*   **Pha Lai:** The oldest plant in the inventory. Units 1-2 (120MW) were rehabilitated/refurbished. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Quang Ninh:** Early subcritical units. Frequent curtailment due to grid constraints in the North. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Mong Duong 1 & 2:** Represent the shift to USC technology. Mong Duong 2 was the first BOT coal project (AES). AES announced exit in 2022, selling stake to Nebras Power. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Hai Phong:** Significant expansion from subcritical (Units 1-2) to USC (Units 3-4). Confidence: HIGH.

**Operational Coal Fleet (South):**
*   **Vinh Tan Complex:** Vinh Tan 1 (BOT) and 2 faced significant local opposition during construction due to dust pollution, leading to riots in 2015. Vinh Tan 4 Extension added a single USC unit. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Duyen Hai Complex:** Duyen Hai 1 (EVN) and 3 (BOT - Taekwang) are critical for the southern grid. Duyen Hai 3 Extension (formerly Duyen Hai 2 site) was transferred from BOT investor to EVN due to financing difficulties. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Tra Vinh:** PV Power's only coal plant. Uses CFB technology suitable for lower-grade coal. Confidence: HIGH.

**Under Construction Coal (The "Stranded Asset" Risk Zone):**
*   **Song Hau 1:** EVN Genco 2 project. Progress is slow (approx 40-50% as of late 2023). Financing constrained by global coal exit policies. Confidence: MEDIUM (Timeline likely to slip).
*   **Long Phu 1:** EVN Genco 3 project. Located in Soc Trang. Foundation issues and financing have caused delays. Originally planned for 2019-2020, now pushed to 2026+. Confidence: MEDIUM.
*   **Van Phong 1:** Sumitomo BOT. Sumitomo announced a policy to halt new coal investments, but proceeded with this project as it was already sanctioned. Construction advanced. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Vung Ang 2:** KEPCO/J-POWER BOT. Highly controversial; financing faced pressure from climate groups. Financial close reached in late 2021 despite global coal exit trends. Confidence: HIGH.

**Gas/LNG Transition:**
*   **Phu My Complex:** The largest gas complex (PV Power). Running on Nam Con Son gas. Facing declining gas pressure. Conversion to LNG capability is being studied. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Ca Mau:** Running on PM3-CAA gas (Malaysia). Gas supply disruptions frequent. PV Power seeking LNG backup. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Nhon Trach 3 & 4:** The first LNG-to-power projects under construction. PV Power is lead. Financing secured. Critical test for Vietnam's LNG policy. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Son My 1 & 2:** PV Power and Tokyo Gas. Investment decisions pending final LNG pricing regulations. Confidence: MEDIUM.

**Cancelled Projects (PDP8 Impact):**
*   **Vinh Tan 3:** KEPCO withdrew. Cancelled in PDP8. Confidence: HIGH.
*   **Long Phu 2 & 3, Song Hau 2:** Cancelled in PDP8 (Decision 500) due to environmental commitments and lack of financing. Confidence: HIGH.
