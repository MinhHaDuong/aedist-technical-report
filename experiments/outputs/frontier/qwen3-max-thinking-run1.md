## 1. Sector Overview

### Evolution of Vietnam’s Electricity Generation Mix

Vietnam’s electricity generation mix has undergone significant transformation over the past two decades, shifting from hydro-dominance to thermal-led expansion to meet surging demand. In 2000, hydropower accounted for over 60% of installed capacity. By 2023, thermal power (coal and gas) represented **~55% of installed capacity** and **~65% of electricity generation**, driven by rapid industrialization and urbanization.

- **Installed Capacity (2023)**: ~85 GW total  
  - Coal: 22.5 GW (26.5%)  
  - Gas (domestic + LNG): 7.5 GW (8.8%)  
  - Hydro: 22.0 GW (25.9%)  
  - Renewables (solar, wind, biomass): 22.0 GW (25.9%)  
  - Others (oil, diesel): <1 GW  
  *(Source: EVN Annual Report 2023; MOIT Electricity Statistics 2023)*

- **Electricity Generation (2023)**: ~290 TWh  
  - Coal: 145 TWh (50%)  
  - Gas: 45 TWh (15.5%)  
  - Hydro: 65 TWh (22.4%)  
  - Renewables: 30 TWh (10.3%)  
  - Others: 5 TWh  
  *(Source: EVN Dispatch Data; IEA Vietnam Energy Outlook 2024)*

Coal’s share peaked around 2020–2022 but is now plateauing due to environmental constraints and policy shifts. Solar and wind saw explosive growth post-2019 under feed-in tariffs (FiTs), but intermittency has reinforced the need for dispatchable thermal capacity.

### Policy Framework: Power Development Plans (PDPs)

Vietnam’s thermal power trajectory is governed by successive national Power Development Plans (PDPs), approved by the Prime Minister:

- **PDP6 (2011)**: Emphasized coal expansion to meet 17% annual demand growth. Targeted 75 GW by 2030, with coal at ~50 GW. Largely superseded.
  
- **PDP7 (2011, Decision 1208/QĐ-TTg)**: Reduced coal targets slightly; promoted gas and renewables. Approved 20+ coal plants.

- **PDP7 Revised (2016, Decision 428/QĐ-TTg)**:  
  - 2030 coal capacity target: **55.3 GW**  
  - Gas capacity: **14.7 GW** (including LNG)  
  - Total capacity: **129.5 GW**  
  - Notable: First inclusion of LNG-to-power projects (e.g., Thị Vải, Nghi Sơn)

- **PDP8 (2023, Decision 500/QĐ-TTg)**:  
  - **Coal phase-down**: Cap at **30.2 GW by 2030**, **no new coal after 2030**  
  - **Gas/LNG expansion**: **27.1 GW by 2030** (19.4 GW LNG, 7.7 GW domestic gas)  
  - Total capacity: **151.4 GW by 2030**  
  - Emphasis on **Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP)** commitments (COP26)

PDP8 marks a strategic pivot: coal is capped and declining post-2030, while LNG is positioned as the primary flexible baseload to complement variable renewables.

### Fuel Supply Landscape

**Domestic Coal**:  
- Mined by **Vinacomin (TKV – Vietnam National Coal and Mineral Industries Group)**  
- Production: ~45–50 Mt/year (2023), mostly sub-bituminous with high ash/moisture  
- Used in older plants (e.g., Phả Lại, Uông Bí); newer plants increasingly blend with imported coal for efficiency

**Imported Coal**:  
- Imports surged to **~50 Mt in 2023** (General Department of Vietnam Customs)  
- Sourced from Indonesia, Australia, South Africa  
- Critical for newer supercritical plants (e.g., Vĩnh Tân 4, Duyên Hải 3)

**Domestic Natural Gas**:  
- Produced by **PetroVietnam (PVN)** from offshore basins:  
  - **Nam Côn Sơn Basin**: Supplies Phú Mỹ, Cà Mau, and Nhơn Trạch clusters via PV Gas pipelines  
  - **Block B (offshore Mekong Delta)**: Long-delayed; FID not yet taken (as of 2025)  
  - **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale)**: ExxonMobil-led; expected 2028–2030; will supply 15 bcm/year, primarily for power and industry

**LNG Import Terminals**:  
- **Thị Vải LNG Terminal (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu)**: 4 MMtpa, operational 2022 (PV Gas)  
- **Nghi Sơn LNG Terminal (Thanh Hóa)**: 4 MMtpa, operational 2023 (PVN–Marubeni JV)  
- **Long An LNG Terminal**: 4 MMtpa, under construction (PV Gas–Sumitomo)  
- **Hai Phong LNG Terminal**: Planned (EDF–Vietnam LNG JV)  
- **Bạc Liêu LNG Terminal**: Planned (AES)  
- Total planned LNG import capacity: **20+ MMtpa by 2030**

### Key Institutional Actors

- **EVN (Electricity of Vietnam)**: State-owned monopoly grid operator and major generator via subsidiaries:  
  - **EVNGENCO 1**: Northern plants (e.g., Phả Lại, Uông Bí)  
  - **EVNGENCO 2**: Central plants (e.g., Phú Mỹ, Nhơn Trạch)  
  - **EVNGENCO 3**: Southern plants (e.g., Cà Mau, Duyên Hải)

- **PetroVietnam (PVN)**: State oil & gas corporation; controls gas supply and owns **PV Power** (generator) and **PV Gas** (midstream)

- **PV Power**: Operates major gas-fired plants (Phú Mỹ 3, Cà Mau 1&2, Vũng Áng 1)

- **BOT Investors**:  
  - **Japan**: JBIC/KEPCO (Mông Dương 2, Vĩnh Tân 4)  
  - **Singapore**: Sembcorp (Mỹ Phước, Duyên Hải 2)  
  - **France**: EDF (Hải Dương coal plant)  
  - **USA**: AES (Bạc Liêu LNG-to-power)  
  - **Thailand**: Gulf Energy (Long Phú 1 – suspended)

- **MOIT (Ministry of Industry and Trade)**: Formulates PDPs, issues investment approvals

- **Provincial Authorities**: Issue land permits, environmental approvals; often lobby for local projects

### Current Challenges

- **Coal Phase-Down under JETP**: Vietnam committed at COP26 (2021) to peak emissions by 2030 and achieve net-zero by 2050. The **$15.5 billion JETP** (led by EU, UK, US, Japan) supports early coal retirement and grid modernization. However, **only 30 GW of coal is now allowed by 2030** (vs. 55 GW in PDP7R), forcing cancellation of ~25 GW of planned coal.

- **LNG-to-Power Economics**: High LNG prices post-2022 (vs. pre-2020 assumptions) have strained project economics. PPAs often lack full cost pass-through, deterring private investment. Government is revising tariff mechanisms.

- **Renewable Integration vs. Baseload Needs**: Solar/wind curtailment reached **12% in 2023** due to grid congestion and lack of flexible generation. Gas/LNG plants are critical for balancing, but deployment lags.

### Per-Plant Discussion

**Phả Lại 1 & 2 (Hải Dương)**: Among Vietnam’s oldest coal plants. Phả Lại 1 (600 MWe) commissioned 1983–1986; Phả Lại 2 (600 MWe) added in 2001–2002. Both subcritical, high-emission. Under JETP, expected to retire by 2030. **Confidence: HIGH** (EVNGENCO 1 annual reports).

**Uông Bí Complex (Quảng Ninh)**: Includes original units (1964–1983) and CFB extension (2011). Total 710 MWe. CFB unit designed for low-grade domestic coal. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Hải Dương Plant (Hải Dương)**: 1,200 MWe supercritical plant developed by EDF (51%), Sumitomo (30%), EVN (19%). COD delayed from 2020 to 2022 due to pandemic and financing. First major EU-invested coal plant. **Confidence: HIGH** (EDF 2022 press release).

**Mông Dương 1 & 2 (Quảng Ninh)**: Mông Dương 1 (600 MWe CFB) owned by PV Power; Mông Dương 2 (1,200 MWe supercritical) by KEPCO/Marubeni. Both operational since 2015. Mông Dương 2 faced environmental protests during construction. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Vĩnh Tân Complex (Bình Thuận)**: Four plants totaling 4,844 MWe. Vĩnh Tân 4 Extension (600 MWe) COD 2023—last coal unit likely to be commissioned in Vietnam. All Chinese-invested or co-developed. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Duyên Hải Complex (Trà Vinh)**: Three plants (3,732 MWe) plus 622 MWe extension. Duyên Hải 2 is Sembcorp’s flagship. All operational by 2020. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Long Phú 1 (Sóc Trăng)**: 1,244 MWe supercritical plant. Suspended in 2017 after Russian contractor (Atomstroyexport) withdrew; Gulf Energy took over but halted due to PDP8 coal cap. **Confidence: HIGH** (MOIT Decision 3734/QĐ-BCT, 2022).

**Sông Hậu 1 (Hậu Giang)**: 1,244 MWe. First major PV Power coal plant in Mekong Delta. COD 2023 after 7-year delay (original 2016). **Confidence: HIGH** (PV Power 2023 report).

**Thái Bình 1 & 2 (Thái Bình)**: Thái Bình 1 (600 MWe) by EVN; Thái Bình 2 (1,200 MWe) by PV Power. Both operational by 2023. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Quảng Trạch 1 & 2 (Quảng Bình)**: Only new coal plants approved under PDP8. Quảng Trạch 1 (1,200 MWe USC) under construction by EVNGENCO 1; COD 2026. Quảng Trạch 2 (PV Power) approved but financing uncertain. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (MOIT PDP8 Implementation Plan, 2024).

**Vũng Áng 1 (Hà Tĩnh)**: 1,200 MWe. Notorious for 2016 Formosa environmental disaster (though plant itself not responsible). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Cancelled Coal Plants (An Khánh, Cà Ná, Long Phú 2&3, Sông Hậu 2)**: All removed in PDP8. An Khánh (Quảng Ninh) was EVN’s planned replacement for Uông Bí but cancelled in 2020. **Confidence: HIGH** (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 2023).

**Phú Mỹ Cluster (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu)**: Vietnam’s largest gas-fired complex (7,620 MWe). All operational since 2004. Supplies southern load center. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Cà Mau 1 & 2 (Cà Mau)**: 2,880 MWe total. Dependent on PM3 gas field (depleting). Future LNG conversion planned. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Nhơn Trạch 1 & 2 (Đồng Nai)**: 1,800 MWe. Gas supply from Nam Côn Sơn basin. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Đồng Nai)**: First LNG-to-power projects in south. Nhơn Trạch 3 under construction (PV Gas–Sumitomo JV); COD 2026. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (construction progress photos, PV Gas announcements).

**Thị Vải LNG Plant (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu)**: Tied to Thị Vải LNG terminal. Marubeni co-developer. Expected 2025 COD. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**Nghi Sơn LNG Plant (Thanh Hóa)**: Linked to Nghi Sơn LNG terminal. PVN–Marubeni. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**Long An LNG Plants (Long An)**: Critical for HCMC load. Long An 1 under construction. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**Bạc Liêu LNG Plant (Bạc Liêu)**: AES project. Approved under PDP8. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (AES press release, MOIT approval).

**Ô Môn (Cần Thơ)**: 600 MWe coal plant in Mekong Delta. Faced local opposition. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Small Provincial Coal Plants (Na Dương, Nậm Cắn, Vĩnh Sơn, Trung Nam, Cần Thơ)**: 220 MWe each, mostly subcritical. Built 2009–2013 to meet local demand. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Oil-Fired Plants (Đa Nhim, Thủ Đức)**: Peaking units; rarely run due to high fuel cost. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**Hiệp Phước & Mỹ Phước (HCMC/Bình Dương)**: Gas-fired CCGT plants serving industrial zones. **Confidence: HIGH**.
