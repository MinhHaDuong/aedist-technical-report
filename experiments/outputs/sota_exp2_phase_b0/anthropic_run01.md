## 1. Sector Overview

Vietnam's power system has grown rapidly to serve an economy targeting 10% GDP growth through 2030. Thermal generation — coal, gas, and oil — remains the backbone, supplying roughly half of national electricity output. Both the original and revised PDP8 restrict coal to projects under construction, capping 2030 capacity at approximately 31 GW. The 2025 plan sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050, with a total converted capacity of 25,798 MW.

**Regulatory architecture:**
- On 15 April 2025, the Prime Minister issued Decision 768/QĐ-TTg approving the Revised PDP8, superseding Decision 500/QĐ-TTg of 15 May 2023.
- On 30 May 2025, MOIT issued Decision 1509/QĐ-BCT approving the implementation plan for the Revised PDP8.

**Key actors:** EVN (state utility, controls ~60% of generation via GENCO 1/2/3); PVN/PV Power (gas-fired fleet); Vinacomin (coal-mine-mouth plants); private and foreign BOT developers (AES, JERA, Marubeni, Sumitomo, EDF, Sembcorp, Jaks Resources, Janakuasa).

**Coal trajectory:** By 2030, coal is projected to be the fourth-largest power source; its share declines to 13–16.9% of the mix, down from 20% under the original PDP8. After 2030, only approved and under-construction projects continue, with coal at 0% by 2050.

**Gas/LNG transition:** The 2025 plan adjusts domestic gas capacity by 2030 to 10,861–14,930 MW. LNG is expected to contribute 9.5–12.3% of total capacity by 2030. Between 2031 and 2035, LNG projects such as Long Sơn and Long An II are expected to begin operations. By 2050, the revised PDP8 envisions LNG plants co-firing with hydrogen (18,200–26,123 MW) and plants fully converted to hydrogen (8,576–11,325 MW).

**JETP progress:** Despite delayed documentation, JETP agreements in Vietnam are continuing to progress. Three clean-energy investment projects have officially penned financing agreements as of July 2025.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives (Major/Disputed Assets Only)

### Vĩnh Tân Thermal Power Centre (Bình Thuận)
Vĩnh Tân has a total capacity of up to 5,600 MW, located in Vĩnh Tân commune, Tuy Phong district, Bình Thuận province. Four plants: Vĩnh Tân I (1,200 MW BOT, commenced construction July 2015 with 95% capital from China Southern Power Grid); Vĩnh Tân II (1,244 MW, EVNGENCO3); Vĩnh Tân IV + extension (1,800 MW, EVNGENCO3). Vĩnh Tân III (~1,980 MW) was removed from PDP8; status: cancelled.

### Duyên Hải Thermal Power Centre (Trà Vinh)
Duyên Hải Power Centre located in Trà Vinh has a total capacity of up to 4,400 MW, including 4 coal-fired thermal power plants using steam turbine technology. Duyên Hải I (1,245 MW, EVNGENCO1, COD 2015–16); Duyên Hải III + extension (1,245+660 MW, EVNGENCO1, COD 2017–19); Duyên Hải II BOT (1,320 MW, Janakuasa Malaysia, COD 2021–22).

### Phú Mỹ Gas Complex (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu)
The Phú Mỹ power complex is the largest group of power generation plants in Vietnam, with a combined capacity of up to 3,900 MW. Five CCGT plants: Phú Mỹ 1 is equipped with three 240 MW gas turbines and a 360 MW steam turbine; full capacity 1,090 MW. Phú Mỹ 2.1 (900 MW, EVN/GENCO3). Phú Mỹ 2.2 is a 729 MW CCGT, COD February 2005, developed by Mekong Energy; EDF (56.25%), Sumitomo (28.13%), JERA (15.62%). Phú Mỹ 3 is a 749 MW CCGT, COD March 2004; owned by Sembcorp Utilities (66.67%), Kyuden International (26.66%), and Sojitz (6.66%). Phú Mỹ 4 is an operating power station of at least 477 MW.

### Long Phú I (Sóc Trăng)
Long Phú I, invested by PVN and Power Machines–PTSC, held its groundbreaking in 2015; 2 units with a total of 1,200 MW. Severely delayed due to Russian contractor sanctions. Status: under construction with uncertain COD.

### Ô Môn Complex (Cần Thơ)
Ô Môn I (existing, ~660 MW gas turbines) to be converted from open-cycle to combined-cycle using Lô B gas. Ô Môn II–IV (~1,050 MW each) are planned domestic-gas/LNG CCGT plants under PDP8 awaiting upstream gas development and PPAs.

### Sơn Mỹ LNG (Bình Thuận)
Two planned LNG plants of approximately 2,250 MW each, linked to the Sơn Mỹ LNG terminal. Development has been slow; no FID reached. Deferred under amended PDP8.
