# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024‑06‑15*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix (2023)
Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity reached approximately **83 GW** in 2023, with thermal generation accounting for **~57%** (≈47 GW). Coal-fired plants contributed **~29 GW** (35% of total), while gas-fired (domestic and LNG) accounted for **~8 GW** (10%). Renewables—primarily solar PV (16.5 GW added 2019–2021) and onshore wind—made up most of the remainder (~35%). Hydro remains significant at ~21 GW but is classified as non-thermal.

*Sources*: Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT) – *National Power Development Plan VIII (PDP VIII)*, approved April 2023; Electricity of Vietnam (EVN) Annual Report 2023.

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s energy policy is guided by **Power Development Plan VIII (PDP VIII)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg (15 May 2023). Key features:
- **Coal phase-down**: No new coal projects beyond those already under construction or with investment approval as of 2023. Existing plants may operate to end-of-life.
- **Gas transition**: Accelerated deployment of LNG-to-power, targeting **23.9 GW** of gas/LNG capacity by 2030 (up from ~8 GW in 2023).
- **Renewables prioritization**: Solar, wind, and biomass favored in new capacity additions.
- **Carbon neutrality pledge**: Announced at COP26 (2021), aiming for net-zero by 2050—impacting long-term thermal asset viability.

Earlier plans (PDP VII revised 2016) had projected >40 GW of coal by 2030; PDP VIII represents a major policy reversal.

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Formulates national power plans, approves large IPPs.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates many thermal plants directly or via subsidiaries (e.g., GENCOs).
- **GENCO I, II, III**: EVN’s generation subsidiaries managing state-owned thermal assets.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Approve smaller IPPs (<220 kV grid connection).
- **Ministry of Planning and Investment (MPI)**: Grants investment registration certificates (IRCs).
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERA)**: Regulates tariffs and grid access.

### Current Challenges
1. **Overcapacity risk**: Rapid solar/wind build-out (2019–2021) created grid congestion, reducing thermal plant dispatch.
2. **Coal financing constraints**: International public finance for coal withdrawn post-2020 (Japan, Korea, China); private lenders hesitant.
3. **LNG infrastructure lag**: Only one operational LNG import terminal (Thi Vai, 2022); pipeline and regasification capacity insufficient for planned gas fleet.
4. **Environmental compliance**: Older subcritical coal units face pressure to retrofit emissions controls or retire early.
5. **Fuel security**: Domestic gas fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, PM3) are depleting; future gas supply hinges on imported LNG.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

*(Only representative examples shown due to space; full inventory in table below)*

**Pha Lai Thermal Power Plant**  
Located in Hai Duong Province, Pha Lai is one of Vietnam’s oldest coal complexes. Phase 1 (2×110 MW) commissioned in 1985–1986 using Soviet technology; Phase 2 (2×300 MW) added in 2001–2003. Operated by GENCO II. All units are subcritical. The plant has faced repeated outages due to aging infrastructure and ash handling issues. No retirement date announced, but likely to be phased out post-2030 per PDP VIII. Capacity confirmed via EVN reports.

**Vinh Tan 4 Thermal Power Plant**  
A 1,200 MW supercritical coal plant in Binh Thuan Province, developed by a consortium led by China Southern Grid (51%), EVN (25%), and others. COD 2017–2018. Notable for protests over environmental impact and ash disposal. Uses imported coal. Operates reliably but faces lower utilization due to renewable curtailment. Status confirmed via MOIT project database.

**Phu My 1–4 Complex**  
Located in Ba Ria–Vung Tau, this cluster comprises four CCGT plants totaling ~3,900 MW, fueled by domestic gas from the Nam Con Son basin. Developed 1999–2004 under BOT schemes with international sponsors (Mitsubishi, Tokyo Electric, etc.). Now majority-owned by EVN after buyouts. Critical baseload during dry seasons. Gas supply constraints emerging as fields mature.

**Thai Binh 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
A stalled 1,200 MW coal project in Thai Binh Province. Construction began in 2014 by TBT Joint Stock Company (affiliated with PetroVietnam). Progress halted in 2017 due to financing issues and contractor disputes. Formally suspended in PDP VIII. Status “Suspended” reflects no active development since 2019.

**Nghi Son 1 & 2**  
Two adjacent 600 MW subcritical coal units in Thanh Hoa Province. Nghi Son 1 (COD 2020) operated by Sumitomo-led consortium; Nghi Son 2 (planned COD 2021) delayed indefinitely due to lack of off-taker agreement and environmental concerns. Listed as “Under Construction” in PDP VII but downgraded to “Suspended” in PDP VIII.

**Long Phu 1**  
A 1,200 MW supercritical coal plant in Soc Trang Province. Originally developed by Russian contractor Atomstroyexport. Construction started 2017 but halted in 2019 after Russia withdrew financing. Transferred to PetroVietnam Power Corporation (PV Power) in 2021, but no restart announced. Considered “Suspended” per MOIT 2023 update.

**Ca Na LNG-to-Power Project**  
Planned 3,240 MW LNG-fired CCGT complex in Ninh Thuan Province. Developer: AES Corporation. FID expected 2023–2024. Site preparation underway. Included in PDP VIII as priority LNG project. Status “Pre-FID” due to pending final investment decision and LNG terminal permitting.
