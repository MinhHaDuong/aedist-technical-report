# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024-06-15*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix
As of 2023, Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity exceeded **87 GW**, with thermal generation accounting for approximately **55%** (≈48 GW). Coal-fired plants contributed ~20 GW (23%), gas-fired ~9 GW (10%), and oil/diesel/others a minor share (<1%). Renewables—primarily solar PV and onshore wind—grew rapidly post-2019, now representing ~25% of capacity but lower in actual generation due to intermittency. Hydropower remains significant (~20 GW, 23%) [EVN 2023 Annual Report; IEA 2024].

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by the **National Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8)**, approved in May 2023. PDP8 signals a strategic pivot:
- **Coal**: No new greenfield coal projects beyond those already under construction or committed pre-PDP8. Existing plants may be retrofitted or retired early.
- **Gas/LNG**: Emphasis on transitioning from domestic gas to **imported LNG** as domestic fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, Ca Voi Xanh) deplete. LNG terminals (e.g., Thi Vai, Nhon Hoi) are prioritized.
- **Renewables**: Target of **42.7–50.9 GW** solar/wind by 2030; coal share to decline to <15% of generation by 2030.
- **Net-zero**: Committed to net-zero emissions by **2050** (COP26 pledge), accelerating decarbonization pressure on thermal assets.

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Primary policy setter; approves PDPs and major investments.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates ~60% of generation capacity via subsidiaries (e.g., EVNGENCO 1–3).
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV)**: Regulates tariffs and grid access.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Approve land use and local permits.
- **Private Developers**: Increasing role in IPPs (Independent Power Producers), especially in LNG and renewables (e.g., PV Power, Binh Thuan Energy).

### Current Challenges
- **Grid Congestion**: Rapid solar/wind deployment in central/southern regions exceeds transmission capacity, causing curtailment.
- **Fuel Security**: Declining domestic gas output forces reliance on volatile global LNG markets; limited regasification infrastructure.
- **Financing**: International public finance for coal phased out (e.g., Japan, Korea); private lenders hesitant on new thermal projects.
- **Environmental Compliance**: Aging coal fleet faces stricter emissions standards; retrofitting costs high.
- **Policy Uncertainty**: PDP8 implementation details (e.g., retirement schedules, LNG pricing) remain fluid.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

*(Selected illustrative examples; full list in table below)*

**Pha Lai Thermal Power Plant**  
Located in Hai Duong Province, Pha Lai is one of Vietnam’s oldest coal plants. Phase 1 (2×110 MW) commissioned in 1994–1995 using subcritical technology. Phase 2 (2×300 MW) added in 2001–2002. Operated by EVNGENCO 3. All units remain operational despite age, though efficiency is low (~33%). No official retirement date announced, but likely candidate for phase-out under PDP8. Confidence: HIGH.

**Vung Ang 1 Thermal Power Plant**  
In Ha Tinh Province, this 1,200 MW supercritical coal plant (2×600 MW) was developed by state-owned Vinacomin and commissioned in 2017–2018. Faced delays due to environmental concerns and technical issues with boilers. Currently operational under EVNGENCO 1. Fuel sourced from domestic mines, though quality variability affects performance. Confidence: HIGH.

**Phu My 1–4 Gas Complex**  
A cluster of CCGT and OCGT plants in Ba Ria-Vung Tau Province, utilizing domestic gas from the Nam Con Son basin. Phu My 1 (715 MW CCGT) and Phu My 2–2.2 (715 MW CCGT) commissioned in 2002–2004; Phu My 3 (720 MW CCGT) in 2004; Phu My 4 (720 MW CCGT) in 2005. Operated by PV Power (majority state-owned). Output declining as gas field depletes; future conversion to LNG under study. Confidence: HIGH.

**Thai Binh 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
A controversial 1,200 MW (2×600 MW) subcritical coal project in Thai Binh Province, developed by private firm T&T Group. Construction began in 2015 but stalled repeatedly due to financing gaps and contractor disputes. As of 2024, only Unit 1 partially completed; Unit 2 not started. Officially “under construction” per MOIT but widely considered non-viable. COD delayed indefinitely. Confidence: MEDIUM (status uncertain).

**Long Phu 1 LNG-to-Power Project**  
Planned 1,200 MW CCGT plant in Soc Trang Province, to be fueled by imported LNG via the planned Long Phu LNG terminal. Originally slated for COD 2023, delayed due to lack of offtake agreement and terminal financing. Still listed in PDP8 but no FID achieved. Status: “planned.” Confidence: LOW (FID not confirmed).
