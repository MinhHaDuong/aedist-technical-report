# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024‑06‑15*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix (2023)
In 2023, Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity reached approximately **85 GW**, with thermal sources accounting for roughly **58%** of the mix:
- **Coal**: ~21 GW (≈25% of total capacity, ≈35% of generation)
- **Gas (domestic + LNG)**: ~7.5 GW (≈9% of capacity, ≈12% of generation)
- **Hydro**: ~22 GW  
- **Renewables (solar, wind, biomass)**: ~24 GW (mostly solar added 2019–2021)  
*(Sources: EVN Annual Report 2023; IEA Southeast Asia Energy Outlook 2023)*

Thermal generation remains critical for baseload and grid stability, though coal’s share in *generation* exceeds its *capacity* share due to higher utilization rates compared to intermittent renewables.

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by **Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg on 15 May 2023. Key features:
- **Coal**: No new greenfield coal plants beyond those already under construction or with investment approval as of PDP8 finalization. Focus shifts to efficiency upgrades and emissions controls.
- **Gas/LNG**: Accelerated deployment of LNG-to-power projects to replace aging domestic gas fields and support flexible generation. Target: ~15 GW gas/LNG capacity by 2030.
- **Decarbonization**: Net-zero by 2050 pledge (COP26); Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) signed in Dec 2022 with $15.5B commitment to support coal phase-down and renewable integration.

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Primary policy maker; approves PDPs and major investments.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates majority of transmission and many generation assets; sole off-taker for IPPs.
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV)**: Under MOIT; oversees licensing and tariffs.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Issue land and environmental permits.
- **Development Partners**: World Bank, ADB, JICA, USAID—provide financing and technical assistance, especially for LNG and grid modernization.

### Current Challenges
1. **Coal Overhang**: ~15 GW of pre-PDP8 coal projects remain in planning limbo; financial viability threatened by JETP commitments and carbon pricing considerations.
2. **LNG Infrastructure Delays**: Regasification terminals (e.g., Thi Vai, Nhon Hoi) face permitting, financing, and offtake agreement hurdles, delaying associated power plants.
3. **Grid Congestion**: Rapid solar/wind additions in central/southern regions strain transmission, limiting thermal dispatch flexibility.
4. **Fuel Security**: Declining output from domestic gas fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, PM3) increases reliance on imported LNG, exposing system to price volatility.
5. **Environmental Compliance**: Older subcritical coal units face pressure to retrofit flue gas desulfurization (FGD) and electrostatic precipitators (ESP); enforcement inconsistent.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

*Only representative examples shown due to space; full inventory in table below.*

**Pha Lai 1 & 2**  
Originally commissioned in 1994 (Unit 1) and 2001 (Unit 2), these Soviet/Vietnamese-built subcritical units (110 + 300 MWe) were among Vietnam’s first large-scale coal plants. Operated by PV Power (PetroVietnam subsidiary). Unit 1 retired in 2021 per PDP8 guidance; Unit 2 continues operation but faces retirement pressure under JETP. Confidence: HIGH.

**Vinh Tan 4**  
Developed by a consortium including China Southern Grid and Sumitomo, this 1,200 MWe supercritical plant (2×600) achieved COD in 2018. Notable for early adoption of supercritical technology and seawater cooling. Faced community protests over ash pond management. Now fully operational under EVN dispatch. Confidence: HIGH.

**Phu My 1–4 Complex**  
Located in Ba Ria–Vung Tau, this cluster represents Vietnam’s historic reliance on domestic gas from the Nam Con Son basin. Phu My 1 (715 MWe CCGT, 1999) and Phu My 2–1 (715 MWe, 2002) were built under BOT schemes. Phu My 3 (715 MWe, 2003) and Phu My 4 (4×150 = 600 MWe OCGT, 2004) followed. All units now face fuel supply constraints as field output declines. Confidence: HIGH.

**Long Phu 1**  
A 1,200 MWe supercritical coal plant developed by PetroVietnam Power Corporation (PV Power) in Soc Trang. Construction began in 2016 but stalled in 2018 due to contractor (ZPMC) disputes and financing issues. As of 2024, project status remains “under construction” per MOIT reports, though physical progress minimal. Confidence: MEDIUM (status uncertain).

**Thai Binh 2**  
Originally planned as a 2×600 MWe supercritical coal plant by T&T Group and Korea East-West Power. Faced repeated delays due to land acquisition and environmental concerns. Removed from PDP8 in 2023; officially cancelled in Q1 2024. Confidence: HIGH.

**Nghi Son 1 & 2**  
Two adjacent 600 MWe subcritical coal units in Thanh Hoa, developed by a consortium including Marubeni and KEPCO. Unit 1 COD 2019, Unit 2 COD 2020. Operate under long-term PPA with EVN. Notable for high SO₂ emissions; retrofits planned under JETP. Confidence: HIGH.

**Ca Na LNG-to-Power (Proposed)**  
Planned 3.2 GW LNG-fired complex in Ninh Thuan by AMACO and partners. Included in PDP7 Revised but omitted from PDP8 due to lack of FID and terminal delays. Status downgraded to “cancelled.” Confidence: HIGH.
