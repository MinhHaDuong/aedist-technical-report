# 1. Sector overview

## 1.1 Electricity mix and thermal role

Vietnam’s power system had about **80.6 GW of commercially accepted capacity at end-2023**. EVN reported the end-2023 mix as approximately **26.8 GW coal-fired thermal power**, **22.9 GW hydropower**, and **21.7 GW wind/solar**, with coal accounting for roughly **33% of installed capacity**. Total electricity generation and imports in 2023 were about **280.6 TWh**, and system peak demand was **46.3 GW**. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/Overview-of-national-power-sources-in-2023-66-142-4147.aspx?utm_source=openai))

Thermal generation remains central to reliability. Vietnam’s operating thermal fleet is dominated by **coal plants in the North/Central Coast/South-Central Coast and gas plants in the Southeast and Mekong Delta**. Coal provides baseload and dry-season reliability; domestic gas plants historically drew from Cửu Long, Nam Côn Sơn, PM3–CAA, and other offshore gas systems; future gas expansion increasingly depends on **LNG-to-power** because domestic fields are maturing or delayed.

## 1.2 Policy framework

Vietnam’s latest national power-development framework is the **Revised PDP8**, approved by **Prime Minister Decision 768/QĐ-TTg on 15 April 2025**, replacing the original PDP8 Decision 500/QĐ-TTg of 15 May 2023. The revised plan was operationalized by **MOIT Decision 1509/QĐ-BCT dated 30 May 2025**, which public summaries describe as containing project schedules and implementation appendices. ([kpmg.com](https://kpmg.com/vn/en/home/insights/2025/07/revised-pdp-8.html?utm_source=openai))

Public legal summaries of the revised implementation plan state that by 2030 the plan targets **14,930 MW of domestic gas-fired thermal power**, while revised-PDP8 commentary indicates that coal and LNG remain major source groups through 2030, with LNG projects treated as important transition and security assets. ([thuvienphapluat.vn](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/EN/Xay-dung-Do-thi/Decision-1509-QD-BCT-2025-Plan-for-implementation-of-amendment-to-National-Electricity-Development-Planning/663569/tieng-anh.aspx?utm_source=openai))

## 1.4 Current challenges

1. **Fuel security.** EVN reported 2023 coal-supply constraints and gas shortages, with Southeast gas supply lower than the demand needed to run gas turbines at maximum capacity. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/EVN-implements-a-series-of-urgent-measures-to-ensure-power-supply-in-the-dry-season-of-2023-66-163-3505.aspx?utm_source=openai))  
2. **LNG bankability.** LNG projects face bankability issues around tariff pass-through, minimum offtake, PPA risk allocation, exchange-rate risk, and fuel-index exposure.  
3. **Planning churn.** Original PDP8 in 2023, PDP8 implementation in 2024, and revised PDP8 plus new implementation plan in 2025 changed schedules and project priorities. ([vepg.vn](https://vepg.vn/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/PM-Decision-500-approving-PDP-VIII_150523.pdf?utm_source=openai))  
4. **Coal transition vs. reliability.** Vietnam has climate commitments and JETP obligations, but coal remains essential to system adequacy, especially in the North.  
5. **Transmission bottlenecks.** Coastal thermal clusters require 500 kV evacuation; delays in grid works can affect COD and dispatch.  
6. **Domestic gas depletion / delays.** Existing Southeast and Southwest gas supply is constrained, while major new supply such as Lô B–Ô Môn has historically been delayed.

# 3. Per-plant concise narratives

To keep the inventory usable, narratives are grouped where plants are physically and institutionally part of the same complex.

### Phả Lại 1–2 — Hải Dương  
Vietnam’s classic northern coal complex, built in two generations: older Soviet-era 110 MW units and later 300 MW units. It remains an important northern baseload plant but is aging and likely faces efficiency and environmental constraints. **Confidence: HIGH for existence/capacity; MEDIUM for unit-level retirement status.**

### Uông Bí — Quảng Ninh  
One of Vietnam’s oldest coal sites, expanded with 300 MW and 330 MW units. Legacy units have changed status over time, so available capacity and exact unit composition should be checked against current EVN dispatch data. **Confidence: MEDIUM.**

### Ninh Bình — Ninh Bình  
Small legacy coal plant exceeding the 30 MWe threshold. It is included for completeness despite its age and possible reserve/limited-dispatch role. **Confidence: MEDIUM–LOW for current availability.**

### Cao Ngạn, Na Dương, Sơn Động, Mạo Khê, Cẩm Phả — TKV coal fleet  
These are Vinacomin/TKV-linked coal plants, generally smaller CFB/subcritical units designed to burn domestic coal or lower-grade coal. They are important for mine-mouth utilization and regional supply. **Confidence: HIGH for existence and approximate capacity; MEDIUM for exact deratings.**

### Quảng Ninh, Hải Phòng, Mông Dương, Thăng Long — Northern coastal coal cluster  
These plants form a major northern thermal backbone. Quảng Ninh and Hải Phòng are EVN-linked 1,200 MW complexes; Mông Dương 1 is EVN-owned and Mông Dương 2 is a BOT project. Thăng Long is a private CFB coal plant. Northern coal plants were heavily dispatched during dry-season shortages, when EVN reported tight coal and gas supply. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/EVN-implements-a-series-of-urgent-measures-to-ensure-power-supply-in-the-dry-season-of-2023-66-163-3505.aspx?utm_source=openai))

### Thái Bình 1–2 and Thai Binh LNG  
Thái Bình 1 is an EVN coal plant; Thái Bình 2 is a PVN coal project that entered operation after major delays and contributed to the post-2022 coal-capacity increase reflected in EVN’s 2023 capacity data. Thai Binh LNG is now listed in revised-PDP8 implementation summaries with a 2028 schedule. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/Overview-of-national-power-sources-in-2023-66-142-4147.aspx?utm_source=openai))

### Nghi Sơn 1–2 and Nghi Sơn LNG  
Nghi Sơn 1 is EVN-owned; Nghi Sơn 2 is a large imported-coal BOT plant. A future LNG project at or near Nghi Sơn appears in revised-PDP8 summaries but needs direct appendix confirmation for schedule, investor and phasing. **Confidence: HIGH for coal plants; LOW–MEDIUM for LNG.**

### Vũng Áng 1–2 and Formosa captive power — Hà Tĩnh  
Vũng Áng 1 is PV Power’s operating 1,200 MW coal plant. Vũng Áng 2 is under construction as a foreign-invested BOT/imported-coal project. Formosa operates large industrial captive generation for its steel complex; exact electric capacity and unit configuration are less transparent and should be checked against plant permits and environmental approvals. **Confidence: HIGH for Vũng Áng; LOW for Formosa attributes.**

### Quảng Trạch 1 and Quảng Trạch II LNG — Quảng Bình  
Quảng Trạch 1 is an EVN coal project under construction. Earlier coal expansion plans for Quảng Trạch 2 have been effectively superseded in planning by LNG Quảng Trạch II, which public revised-PDP8 implementation summaries list for 2028. ([vietnamenergy.vn](https://vietnamenergy.vn/approval-of-the-implementation-plan-for-the-adjustment-of-pdp-viii-34295.html?utm_source=openai))

### Vĩnh Tân complex — Bình Thuận  
Vĩnh Tân is one of Vietnam’s largest coal clusters: BOT Vĩnh Tân 1, EVN’s Vĩnh Tân 2 and Vĩnh Tân 4 including extension. Vĩnh Tân 3 was planned but is treated as cancelled/suspended. The complex has been associated with environmental concerns, especially ash and dust management, and remains central to South-Central supply. **Confidence: HIGH for operating units; MEDIUM for cancellation characterization.**

### Duyên Hải complex — Trà Vinh  
Duyên Hải 1, 3, 3 Extension and BOT Duyên Hải 2 form a major Mekong-coastal coal complex. EVN specifically cited coal-supply measures for Duyên Hải 3 during 2023 dry-season supply stress. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/EVN-implements-a-series-of-urgent-measures-to-ensure-power-supply-in-the-dry-season-of-2023-66-163-3505.aspx?utm_source=openai))

### Sông Hậu and Long Phú — Mekong Delta coal  
Sông Hậu 1 is operating; Sông Hậu 2 remains uncertain. Long Phú 1 has been long delayed but appears in public revised-PDP8 implementation commentary with a 2026 schedule. Long Phú 2 and 3 are treated as cancelled/not proceeding. ([vietnamenergy.vn](https://vietnamenergy.vn/approval-of-the-implementation-plan-for-the-adjustment-of-pdp-viii-34295.html?utm_source=openai))

### Phú Mỹ gas complex — Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu  
Phú Mỹ is Vietnam’s core domestic-gas power hub, with EVN and BOT CCGT blocks including Phú Mỹ 1, 2.1, 2.2, 3 and 4. It relies on Southeast gas sources, which EVN has reported as constrained relative to full gas-turbine demand. ([en.evn.com.vn](https://en.evn.com.vn/d6/news/EVN-implements-a-series-of-urgent-measures-to-ensure-power-supply-in-the-dry-season-of-2023-66-163-3505.aspx?utm_source=openai))

### Bà Rịa, Thủ Đức, Cần Thơ/Trà Nóc, Hiệp Phước existing  
These are older gas/oil thermal assets, many with reserve, peaking, industrial, or legacy roles. They exceed the 30 MWe inclusion gate but require careful current-availability verification because nameplate, dependable capacity and market status may differ. **Confidence: LOW–MEDIUM.**

### Nhơn Trạch 1–4 — Đồng Nai  
Nhơn Trạch 1–2 are operating domestic-gas CCGTs owned by PV Power/PV Power NT2. Nhơn Trạch 3–4 are Vietnam’s first large LNG-fired CCGT pair and are under construction/commissioning around the mid-2020s. **Confidence: HIGH.**

### Cà Mau 1–2 — Cà Mau  
PV Power’s 1,500 MW Cà Mau complex is a major Southwest gas-fired CCGT asset using PM3–CAA gas. It is important for Mekong Delta supply and fertilizer/power gas allocation. **Confidence: HIGH.**

### Ô Môn 1–4 — Cần Thơ  
Ô Môn is designed around the Lô B–Ô Môn gas chain. Ô Môn 1 exists but has historically operated on oil or constrained gas arrangements; Ô Môn 2–4 are planned CCGT projects tied to upstream gas development. A 2024 PDP8 implementation summary listed Ô Môn IV at 1,050 MW with expected 2028 operation. ([frasersvn.com](https://www.frasersvn.com/legal-updates-and-publications/pdp-viii-implementation-plan?utm_source=openai))

### LNG pipeline projects — Long An, Bạc Liêu, Sơn Mỹ, Long Sơn, Cà Ná, Quảng Ninh, Thái Bình, Nghi Sơn, Quảng Trạch II, Hải Phòng, Quỳnh Lập/Nghệ An  
These projects form Vietnam’s future imported-LNG thermal portfolio. Revised-PDP8 summaries indicate continuing state support for LNG as a transition and security fuel, but project-level bankability remains uneven. Decision 1509 is the controlling schedule source, and public summaries identify Thai Binh LNG, Quang Trach II LNG and Quang Ninh LNG with 2028–2029 timing. ([vietnamenergy.vn](https://vietnamenergy.vn/approval-of-the-implementation-plan-for-the-adjustment-of-pdp-viii-34295.html?utm_source=openai))

### Cancelled / historical coal projects — Kiên Lương, Quỳnh Lập coal, Nam Định 1, Vĩnh Tân 3, Long Phú 2–3, Quảng Trạch 2 coal  
These are included because the user’s scope includes projects that appeared in formal planning cycles. Many were delayed by financing, environmental pressure, developer withdrawal, or replacement by LNG/renewable alternatives. **Confidence varies; LOW where only planning history is verified here.**
