## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Mix

Gas-fired generation accounts for just under 10% of Vietnam's installed power generation capacity, while coal-fired power plants account for about 30% of the country's electricity production. About 20 GW of power stations in Vietnam are coal-fired. As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction. As of April 2026, over 6 GW of coal-fired power plants are under construction in Vietnam and nearly 3 GW under development. The Phú Mỹ gas complex in Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu alone represents roughly 3,900 MW of gas-fired CCGT capacity. To date, about 30 coal-fired power plants have been developed and are operating.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

Vietnam's power sector is governed by a series of National Power Development Plans (PDPs). In May 2023 the Prime Minister issued Decision 500, approving the national power development plan known as PDP8, covering a term of 27 years (2023 to 2050). On 15 April 2025, the Prime Minister issued Decision 768 approving the Revised PDP8, which supersedes Decision 500. Subsequently, on 30 May 2025, the Ministry of Industry and Trade issued Decision 1509 approving the implementation plan for the Revised PDP8.

Key thermal-power policy parameters:

- **Coal:** Thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 under the PDP8 include coal with a total capacity of 30,127 MW. Within the PDP8, Vietnam cancels around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline, while keeping a pipeline of new coal projects to around 13 GW. Both plans restrict coal to projects under construction, capping 2030 capacity at ~31 GW. The 2025 plan sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050.
- **Domestic gas:** PDP8 puts a focus on power stations fired with domestic gas rather than the expansion of the infrastructure serving imported LNG. Maximum priority will be given to the commercialization of domestic gas assets (Block B, Blue Whale, and Yellow Leopard).
- **LNG:** Vietnamese officials have said that the country plans to build at least 15 LNG-fired power plants by 2035 with combined generation capacity of more than 22 GW. Under the revised PDP8, LNG-fired capacity is projected to reach 22 GW by 2030, accounting for roughly 9.5–12.3% of total installed capacity. LNG thermal power plants should be largely converted to hydrogen once the technology is commercialized.
- **Transition:** In December 2022, Vietnam and a coalition of countries led by the EU and UK concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) agreement with US$15.5 billion to fund the phase-out of unabated coal generation. The agreement proposed reducing Vietnam's coal fleet capacity from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030.

### 1.3 Key Institutional Actors

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)** | Regulator; issues implementation plans, tariff ranges, PPA frameworks |
| **Vietnam Electricity (EVN)** | State utility; sole offtaker; operates ~60% of generation fleet via GENCO 1/2/3 subsidiaries |
| **PetroVietnam (PVN)** | State oil & gas group; through PV Power operates gas plants and some coal plants |
| **Vinacomin (TKV)** | State coal mining group; owns mine-mouth coal plants |
| **BOT Developers** | Mitsubishi, KEPCO, Sumitomo, ACWA, Marubeni, Jaks/CPECC, AES (Mông Dương 2) |
| **Prime Minister's Office** | Approves PDPs, designates national priority projects |

### 1.4 Current Challenges

Vietnam faced power shortages in 2023, particularly in the North, with power supplied on alternative schedules for manufacturing, operations and residential areas. Of the approximately 14 gas-fired power projects currently underway, only the Nhon Trach 3 and 4 plants were expected to start operations in Q2 and Q3 of 2025. Many LNG projects are facing challenges in securing output offtake agreements to ensure stable cash flows, as well as in planning long-term fuel supply volumes and prices. Obstacles include time-consuming PPA and GCA negotiations, and the fact that LNG port infrastructure demands significant investment and must be completed before or alongside LNG power plants.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives (Selected)

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Plants

#### Phả Lại Power Complex (NĐ Phả Lại)
Hải Dương province. One of Vietnam's oldest and largest coal complexes. Phase 1 (4 × 110 MW = 440 MW, subcritical, Soviet-era units) commissioned in the 1980s; Phase 2 (2 × 300 MW = 600 MW, subcritical, Japanese-funded by JBIC) commissioned circa 2001–2002. Operated by EVN GENCO 1. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. **Confidence: HIGH** for existence and approximate capacity; MEDIUM for exact unit-level breakdown.

#### Uông Bí (NĐ Uông Bí)
Quảng Ninh province. Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi power station) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. Original units date to the 1960s–1970s; expansion units added over time. Total capacity approximately 630 MW across old and expanded phases. Operated by EVN GENCO 1 / TKV. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — exact current configuration and retirement of oldest units is uncertain.

#### Vĩnh Tân Power Center (NĐ Vĩnh Tân 1, 2, 4, 4MR)
Bình Thuận province. One of the largest coal complexes. In March 2019, Vietnam's State Audit Agency found that the 1,200 MW Vinh Tan 1 plant had violated environmental laws, including discharging cooling water without approval and nitrogen oxide emissions in excess of permit limits. Vĩnh Tân 2 (1,244 MW, EVN), Vĩnh Tân 4 (1,200 MW, EVN), Vĩnh Tân 4 Extension (600 MW, EVN). Total complex ~4,244 MW. Vĩnh Tân 1 is a BOT plant. **Confidence: HIGH** for major units.

#### Duyên Hải Power Center (NĐ Duyên Hải)
Trà Vinh province. Duyên Hải 1 (1,244 MW, EVN), Duyên Hải 3 (1,244 MW, EVN), Duyên Hải 3 Extension (688 MW, EVN GENCO 1). Duyên Hải 2 is a BOT plant developed by foreign investors. Total complex approximately 3,776 MW (including Duyên Hải 2 at ~600 MW). **Confidence: HIGH** for major units; MEDIUM for Duyên Hải 2 exact capacity.

#### Mông Dương (NĐ Mông Dương)
Quảng Ninh province. Mông Dương 1 (1,080 MW, EVN/TKV), Mông Dương 2 (1,200 MW, BOT by AES). Mông Dương 2 is among BOT plants for which Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting from 15% global minimum tax. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Nghi Sơn (NĐ Nghi Sơn)
Thanh Hóa province. Nghi Sơn 1 (600 MW, EVN). Nghi Son 2 is a joint venture with Japanese and Vietnamese partners with a capacity of 1,200 MW. BOT. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Vũng Áng (NĐ Vũng Áng)
Hà Tĩnh province. Vung Ang power station is an operating power station of at least 1,860 MW. Vũng Áng 1 (1,200 MW, EVN/PV Power). Vung Ang II (1.32 GW) was officially inaugurated in April 2026 as a USC project under BOT by Mitsubishi Corporation (60%) and KEPCO (40%). The project entered construction in 2021; the first turbine started operations in 2025 but was hit by a storm, delaying commercial operations. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Sông Hậu 1 (NĐ Sông Hậu 1)
Hậu Giang province. Song Hau Thermal Power Plant is an operating power station of at least 1,200 MW. Petrovietnam implements the Song Hau 1 Thermal Power Plant with a designed capacity of up to 1,200 MW. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Vân Phong 1 (NĐ BOT Vân Phong 1)
Khánh Hòa province. Developed by Sumitomo Corporation, the Van Phong 1 coal-fired power plant is located in Khanh Hoa province with a capacity of 1,320 MW. Van Phong power station is an operating power station of at least 1,432 MW. BOT. **Confidence: HIGH** for capacity; status recently changed to operating.

#### Na Dương (NĐ Na Dương)
Lạng Sơn province. Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002, and was completed in 2005. Phase 2: In July 2025, the unit was slated to be operational before December 31, 2026. In April 2026, the Prime Minister urged accelerated implementation of Na Duong Phase 2. **Confidence: HIGH** for Phase 1; Phase 2 ~110 MW under construction.

#### Thái Bình 2 (NĐ Thái Bình 2)
Thái Bình province. The Thai Binh 2 project is a 1,200 MW coal-fired power plant being developed by PVN (PetroVietnam). Despite delays and financial difficulties, the project is nearing completion. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Quảng Trạch 1 (NĐ Quảng Trạch 1)
Quảng Bình province. Operated by EVN, the Quang Trach 1 coal power plant in Quang Binh province is under construction with a capacity of 1,200 MW. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Nam Định 1 (NĐ Nam Định 1)
The Nam Dinh 1 project, a joint venture between Taekwang Power (South Korea) and ACWA Power (Saudi Arabia), is located in Nam Dinh province and will have a capacity of 1,200 MW, anticipated to begin operations by the late 2020s. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — financial close uncertain.

#### Cancelled/Shelved Coal Projects
Coal-fired power plants which can no longer be developed will be replaced by LNG or RE projects. This total capacity does not include the capacity of delayed/abandoned projects (Cong Thanh, Nam Dinh I, Quang Tri, Vinh Tan III, Song Hau II). Note: Some of these have been converted to LNG in planning (e.g., Công Thanh). **Confidence: MEDIUM** — status of individual shelved projects is fluid.

### 2.2 Gas-Fired Plants (Domestic Gas)

#### Phú Mỹ Complex (NĐ Phú Mỹ)
Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu province. Phú Mỹ Power Plants is a power generation complex located in Phú Mỹ, Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu province, with a combined capacity of 3,900 MW. The complex consists of Phú Mỹ 1, 2-1, 2-2, 3 and 4 power plants, all fuelled by natural gas. Gas is supplied from Bạch Hổ oil field and Nam Con Son Basin's gas fields.

- **Phú Mỹ 1:** A combined cycle plant operated by EVN, equipped with three gas turbines of 240 MW each and a steam turbine of 360 MW, full capacity 1,090 MW. Construction started September 1998, commissioned May 2000.
- **Phú Mỹ 2-1:** A 900 MW combined cycle power plant with six units, operated by EVN. Construction started 1995, commissioned 1997; extension commissioned end of 2002 by Siemens.
- **Phú Mỹ 2-2:** A 715 MW combined cycle power plant operated by Mekong Energy Limited (EDF/Sumitomo/TEPCO consortium), implemented as a 20-year BOT project. Now transferred to EVN.
- **Phú Mỹ 3:** A 746 MW CCGT, developed by a BOT consortium of BP, SembCorp, Kyushu Electric and Sojitz. Started commercial operation in 2004. Transferred to EVN in 2024.
- **Phú Mỹ 4:** A 450 MW combined-cycle power plant with two gas turbines and one steam turbine, operated by EVN, operational in 2004.

**Confidence: HIGH** for all Phú Mỹ plants.

#### Cà Mau 1 & 2 (NĐ Cà Mau)
Cà Mau province. Located in the Ca Mau Power–Gas–Fertilizer Complex, Ca Mau Power Plant 1 and 2 are large gas power plants belonging to Petrovietnam. Each ~750 MW CCGT, PV Power. Using gas from PM3-CAA field. COD circa 2008–2009. **Confidence: HIGH** for existence; capacity from known industry data.

#### Nhơn Trạch 1 & 2 (NĐ Nhơn Trạch)
Đồng Nai province. Nhơn Trạch 1 (~450 MW CCGT) and Nhơn Trạch 2 (~750 MW CCGT), PV Power. COD circa 2008–2009 and 2011–2012. **Confidence: HIGH** — well-established PV Power assets.

#### Bà Rịa (NĐ Bà Rịa)
Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu province. Older open-cycle gas turbine plant, ~389 MW. One of Vietnam's earliest gas-to-power projects. EVN GENCO 3. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — exact current operating capacity uncertain given age.

#### Dung Quất Power Complex (NĐ Dung Quất)
Quảng Ngãi province. Planned to use domestic gas from the Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) field. The gas from the Blue Whale field is expected to power the Dung Quat Power Complex, comprising three CCGT plants each about 750 MW. EVN is the developer of plants I and III, and Sembcorp is in charge of plant II. Total capacity 2,250 MW. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — dependent on upstream gas development.

#### Ô Môn Complex (NĐ Ô Môn)
Cần Thơ province. Planned to use gas from Block B (Ô Môn). Ô Môn I (~660 MW, operating as OCGT/partial CCGT); Ô Môn II, III, IV planned (~750 MW each). The Block B Ô Môn and Hiệp Phước 1 combined cycle power plants, with a combined capacity of 6,634 MW, are considered the only other projects that could become commercially operational before 2030. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — Ô Môn I operating confirmed; others pre-FID.

### 2.3 LNG Plants

#### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (NĐ Nhơn Trạch 3&4)
Vietnam's first LNG-fueled power plants, Nhon Trach 3 and 4, were inaugurated on December 14, 2025, in Dong Nai province. The two plants have combined investment capital of about USD 1.4 billion and total capacity of 1,624 MW. PV Power serves as the investor, while a consortium of Lilama and Samsung C&T serves as the EPC contractor. The plants are equipped with GE's 9HA.02 gas turbines, expected to achieve an efficiency of 62–64%. The plants have officially entered commercial operation. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Thái Bình LNG (NĐ LNG Thái Bình)
A $2.56 billion LNG project in Thai Binh province, designed with two turbines (first online Q4/2028, second Q4/2029). Approved December 2023, jointly invested by Tokyo Gas, Kyuden Group, and Truong Thanh Vietnam Group. With an installed capacity of 1,500 MW, the plant will utilize CCGT technology. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Other Planned LNG
According to Decision 1509, Thai Binh LNG is among 11 projects set for operation in 2028–2029, including: Quang Ninh LNG (1,500 MW); Quang Trach II LNG (1,500 MW); Hai Lang LNG Phase 1 (1,500 MW); Hiep Phuoc LNG Phase 1 (1,200 MW); Long An I LNG (1,500 MW); Son My I (2,250 MW); Son My II (2,250 MW); Nghi Son LNG (1,500 MW); Ca Na LNG.

Additional projects from the Revised PDP8: LNG Hai Phong Phase I (1,600 MW) and LNG Hiep Phuoc Phase II (1,500 MW) for the 2025–2030 period. For 2031–2035: LNG Cong Thanh (1,500 MW), LNG Hai Phong Phase II (3,200 MW), and LNG Quang Trach III (1,500 MW).
