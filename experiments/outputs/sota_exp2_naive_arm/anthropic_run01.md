## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Electricity Generation Mix

In 2024, Vietnam's total electricity generation reached 308 TWh, with coal-fired power contributing the largest share at 148 TWh—48% of the total. Hydropower followed at 98 TWh (32%); solar and wind generation together produced 39 TWh (13%); gas-fired power accounted for 23.4 TWh, or 7% of total generation.

The power mix is 54% from thermal, including 44% from coal. Around 9.3 GW of coal plants have been commissioned since 2020, leading to high coal imports. Vietnam's electricity mix includes roughly 48% coal, 34% hydropower, and 8% solar.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

On May 15, 2023, the Vietnamese government released PDP8 for 2021–2030 with a vision to 2050. Its release was severely delayed due to disagreements regarding the future power mix, particularly the pace of coal phase-down and expansion of renewables.

On 15 April 2025, the Prime Minister issued Decision 768/QD-TTg, approving the Revised PDP8, which supersedes Decision 500/QD-TTg dated 15 May 2023. Subsequently, on 30 May 2025, MOIT issued Decision 1509/QD-BCT approving the implementation plan for the Revised PDP8.

Key thermal capacity targets under the Revised PDP8:

- **Coal:** Coal-fired power capacity will be capped at 31,055 MW through 2030, with no new coal projects to be approved thereafter.
- **Domestic gas:** By 2030, the total capacity of domestic gas-fired power plants shall reach 14,930 MW.
- **LNG:** The updated plan projects 22,524 MW of LNG capacity by 2030.
- The thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas, LNG, and coal with a total capacity of 14,930 MW, 22,400 MW, and 30,127 MW respectively.

Within the PDP8, Vietnam cancels around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline, while keeping a pipeline of new coal projects to around 13 GW. The PDP8 still allows completion of eleven coal-fired power plants already included in the revised PDP7.

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **Coal import dependence:** Vietnam imported 42 million tonnes of thermal coal in 2024, a 27% year-on-year increase, primarily from Indonesia. Vietnam is the world's fifth-largest coal importer in 2025 and the leading coal importer in Southeast Asia.
- **LNG infrastructure delays:** Vietnam's 2031 LNG target of 25.1 million tonnes requires ~$10 billion in terminal investments, but only two projects are currently under construction. As of late 2025, delays in building infrastructure and global shortages of gas turbines had led to significant delays in installed capacity for LNG.
- **JETP commitments:** In December 2022, Vietnam concluded a JETP agreement with US$15.5 billion to cap and phase out unabated coal generation—reducing coal fleet capacity from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030.
- **Aging fleet:** Vietnam's oldest power station (Uông Bí) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. The Phả Lại Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade.

## 2. PLANT-BY-PLANT NARRATIVES

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Power Plants

#### Phả Lại (Pha Lai) — Hải Dương Province
Vietnam's largest legacy coal complex. **Phase 1** (4 × 110 MW = 440 MW) commissioned 1983–1986, Soviet-designed subcritical units using domestic anthracite. **Phase 2** (2 × 300 MW = 600 MW) commissioned 2001–2002 with Japanese ODA financing, subcritical. Owned by EVN through Pha Lai Thermal Power JSC (PPC). Units started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — capacity figures from GEM and Wikipedia; exact COD dates from MOIT Report 58/BC-CBT.

#### Uông Bí (Uong Bi) — Quảng Ninh Province
Vietnam's oldest power station (Uông Bí) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. Original Soviet units from the 1960s–1970s; expansion unit(s) added subsequently. Mine-mouth plant using Quảng Ninh anthracite. Total capacity approximately 630 MW across multiple phases. Owned by Vinacomin/TKV subsidiary. **Confidence: LOW–MEDIUM** — exact current operating capacity and decommissioning status of oldest units uncertain.

#### Na Dương (Na Duong) — Lạng Sơn Province
Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100 MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002 and was completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine. As of October 2025, a biomass co-firing test was being conducted at Na Dương-1. **Phase 2** (Na Dương-2): In December 2009, Vinacomin signed an MOU with Marubeni for construction of a second 100 MW phase. Construction started in 2015; financing secured November 2024. **Confidence: HIGH** for Phase 1; MEDIUM for Phase 2.

#### Mông Dương (Mong Duong) — Quảng Ninh Province
Two separate plants at a single complex. **Mông Dương 1** (2 × 500 MW = 1,000 MW): EVN/EVNGENCO1, subcritical, COD ~2015. **Mông Dương 2** (2 × 600 MW = 1,200 MW): BOT project, AES Corporation (now AES-VCM Mong Duong), COD 2015. Listed among seven BOT coal plants considered for global minimum tax exemption in September 2025. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — capacity well attested; exact COD dates cross-referenced with GEM.

#### Vĩnh Tân (Vinh Tan) Power Center — Bình Thuận Province
Multi-plant complex in Tuy Phong district. **Vĩnh Tân 1** (2 × 600 MW = 1,200 MW): Chinese BOT (CGNPC consortium), COD 2018. In March 2019, Vietnam's State Audit Agency found environmental violations including discharging cooling water without approval. **Vĩnh Tân 2** (2 × 622 MW = 1,244 MW): Operating since 2014. Located in Bình Thuận. EVNGENCO 3 has equity stakes. **Vĩnh Tân 4** (2 × 600 MW = 1,200 MW): EVN, subcritical, COD ~2017–2019. **Vĩnh Tân 4 Extension** (1 × 600 MW): COD ~2020. **Confidence: HIGH** for operating plants.

#### Duyên Hải (Duyen Hai) Power Center — Trà Vinh Province
Duyên Hải I & III is a 2,488 MW thermal power project; commissioned in 2016. **Duyên Hải 1** (2 × 622 MW = 1,244 MW, EVN/EVNGENCO1). **Duyên Hải 3** (2 × 622 MW = 1,244 MW, EVN). **Duyên Hải 3 Extension** (1 × 688 MW, COD ~2019, EVNGENCO1). Duyên Hải II (1,320 MW) came online in 2021, developed by Teknik Janakuasa as a BOT project. **Confidence: HIGH.**

#### Vũng Áng (Vung Ang) — Hà Tĩnh Province
An operating power station of at least 1,860 MW. **Vũng Áng 1** (2 × 600 MW = 1,200 MW): EVN/EVNGENCO2, subcritical, COD ~2014–2015. **Vũng Áng 2** (2 × 600 MW = 1,200 MW): Being developed by a consortium that includes Mitsubishi Corporation and KEPCO. Under construction as of 2025. **Vũng Áng 3** (1,200 MW): Samsung signed an MOU in October 2014 to build the $2.5 billion plant — subsequently cancelled under PDP8. **Confidence: HIGH** for Vũng Áng 1; MEDIUM for Vũng Áng 2.

## 5. RECONCILIATION NOTES AND KNOWN DISCREPANCIES

1. **Coal capacity totals:** About 20 GW of power stations in Vietnam are coal-fired (Wikipedia, citing pre-2020 data). The GEM tracker estimated 24.7 GW as of January 2023. My inventory totals ~25.5 GW operating, reflecting commissioning of Thai Binh 2, Nghi Son 2, Hai Duong BOT, and Van Phong 1 since then. This is plausible but should be validated against MOIT's latest official statistics.

2. **Vinh Tan 2 capacity:** Some sources report 1,244 MW (2 × 622 MW), others 1,200 MW. The 622 MW per-unit figure comes from Power Technology, which states "the 1,244MW thermal project."

3. **Duyen Hai complex:** Power Technology describes "Duyên Hải I & III" as "a 2,488 MW" project. This appears to combine DH1 (1,244 MW) and DH3 (1,244 MW) but excludes DH3 Extension (688 MW) and the separate DH2 BOT (1,320 MW).

4. **Nhon Trach 3&4 capacity:** Combined capacity of 1,624 MW (official PV Power figure). Earlier reports cited 1,300–1,760 MW range. The 2 × 812 MW figure is now confirmed.

5. **PDP8 vs. Revised PDP8 coal cap:** The original PDP8 set ~30,127 MW coal by 2030. The revised PDP8 caps coal at 31,055 MW. The slight increase reflects adjusted planning for plants already under construction.

6. **LNG pipeline realism:** Only two LNG terminal projects (combined 6 MTPA capacity) are currently under construction and expected operational by 2027, against a 2031 target of 25.1 MTPA. Most LNG power projects in the table are aspirational.

## 7. LIMITATIONS AND CAVEATS

1. **Completeness:** This inventory aims to be comprehensive for >30 MW thermal assets but may miss some smaller industrial/captive CHP plants (e.g., cement, sugar, steel industries) that do not appear in national power planning documents. The Formosa Ha Tinh captive plant is included as a notable example, but other industrial captive assets may exist below the reporting radar.

2. **Status lag:** Plant statuses change continuously. The "Status as-of" column reflects the most recent verifiable date from public sources, which may lag actual conditions by months.

3. **Capacity precision:** Many Vietnamese plants' capacities are reported inconsistently across sources (e.g., nameplate vs. net; summer vs. winter rating). All figures should be treated as ±5% unless specifically verified from commissioning certificates.

4. **LNG pipeline uncertainty:** The majority of planned LNG projects lack final investment decisions, secured gas supply agreements, or completed PPA negotiations. Brent-linked LNG contracts in 2024 ranged from $8.86 to $11.90/MMBtu, undermining gas competitiveness versus coal. Many of these projects may never reach construction.

5. **Source verification:** No fabricated URLs have been included. Where exact URLs could not be confirmed, "URL not verified" is noted. Wikipedia and GEM Wiki are treated as secondary sources that aggregate primary Vietnamese-language reporting; wherever possible, the underlying primary source (MOIT reports, press releases, corporate filings) is cited alongside.

6. **Double-counting risk:** The Duyên Hải and Vĩnh Tân complexes each contain multiple separately financed plants sharing a site name. Care has been taken to list each separately, but some aggregate figures in the literature (e.g., "Duyên Hải Power Center 4,496 MW") combine all phases.
