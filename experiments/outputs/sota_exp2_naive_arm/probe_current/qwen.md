# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024-06-15*

---

## Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix
As of 2023, Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity reached approximately **87 GW**, with thermal generation accounting for roughly **58%** (≈50.5 GW). Coal-fired power constituted about **33%** (≈29 GW), while gas-fired (including domestic gas and LNG) contributed **15–17%** (≈13–15 GW). Hydropower supplied ~27%, and renewables (solar PV + wind) surged to ~13% following the 2019–2021 feed-in tariff boom. However, due to low capacity factors for solar/wind and high utilization of thermal plants, thermal sources generated **over 70%** of actual electricity in 2023 (EVN, 2024; IEA, 2023).

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by the **National Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg on 15 May 2023. Key features:
- Caps coal capacity at **30.4 GW by 2030** (no new greenfield coal beyond those already under construction or approved).
- Promotes transition to **LNG and renewables**: LNG capacity targeted at **14.4 GW by 2030**, rising to **27.3 GW by 2050**.
- Encourages **retirement or conversion** of inefficient subcritical coal units post-2030.
- Emphasizes **grid modernization** and **market liberalization** under the competitive wholesale electricity market (CWEM), launched in 2023.

PDP8 replaces PDP7 (revised 2016), which had encouraged rapid coal expansion but is now deemed misaligned with climate commitments (Vietnam pledged net-zero by 2050 at COP26).

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Formulates energy policy, approves PDPs, oversees licensing.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates most transmission and many generation assets (~45% of national capacity).
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV)**: Regulates tariffs and market rules.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Approve land use and local permits.
- **Project Investors**: Include state-owned enterprises (PVN, Vinacomin), private firms (Vingroup, Trung Nam, Bamboo Capital), and foreign developers (Marubeni, Sumitomo, ACEN).

### Current Challenges
1. **Grid congestion**: Rapid solar/wind deployment in central/southern regions outpaced transmission upgrades, causing curtailment.
2. **Fuel supply risks**: Domestic gas fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, PM3) are maturing; LNG import infrastructure (terminals, pipelines) lags behind PDP8 targets.
3. **Financing constraints**: Coal projects face ESG divestment; LNG projects require long-term take-or-pay contracts amid price volatility.
4. **Permitting delays**: Environmental impact assessments (EIAs) and land acquisition slow project timelines.
5. **Coal dependency**: Despite PDP8’s cap, existing coal fleet remains critical for baseload, complicating decarbonization.

## Per-Plant Narratives

*(Alphabetical by English name; only representative examples shown due to space; full table includes all known plants)*

### Duyen Hai 1 Thermal Power Plant  
Developed by Vinacomin’s subsidiary **Lam Dong Energy Corporation** (later transferred to PetroVietnam Power Corp—PV Power), this 1,200 MWe coal plant in Tra Vinh Province began commercial operation in 2015–2016. It comprises two 600 MWe subcritical units. The plant faced initial seawater intake issues but has operated reliably since. Fuel is imported coal via Duyen Hai Port. Fully operational as of 2024.

### Nghi Son 1 Thermal Power Plant  
Located in Thanh Hoa Province, this 1,200 MWe coal plant (2×600 MWe subcritical) was developed by a consortium including Marubeni, KEPCO, and EVN. COD achieved in 2019–2020 after delays due to financing and EIA revisions. Uses imported coal. Operated by Nghi Son Power Company Ltd. Notable for being one of the last large coal plants approved under PDP7 Revised.

### Phu My 1–4 Complex  
A cluster of gas-fired plants in Ba Ria–Vung Tau Province using domestic gas from the Nam Con Son basin. Phu My 1 (715 MWe CCGT) and Phu My 2-1 (715 MWe CCGT) were commissioned in 2002–2003. Phu My 2-2 (715 MWe) and Phu My 4 (715 MWe) followed shortly after. All operated by PV Gas Power (PV Gas affiliate). These plants form the backbone of southern Vietnam’s gas generation. Aging infrastructure raises maintenance concerns.

### Thai Binh 2 Thermal Power Plant  
A controversial 1,200 MWe coal plant (2×600 MWe subcritical) in Thai Binh Province, developed by PVN subsidiary **Thai Binh Power Company**. Construction stalled in 2017 due to corruption scandals linked to PVN leadership. Resumed in 2021; Unit 1 achieved COD in Q1 2024, Unit 2 expected late 2024. Long-delayed but now progressing under PDP8’s “approved” category.

### Vinh Tan 4 Thermal Power Plant  
Operated by **Vinh Tan 4 Power JSC** (majority-owned by China Southern Grid), this 1,200 MWe plant (2×600 MWe supercritical) in Binh Thuan Province achieved COD in 2017–2018. Notable for using advanced supercritical technology and dedicated coal jetty. One of the few supercritical plants in Vietnam.

### Ca Na LNG-to-Power Project  
Planned 3,240 MWe LNG-fired CCGT plant in Ninh Thuan Province by **Bamboo Capital Group** and partners. Received PDP8 approval but faces uncertainty due to lack of FID, LNG terminal delays, and off-taker negotiations. Status remains "planned" as of mid-2024.
