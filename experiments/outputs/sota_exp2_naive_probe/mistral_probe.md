## **Sector Overview: Vietnam’s Thermal Power Sector**

### **1. Evolution of the National Electricity Generation Mix**

#### **Installed Capacity (GW) and Generation (TWh)**
- **2000:** ~10 GW (mostly hydro and coal), ~30 TWh.
- **2010:** ~21 GW (hydro 38%, coal 20%, gas 30%), ~80 TWh.
- **2020:** ~69 GW (hydro 30%, coal 34%, gas 14%, renewables 22%), ~240 TWh.
- **2025 (projected):** ~100 GW (coal 30%, gas 15%, LNG 10%, renewables 40%), ~400 TWh.
- **2030 (target):** ~130 GW (coal 25%, gas 15%, LNG 15%, renewables 40%), ~570 TWh.

**Sources:**
- Vietnam Electricity (EVN) Annual Reports (2010–2023)
- Vietnam Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8, 2021–2030, vision to 2045)
- IEA Vietnam Energy Outlook (2021)

#### **Key Trends**
- Rapid growth in coal capacity since 2010, peaking in the mid-2020s.
- Gas and LNG expected to replace coal in the 2030s.
- Renewables (solar, wind) surged post-2018, now a major share of capacity but lower capacity factors.

---

### **2. Policy Framework**

#### **Regulation and Planning**
- **Electricity Law (2004, amended 2012, 2020):** Governs the power sector, including generation, transmission, and distribution.
- **Power Development Plans (PDPs):** Issued every 5–10 years by the Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT). The latest is **PDP8 (2021–2030, vision to 2045)**, approved in May 2023.
  - Sets targets for capacity, generation, and fuel mix.
  - Introduces LNG as a major fuel source post-2025.
  - Aims to reduce coal’s share from 34% (2020) to 20% (2030).
- **Direct Power Purchase Agreements (DPPA):** Pilot program launched in 2022 to allow direct sales between generators and large consumers.

#### **Reforms and Goals**
- **Market Liberalization:** Gradual transition to a competitive wholesale electricity market (Vietnam Wholesale Electricity Market, VWEM).
- **Net-Zero Pledge:** Vietnam committed to net-zero by 2050 at COP26 (2021), with intermediate targets for renewables and LNG.
- **Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP):** $15.5 billion pledge from G7 and partners to accelerate coal phase-out and renewables deployment.

**Sources:**
- MOIT, Decision No. 500/QD-TTg (PDP8, 2023)
- Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV) reports
- IEA, “Vietnam 2021: Energy Policy Review”

---

### **3. Energy Supply Landscape**

#### **Domestic Resources**
- **Coal:** Mostly anthracite from Quang Ninh province. Reserves ~3.5 billion tons, but declining quality and increasing extraction costs.
- **Gas:** Offshore fields in the Mekong Delta (Bach Ho, Nam Con Son basin). Production peaked in 2010s, now declining.
- **Hydropower:** Most large sites already developed; further expansion limited by environmental and social constraints.

#### **Imports**
- **Coal:** Imports from Indonesia, Australia, and Russia (2023: ~50 million tons).
- **LNG:** First LNG terminal (Thi Vai, 1 MTPA) operational in 2023. Additional terminals planned (Son My, Ca Na).
- **Electricity:** Limited imports from China and Laos (hydropower).

#### **Infrastructure**
- **Coal Terminals:** Major ports at Quang Ninh, Cam Pha, Nghi Son.
- **Gas Pipelines:** Nam Con Son, PM3, and Cuu Long basins.
- **LNG Terminals:** Thi Vai (operational), Son My (under construction), Ca Na (planned).
- **Interconnectors:** 220kV and 500kV transmission lines to China and Laos.

**Sources:**
- Vietnam National Coal and Mineral Industries Group (Vinacomin) reports
- PetroVietnam (PVN) annual reports
- PDP8 (2023)

### **5. Current Challenges**

#### **Energy Trilemma**
- **Energy Security:** Declining domestic gas production, reliance on coal imports, delays in LNG infrastructure.
- **Affordability:** Subsidized electricity tariffs, rising fuel costs, financial stress on EVN.
- **Environmental Sustainability:** Air pollution from coal, CO2 emissions, pressure to phase out coal.

#### **International Markets**
- **Coal:** Price volatility, geopolitical risks (Indonesia export bans, Russia sanctions).
- **LNG:** Global price spikes, long-term supply contracts, infrastructure bottlenecks.
- **Financing:** International banks and investors withdrawing from coal projects; JETP funding conditional on coal phase-out.

#### **Domestic Issues**
- **Grid Constraints:** Congestion, especially in the south (renewables curtailment).
- **Land Use:** Conflicts over land acquisition for power plants and transmission lines.
- **Workforce:** Skills gap for new technologies (LNG, renewables, smart grids).
