# Vietnam Thermal Power Sector Inventory (≥30 MWe)

## Sector Overview

### Evolution of the National Electricity Generation Mix

Vietnam’s electricity sector has undergone rapid transformation since economic liberalization (*Đổi Mới*) in 1986. Historically dominated by hydropower, the generation mix has shifted decisively toward thermal sources—first domestic gas, then coal—as demand surged.

- **Installed Capacity (End-2023)**: ~85 GW total national capacity  
  - Thermal (coal + gas): ~47 GW (55%)  
    - Coal: ~22 GW  
    - Gas (mostly domestic): ~7.5 GW  
    - Oil/diesel (minor): <1 GW  
  - Hydro: ~22 GW (26%)  
  - Renewables (solar + wind): ~16 GW (19%) — largely added post-2019  

- **Electricity Generation (2022)**: ~295 TWh  
  - Coal: ~125 TWh (42%)  
  - Hydro: ~85 TWh (29%)  
  - Gas: ~40 TWh (14%)  
  - Solar/Wind: ~35 TWh (12%)  
  - Oil: ~10 TWh (3%)  

*(Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; IEA Southeast Asia Energy Outlook 2023)*

The shift began in the 2000s with gas-fired plants fueled by offshore fields (e.g., Nam Côn Sơn, Lan Tây–Lan Đỏ). Coal expansion accelerated under the Power Development Plan VII (PDP VII, 2011) and its revision (PDP VII Revised, 2016), which envisioned ~55 GW of coal by 2030. However, environmental concerns, financing constraints, and renewable cost declines led to a major policy pivot in PDP VIII (approved May 2023), which caps coal at ~30 GW by 2030 and emphasizes LNG and renewables.

### Policy Framework

Vietnam’s power sector is centrally planned through **Power Development Plans (PDPs)** issued by the Prime Minister upon recommendation by the Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT). Key plans:

- **PDP VI (2007)**: Emphasized gas and hydro; limited coal.
- **PDP VII (2011)**: Launched large-scale coal program (~75 GW total capacity by 2030).
- **PDP VII Revised (2016)**: Reduced coal target but still aggressive (~55 GW coal by 2030).
- **PDP VIII (Decision No. 500/QD-TTg, May 15, 2023)**: Landmark shift:
  - Peak coal capacity capped at **30.2 GW** (no new greenfield projects beyond those already approved).
  - LNG import capacity target: **14.7 Bcm/year by 2030**, supporting ~22.4 GW gas/LNG capacity.
  - Renewables (excluding hydro): ~40 GW by 2030.
  - Net-zero by 2050 (COP26 commitment).

Reforms include gradual unbundling of state-owned utility **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**, though generation remains dominated by EVN subsidiaries and state-linked firms (e.g., PVN, TKV). Independent Power Producer (IPP) framework exists but faces challenges in PPAs and bankability.

### Energy Supply Landscape

- **Coal**: Vietnam has modest domestic reserves (~3.4 billion tonnes, mostly low-quality lignite in Quang Ninh). Domestic production peaked at ~48 Mt in 2020 but is declining. Over 60% of coal for power is now imported (Indonesia, Australia, Russia).
- **Gas**: Historically reliant on **domestic offshore fields** (Nam Côn Sơn basin: Lan Tây, Lan Đỏ, Rồng; PM3-Cà Mau). Production peaked ~10 Bcm/year (~2015) and is now declining. New supply depends on **imported LNG**.
- **LNG Infrastructure**: First terminal (Thị Vải, 4 MMtpa) operational since 2022 (PV GAS). Additional terminals planned: Son My (Bình Thuận), Nghi Sơn (Thanh Hóa), Duyên Hải (Trà Vinh), Thị Vải expansion.
- **Interconnectors**: Limited cross-border imports (<2 GW from China, Laos, Cambodia), primarily for local balancing, not bulk supply.

### Key Institutional Actors

| Entity | Role | Influence |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)** | Formulates PDPs, issues investment decisions, regulates tariffs | Highest policy authority |
| **Prime Minister (PM)** | Approves PDPs and major projects (>220 kV or strategic) | Final approval gate |
| **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)** | State utility; owns ~50% of generation, all transmission, most distribution | Dominant market player; slow to adapt to IPPs |
| **Vietnam Oil and Gas Group (PVN)** | State oil/gas company; owns gas fields, LNG terminals, gas-fired plants via PV GAS/PV Power | Controls gas supply chain |
| **Vietnam National Coal-Mineral Industries Group (TKV)** | State coal miner; owns coal mines and some coal plants | Declining influence as imports rise |
| **Provincial People’s Committees** | Issue land permits, environmental approvals; often promote local projects | Strong local veto power |
| **Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment (MONRE)** | Environmental impact assessments (EIA) | Increasingly stringent, especially for coal |
| **Development Finance Institutions (DFIs)** | Provide project finance (e.g., JICA, KEXIM, ADB) | Critical for large IPPs; now restricting coal |

Relationships are hierarchical: MOIT → PM for approvals; EVN/PVN/TKV report to MOIT; provinces implement locally but defer to central ministries on strategic energy.

### Current Challenges

- **Energy Trilemma**: 
  - **Security**: Rapid demand growth (~10%/yr pre-pandemic) strains grid; coal plant delays create supply gaps.
  - **Affordability**: Rising fuel import bills (coal, LNG) pressure tariffs; EVN incurred ~$2.4B loss in 2022.
  - **Sustainability**: Coal emissions (~200 Mt CO₂/yr) conflict with net-zero pledge; air/water pollution from plants sparks protests.
- **International Markets**: Sanctions risk (Russia coal), LNG price volatility (post-Ukraine war), and withdrawal of public finance for coal (G7, OECD) limit options.
- **Grid Constraints**: Renewable zones (Central Highlands, South) lack transmission; curtailment reached 15% in 2020.

## Per-Plant Discussion

*Note: Only representative or high-profile plants discussed here due to space; full inventory in table.*

### Pha Lai Thermal Power Plant (Hải Dương)
- **History**: Phase I (2×110 MW) commissioned 1989–1991; Phase II (2×300 MW) added 2001–2003. One of Vietnam’s first large coal plants.
- **Issues**: Relied on low-grade domestic anthracite; faced efficiency and emission challenges. Unit 1 retired in 2020.
- **Status Confidence**: HIGH (EVN annual reports confirm retirement).

### Phú Mỹ Power Complex (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu)
- **History**: Built 1998–2004 across 6 plants (Phú Mỹ 1–4, combined cycle; Phú Mỹ 2–2 OCGT). Total ~3,900 MW. Fueled by Nam Côn Sơn gas.
- **Issues**: Gas supply curtailments post-2015 reduced output. Now transitioning to LNG co-firing studies.
- **Status Confidence**: HIGH (PV Power filings, MOIT data).

### Vĩnh Tân Power Center (Bình Thuận)
- **History**: Planned as 5,200 MW complex (4 plants). Vĩnh Tân 1 (BOT, 1,200 MW) COD 2018; VT2 (EVN, 1,200 MW) COD 2015; VT4 (TKV, 1,200 MW) COD 2019. VT3 (1,200 MW) canceled in PDP VIII.
- **Issues**: Severe ash pond overflow in 2016 caused marine pollution; protests led to stricter EIA rules. VT3 cancellation reflects coal cap.
- **Status Confidence**: HIGH (Government decisions, company reports).

### Long Phú 1 Thermal Power Plant (Sóc Trăng)
- **History**: Planned as 1,200 MW Russian-financed (Zarubezhneft) supercritical coal plant. Groundbreaking 2016.
- **Issues**: Sanctions on Russian entities post-2022 halted construction. Officially “suspended” in PDP VIII.
- **Status Confidence**: MEDIUM (Multiple news reports; no formal cancellation decree found).

### Thái Bình 2 Thermal Power Plant (Thái Bình)
- **History**: 600 MW Russian-backed (Power Machines) plant. Construction started 2011.
- **Issues**: Massive delays due to corruption scandal (PetroVietnam officials jailed); resumed 2021. COD expected 2024.
- **Status Confidence**: MEDIUM (MOIT statements, VnExpress coverage).

### Duyên Hải Power Complex (Trà Vinh)
- **History**: DH1 (2×622 MW) COD 2015; DH3 (2×622 MW) COD 2017. DH2 (planned 2×600 MW) suspended indefinitely.
- **Issues**: Ash disposal controversies; DH2 dropped due to overcapacity concerns.
- **Status Confidence**: HIGH (EVN reports).

### LNG-Based Projects (e.g., Sơn Mỹ 1, Nghi Sơn LNG-to-Power)
- **History**: Announced post-2020 as PDP VII replacements for coal. Sơn Mỹ 1 (2,000 MW) planned by AES/PEA.
- **Issues**: Offtake uncertainty (no standardized LNG PPA); financing challenges amid high global prices.
- **Status Confidence**: LOW–MEDIUM (MOIT announcements; no final investment decisions confirmed).
