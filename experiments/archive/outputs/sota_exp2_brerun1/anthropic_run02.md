## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

Vietnam's installed power capacity exceeded 80 GW by end-2024, with thermal generation (coal, gas, oil) providing the majority of baseload electricity. As of January 2023, Vietnam had an estimated 24.7 GW of operating coal-fired capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction.

The national Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8), approved as Decision 500/QĐ-TTg (15 May 2023) and its Implementation Plan (Decision 262, 1 April 2024), set thermal targets through 2030: **30,127 MW coal; 14,930 MW domestic gas; 22,400 MW LNG**. The amended PDP8, issued as Decision 768 in April 2025, adjusted LNG capacity to 22,524 MW by adding Long Sơn and Long An II projects for the 2031–2035 period.

Of the 13 original LNG projects totaling 22,400 MW, only 3 projects with 2,824 MW were under construction on schedule by early 2025. Nhơn Trạch 3&4 (1,624 MW) entered commercial operation as Vietnam's first LNG-fired combined-cycle power plant.

Key actors include EVN and its generation subsidiaries (Genco 1, 2, 3), PVN/PVPower, Vinacomin/TKV, and foreign BOT investors (AES, Sumitomo, Marubeni-KEPCO, Jaks-CPECC, COPI). Coal phase-down by 2050 is the stated policy goal, with plants to convert to biomass/ammonia co-firing. The JETP partnership committed US$15.5 bn to support this transition. Three coal projects totaling 5,300 MW face financing and ownership difficulties, while the Quảng Trị coal plant (1,320 MW) has been officially stopped.
