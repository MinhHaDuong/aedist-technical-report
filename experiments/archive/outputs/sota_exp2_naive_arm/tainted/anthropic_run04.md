## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Mix

As of late 2023, Vietnam's installed capacity breakdown featured coal at 33.2% (26,757 MW), hydropower at 28.4% (22,872 MW), and wind plus solar at 27% (21,664 MW), with gas, oil, and other sources making up the remainder. By 2023, Vietnam's total installed capacity reached approximately 80 GW. As of end-2023, gas-fired power plants represented 9% of Vietnam's installed capacity, against 29% for coal-fired power plants.

In 2021, gas represented 11% of Vietnam's installed capacity (8.3 GW) and 11% of its power production (27 TWh), far behind coal with 30% (23.4 GW) and 48% respectively (120 TWh).

### 1.2 Policy Framework

**PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, May 2023):** On May 15, 2023, the Vietnamese government released Vietnam's Eighth Power Development Plan (PDP8) for the period of 2021-2030, with a vision to 2050. The plan projects nearly doubling the nation's power capacity from 80 GW in 2023 to roughly 150 GW by 2030.

**Thermal capacity targets under PDP8 to 2030:** Domestic gas thermal power capacity: 14,930 MW; LNG thermal power capacity: 22,400 MW; Coal-fired power capacity: 30,127 MW; Cogeneration power sources, power sources using residual heat, blast furnace gas, and by-products: 2,700 MW.

**PDP8 Implementation Plan (Decision 262/QĐ-TTg, April 2024):** The PDP8 Implementation Plan sets out details of domestic gas, LNG and coal power projects to be developed until 2030, including name, planned capacity, location, required year of operation and additional notes on the project status.

**Amended PDP8 (Decision 768, April 2025):** On April 15, 2025, just under two years after unveiling the original PDP8, the prime minister approved updates to the PDP8 (the "Amended PDP8"). Vietnam's power planning primarily aims to provide sufficient electricity for domestic demand, aligning with an average GDP growth rate of about 10 percent per year during 2026 to 2030.

**Coal policy:** Within the PDP8, Vietnam cancels around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline, while keeping a pipeline of new coal projects to around 13 GW. The PDP8 still allows the completion of eleven coal-fired power plants that were already included in the revised PDP7. Vietnam does not have a plan to develop new coal-fired plants after 2030.

**Gas strategy:** Gas-fired thermal power should play a key role in the transition to RE and the retirement of coal. PDP8 puts a focus on power stations fired with domestic gas rather than the expansion of infrastructure serving imported LNG. Vietnamese officials have said that the country plans to build at least 15 LNG-fired power plants by 2035 with combined generation capacity of more than 22 GW.

**JETP:** In December 2022, Vietnam and a coalition of countries led by the EU and UK concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership agreement with US$15.5 billion. The agreement proposed reducing Vietnam's coal fleet capacity from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030.

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- The forecast of power shortage became a reality in Vietnam, as the North of Vietnam faced great challenges in 2023 for power shortage. Power was supplied on alternative schedules for manufacturing, operations and residential areas.
- International LNG markets are volatile and domestic gas supplies uncertain. Projects could face delays or become stranded assets due to a shortage of affordable LNG.
- Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade.
- The Implementation Plan does not provide guidelines to address existing issues and concerns over power project bankability such as identifying a mechanism for selection of investors, feed-in tariffs, or bankable transaction templates.
- Delayed/abandoned coal projects include Cong Thanh, Nam Dinh I, Quang Tri, Vinh Tan III, and Song Hau II.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Power Plants

**Ninh Bình (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Ninh Bình):** Ninh Bình Thermal Power Plant with a capacity of 100 MW was constructed with support from China and has been in operation since 1974. One of Vietnam's oldest surviving coal plants. Multiple 25 MW subcritical units. Now well past design life. Confidence: HIGH (existence and location), MEDIUM (current operating capacity—some units may be decommissioned).

**Uông Bí (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Uông Bí):** In 1961, with the help from the Soviet Union, Vietnam commenced construction of Uong Bi thermal power plant—the main power source for the North. Uong Bi power station has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. The original plant had several small units (12–110 MW class). A 300 MW expansion unit was added circa 2014. Most original units are retired or mothballed. Confidence: HIGH (existence), LOW (exact current operating capacity of old units).

**Phả Lại (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Phả Lại):** Phase 1 consists of 4×110 MW Soviet-design subcritical units, commissioned 1983–1986. Phase 2 added 2×300 MW subcritical units (Japanese technology), commissioned 2001–2002. Located in Hải Dương province. Operated by EVNGENCO1. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. Confidence: HIGH.

**Na Dương 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Na Dương 1):** Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100 MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002 and was completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine. CFB technology. Confidence: HIGH.

**Na Dương 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Na Dương 2):** As of April 2024, the project reportedly had a total capital investment of 4.09 billion VND. In November 2024, Vietcombank Ha Thanh signed a credit contract to finance the Na Duong power plant for 2.63 billion VND. Single ~110 MW CFB unit under construction. Confidence: HIGH.

**Cao Ngạn (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Cao Ngạn):** 2×50 MW CFB units in Thái Nguyên, owned by Vinacomin. Commissioned ~2007. Mine-mouth plant using local anthracite. Confidence: HIGH.

**Sơn Động (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Sơn Động):** Single 220 MW CFB unit in Bắc Giang province, operated by Vinacomin. COD circa 2014. Confidence: MEDIUM (exact COD).

**Cẩm Phả 1 & 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Cẩm Phả):** Located in Quảng Ninh. Phase 1: 2×150 MW subcritical (COD ~2010). Phase 2: 2×220 MW CFB (COD ~2015). Operated by Vinacomin. Confidence: MEDIUM (exact phase breakdown and dates).
