## 1 — Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Mix

Vietnam's total installed capacity exceeded 88,400 MW as of August 2025. As of late 2023, the capacity breakdown featured coal at 33.2% (26,757 MW), hydropower at 28.4% (22,872 MW), and wind plus solar at 27% (21,664 MW), with gas, oil, and other sources making up the remainder. Coal-fired power generation accounted for about 48.7% of Vietnam's total production of electricity through October 2024. As of 2025, gas-fired plants totalled approximately 10 GW of installed capacity (including ~8.3 GW domestic gas and ~1.6 GW LNG as of September 2025). Vietnam's electricity output in 2024, including imports, grew 9.9% year-on-year to 308.73 billion kWh.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

On May 15, 2023, the Vietnamese government released Vietnam's Eighth Power Development Plan (PDP8) for the period 2021–2030, with a vision to 2050. On April 15, 2025, the Prime Minister approved updates to the PDP8 (the "Amended PDP8"), which significantly increases targets for the country's power capacity and further emphasizes renewable energy's role in the overall energy mix.

Key thermal targets under PDP8 / Amended PDP8:

| Fuel | Capacity by 2030 | Source |
|------|------------------|--------|
| Coal | ~30,127–31,055 MW (cap) | PDP8/Amended PDP8 |
| Domestic gas | 10,861–14,930 MW | PDP8/Amended PDP8 |
| LNG | 22,400–22,524 MW | PDP8/Amended PDP8 |

Vietnam does not plan any new coal-fired power plants by 2030; the installed capacity of coal-fired power plants up to 2030 under PDP8 only includes projects that are in the development stage. Under PDP8, it is intended that by 2050, coal-fired power plants will no longer be used for power generation; the production of thermal energy using biomass and ammonia is considered as the alternative.

To support LNG power development, Vietnam has reduced import duties on LNG from 5% to 2%. The country will guarantee an offtake of at least 65% of the annual electricity output from projects using imported LNG for up to 10 years.

### 1.3 Key Institutional Actors

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Ministry of Industry & Trade (MOIT)** | Power-sector regulator; responsible for PDP implementation |
| **Vietnam Electricity (EVN)** | State-owned vertically-integrated utility; owns most thermal capacity through subsidiaries EVNGENCO1, EVNGENCO2, EVNGENCO3 |
| **PetroVietnam (PVN)** / PV Power | State oil-gas conglomerate; operates gas-fired plants (Ca Mau, Nhon Trach, Vung Ang 1) and gas supply |
| **Vinacomin (TKV)** | State coal-mining corporation; owns some coal plants (Mong Duong 1, Na Duong, Cao Ngan, etc.) |
| **Dong Bac Corporation** | State coal miner; operator of some coal plants |
| **BOT investors** | JERA/Mitsubishi, AES, KEPCO, Marubeni, Sembcorp, Teknik Janakuasa, CSEC, etc. |

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **Coal transition dilemma:** The two coal-fired power plants set to cease operation by 2030 are the 440 MW Pha Lai in Hai Duong and 100 MW Ninh Binh, if they cannot update technology nor improve emissions. Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years within this decade.
- **LNG infrastructure gap:** Domestic natural gas projects are currently behind schedule, which may result in delay of domestic gas-to-power projects; to date there are no precedent LNG-to-power projects in Vietnam. (This was written in 2023; Vietnam's first LNG power plants, Nhon Trach 3 and 4 in Dong Nai, are expected to begin commercial operations in 2025.)
- **BOT tax exposure:** In September 2025, Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting the foreign owners of seven coal power plants from the requirement to pay a 15% global minimum corporate tax rate set by the OECD.
- **Demand growth:** Power demand was forecast to increase in 2025 by 11% to 14% above 2024 levels.

## 2 — Per-Plant Narratives (Selected Major Plants)

### COAL — Operating

**Pha Lai 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Pha Lại 1)** — Hai Duong, 440 MW (4 × 110 MW). Pha Lai TPP 1 is the oldest of the thermal power plants, operated for approximately 40 years, with a capacity of 440 MW. Soviet-built units commissioned 1983–1986 using subcritical technology. Set to cease operation by 2030 if it cannot update technology nor improve emissions. Owner: EVNGENCO2.

**Pha Lai 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Pha Lại 2)** — Hai Duong, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Commissioned 2001–2002, subcritical. By 2045, targeted to be replaced by 600 MW of clean energy. Owner: Pha Lai Thermal Power JSC (EVNGENCO2 subsidiary).

**Ninh Binh (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Ninh Bình)** — Ninh Binh, 100 MW. Old Soviet-era plant, one of Vietnam's earliest. The World Bank presented initial research results for repurposing of Ninh Binh coal-fired power plant. Targeted for closure by 2030.

**Uong Bi (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Uông Bí)** — Quang Ninh, ~600 MW (multiple units expanded over decades). By 2050, 600 MW Uong Bi is targeted for replacement by clean energy. Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Na Duong (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Na Dương)** — Lang Son, 110 MW (2 × 55 MW). Subcritical, uses local lignite. Commissioned ~2004. By 2045, targeted for replacement. Owner: Vinacomin.

**Cao Ngan (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Cao Ngạn)** — Thai Nguyen, 115 MW (2 × 57.5 MW). Commissioned ~2006. UNDP explored transition pathways for Cao Ngan. Owner: Vinacomin.

**Son Dong (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Sơn Động)** — Bac Giang, 220 MW. Subcritical. By 2050, targeted for replacement by clean energy. Owner: Dong Bac Corporation.

**Cam Pha 1 & 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Cẩm Phả)** — Quang Ninh, 600 MW (4 × 150 MW total for Cam Pha 1 & 2). Subcritical; uses local anthracite. By 2050, 600 MW Cam Pha 1&2 targeted for replacement. Owner: Vinacomin.

**Quang Ninh 1 & 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Quảng Ninh)** — Quang Ninh, 1,200 MW (4 × 300 MW). In 2006, Quang Ninh Thermal Power JSC broke ground on the two-unit, $600 million, 600-MW Quảng Ninh-1 coal-fired power plant near Hạ Long city. When built, Quang Ninh power station was the largest coal plant permitted in Vietnam. Phases 1 and 2 commissioned ~2010–2014. Chinese-financed. By 2050, 1,200 MW Quang Ninh targeted for replacement. Owner: Quang Ninh Thermal Power JSC (EVN majority).

**Mong Duong 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Mông Dương 1)** — Quang Ninh, 1,080 MW (2 × 540 MW). Subcritical. Commissioned 2015. Owner: EVNGENCO1/Vinacomin.

**Mong Duong 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện BOT Mông Dương 2)** — Quang Ninh, 1,240 MW (2 × 620 MW). Mong Duong power station is an operating power station of at least 2,320 MW in Mong Duong, Cam Pha, Quang Ninh. (Combined with Mong Duong 1.) Mong Duong 2 was an AES BOT project. In 2015, AES commissioned the 1,240 MW Mong Duong 2 coal-fired power plant in Quang Ninh province. Subcritical.

**Hai Phong 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Hải Phòng 1)** — Hai Phong, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Commissioned 2010–2011. By 2050, 600 MW Hai Phong 1 targeted for replacement by clean energy. Owner: Hai Phong Thermal Power JSC (EVN).

**Hai Phong 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Hải Phòng 2)** — Hai Phong, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Commissioned 2013–2014. Owner: Hai Phong Thermal Power JSC.

**Thai Binh 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Thái Bình 1)** — Thai Binh, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Commissioned 2018. Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Thai Binh 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Thái Bình 2)** — Thai Binh, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Long-delayed project; commissioned ~2022–2023 after significant cost overruns. Owner: PetroVietnam.

**Nghi Son 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Nghi Sơn 1)** — Thanh Hoa, 600 MW (2 × 300 MW). Commissioned ~2013–2014. Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Nghi Son 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện BOT Nghi Sơn 2)** — Thanh Hoa, ~1,330 MW (2 × 665 MW). Nghi Son coal power station is an operating power station of at least 1,920 MW in Nghi Son Economic Zone, Thanh Hoa. (Combined Nghi Son 1+2 total.) BOT by Marubeni/KEPCO. Supercritical technology. COD ~2022–2023.

**Vung Ang 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Vũng Áng 1)** — Ha Tinh, 1,200 MW (2 × 600 MW). Vung Ang 1 plant in Ha Tinh Province, with a capacity of 1,200 MW, owned by PetroVietnam Power Corporation, commissioned in December 2014. Subcritical.

**Formosa Ha Tinh (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Formosa Hà Tĩnh)** — Ha Tinh, 650 MW. Operated by Hung Nghiep Formosa Ha Tinh Steel Corporation as a captive facility for the steel complex, capacity of 650 MW, entered operation in 2015.

**Vinh Tan 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện BOT Vĩnh Tân 1)** — Binh Thuan, 1,200 MW (2 × 600 MW). BOT by CSEC (China). Commissioned ~2018–2019.

**Vinh Tan 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Vĩnh Tân 2)** — Binh Thuan, 1,244 MW (2 × 622 MW). The Vinh Tan Coal Fired Power Plant II has been operating since 2014. The 1,244 MW thermal project is located in Binh Thuan. Owner: EVNGENCO3 has equity stakes in the project.

**Vinh Tan 4 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Vĩnh Tân 4)** — Binh Thuan, 1,200 MW (2 × 600 MW). The 1,800 MW Vinh Tan Coal Fired Power Plant IV thermal power project is located in Binh Thuan; it was commissioned in 2017. (Note: 1,800 MW figure includes extension unit.) Owner: EVN.

**Vinh Tan 4 Extension (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Vĩnh Tân 4 mở rộng)** — Binh Thuan, 600 MW (1 × 600 MW). Commissioned ~2019–2020. Vinh Tan power station is an operating power station of at least 4,244 MW in Vinh Tan, Tuy Phong, Binh Thuan, with multiple units. (Combined Vinh Tan 1+2+4+ext total.)

**Duyen Hai 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Duyên Hải 1)** — Tra Vinh, 1,244 MW (2 × 622 MW). Duyen Hai Power Plant I & III, a 2,488-MW coal-fired facility in Tra Vinh, was commissioned in 2016. (Combined I + III.) Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Duyen Hai 3 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Duyên Hải 3)** — Tra Vinh, 1,244 MW (2 × 622 MW). Commissioned 2016–2017. Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Duyen Hai 3 Extension (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Duyên Hải 3 mở rộng)** — Tra Vinh, 688 MW (1 × 688 MW). Commissioned ~2019. Supercritical. Owner: EVNGENCO1.

**Duyen Hai 2 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện BOT Duyên Hải 2)** — Tra Vinh, 1,320 MW (2 × 660 MW). Duyen Hai Power Plant II is a 1,320-MW coal-fired facility that came online in 2021, located in Tra Vinh. BOT by Janakuasa (Malaysia).

**Song Hau 1 (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Sông Hậu 1)** — Hau Giang, 1,200 MW (2 × 600 MW). Long-delayed; commissioned ~2020–2022. Owner: PetroVietnam.

**Hai Duong (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện BOT Hải Dương)** — Hai Duong, 1,200 MW (2 × 600 MW). BOT by Jaks Resources (Malaysia) / China Energy Engineering Corp. Commissioned ~2020–2021. Subcritical.

### LNG — Under Construction / Commissioning

**Quynh Lap LNG (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện LNG Quỳnh Lập)** — Nghe An, 1,500 MW. The Quynh Lap LNG project will consist of a 1,500 MW gas CCGT plant and a 250,000 m³ LNG terminal. Construction of the plant will begin in 2027. Run by a consortium of SK Group, PV Power, and NASU.

**Song Doc LNG (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện LNG Sông Đốc)** — Ca Mau, 3,000 MW. The Song Doc LNG Thermal Power Plant is a 3,000 MW gas-fired power project planned in Ca Mau. It is a CCGT power plant.

**Quang Trach II LNG** — Quang Binh, ~1,500 MW. Converting the Quang Trach II project to LNG before 2030. (Originally planned as coal.)
