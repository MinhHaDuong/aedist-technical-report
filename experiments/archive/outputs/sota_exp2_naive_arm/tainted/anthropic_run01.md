# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Assets: Reference Inventory

*Compiled May 2026. All values carry an implicit as-of date; see "Status as-of-date" column and Notes for specifics.*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Mix (as of end-2024)

As of 2024, according to EVN, Vietnam's total installed power capacity reached 82,387 MW: coal-fired power 26,757 MW (32.5%), gas and oil-fired power 8,653 MW (10.5%), hydropower 23,664 MW (28.7%), renewable energy (wind and solar) 21,447 MW (26%), and imports and other sources 1,866 MW (2.3%).

By the end of 2025, the total installed capacity of the national power system reached about 87,600 MW, an increase of nearly 6,400 MW compared to 2024.

In terms of generation output, coal combustion accounted for around 50% of total electricity generation, with another 7% coming from gas-fired units in 2024. In Q1 2025, coal-fired power accounted for 56.5% of generation, gas turbines 6.4%, hydropower 19.1%, renewable energy 16.0%, and imported electricity 1.8%.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

Vietnam's thermal power sector is governed by a cascading hierarchy of planning instruments:

- **PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 15 May 2023):** The National Power Development Master Plan set out thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 including domestic gas (14,930 MW), LNG (22,400 MW), and coal (30,127 MW).

- **PDP8 Implementation Plan (Decision 262/QĐ-TTg, 1 April 2024):** Set out details of domestic gas, LNG and coal power projects to be developed until 2030, including name, planned capacity, location, required year of operation and project status.

- **Revised PDP8 (Decision 768/QĐ-TTg, 15 April 2025):** Issued on 15 April 2025 and supersedes Decision 500. It reflects Vietnam's commitment to energy security, environmental responsibility, and net-zero emissions by 2050. Both plans restrict coal to projects under construction, capping 2030 capacity at ~31 GW. The 2025 plan sets a firmer roadmap: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050.

- **Revised PDP8 Implementation Plan (Decision 1509/QĐ-BCT, 30 May 2025):** Issued by the Ministry of Industry and Trade as a prompt action to operationalize Decision 768.

- **JETP (December 2022):** Vietnam concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership agreement with US$15.5 billion to cap and phase out unabated coal generation, reducing the coal fleet from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030.

- **Electricity Law 2024:** With its adoption, the Amended PDP8 assigns authorities to complete the development of a pricing scheme based on market principles.

### 1.3 Key Institutional Actors

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **MOIT** (Ministry of Industry and Trade) | Policy, planning, sector oversight; responsible for PDP implementation |
| **EVN** (Vietnam Electricity) | State utility; sole transmission operator; power offtaker; owns generation subsidiaries (GENCO 1, GENCO 2, GENCO 3) |
| **PVN** (PetroVietnam) | National oil & gas corporation; upstream gas; owns PV Power (generation) and PV Gas (midstream) |
| **TKV/Vinacomin** | National coal-mineral corporation; owns mine-mouth coal plants |
| **BOT developers** | Foreign-invested build-operate-transfer projects (Mitsubishi, KEPCO, AES, Sumitomo, Marubeni, JAKS, etc.) |

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **Gas turbine global shortages:** Turbine bottlenecks add to regulatory and bankability challenges. Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for domestic gas and LNG-fired power capacity by a combined 25.2 GW.

- **LNG infrastructure gaps:** Vietnam currently has only one operational LNG terminal, at Thi Vai, with a capacity of 1 million metric tons per year. As of late 2025, delays in building infrastructure and global shortages of gas turbines had led to significant delays.

- **PPA and pricing framework:** There is still no specific LNG pricing framework for power projects. The government has still not given EVN approval to sign power purchase agreements for LNG-based electricity generation.

- **Coal phase-out tension:** Vietnam's oldest station (Uông Bí) has passed 40 years. The Phả Lại complex started in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years within this decade. It remains unclear whether these will be decommissioned under the 40-year rule.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives (Selected Key Plants)

### Coal — Operating

**Uông Bí (Uong Bi)** — Quảng Ninh. Vietnam's oldest thermal plant; original Soviet-era units from the 1960s–70s. Expanded over decades; current effective capacity includes subcritical units. It has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. Owner: EVN/GENCO 1. Confidence: MEDIUM (total capacity figures vary by source and unit retirement status).

**Phả Lại (Pha Lai)** — Hải Dương. Two-phase complex: Phase 1 (4×110 MW subcritical, Soviet-designed, 1983–1986) and Phase 2 (2×300 MW subcritical, Japanese-built, 2001–2002). Units started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. Owner: Pha Lai Thermal Power JSC (PPC), an EVN subsidiary. Confidence: HIGH.

**Na Dương 1 (Na Duong 1)** — Lạng Sơn. A two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002 and was completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine. Confidence: HIGH.

**Vĩnh Tân Complex (Vinh Tan)** — Bình Thuận. Multi-plant power center (Vinh Tan 1, 2, 4, 4 Extension) totaling ~4,244 MW. In March 2019, Vietnam's State Audit Agency found Vinh Tan 1 had violated environmental laws, including discharging cooling water without approval and nitrogen oxide emissions in excess of limits. Voter feedback reported "hundreds of households" impacted by pollution. Confidence: HIGH for existence and approximate capacity; MEDIUM for exact unit ratings.

**Vũng Áng 1 (Vung Ang 1)** — Hà Tĩnh. Vung Ang power station is an operating power station of at least 1,860 MW. Phase 1 (2×600 MW) operated by PV Power/EVN since 2014–2015. Confidence: HIGH.

**Duyên Hải Complex (Duyen Hai)** — Trà Vinh. Major power center with multiple phases: Duyen Hai 1 (1,244 MW), Duyen Hai 3 (1,244 MW), Duyen Hai 3 Extension (688 MW), and Duyen Hai 2 (BOT). Duyên Hải-2 is among BOT coal plants exempt from global minimum tax considerations. Confidence: HIGH for existence; MEDIUM for Duyen Hai 2 capacity.

**Sông Hậu 1 (Song Hau 1)** — Hậu Giang. An operating power station of at least 1,200 MW. Developed by PVN, this subcritical plant experienced significant construction delays before commissioning in 2020–2022. Confidence: HIGH.

**Mông Dương 2 (Mong Duong 2)** — Quảng Ninh. 2×600 MW BOT project developed by AES Corporation and partners. Listed among BOT plants considered for global minimum tax exemption. Confidence: HIGH.

**Nghi Sơn 2 (Nghi Son 2)** — Thanh Hóa. Developed by Marubeni with Japanese and Vietnamese partners, capacity of 1,200 MW, supplying significant power to the national grid. BOT project commissioned 2022–2023. Confidence: HIGH.

### Coal — Under Construction

**Quảng Trạch 1 (Quang Trach 1)** — Quảng Bình. Operated by EVN, under construction with a capacity of 1,200 MW, part of the larger Quang Trach power complex. Confidence: HIGH.

**Vũng Áng 2 (Vung Ang 2)** — Hà Tĩnh. Located in Ha Tinh province, being developed by a consortium including Mitsubishi Corporation and KEPCO, with a planned capacity of 1,200 MW. KEPCO invested US$189 million for a 40% stake, alongside Mitsubishi's Diamond Generating Asia (40%) and Chugoku Electric Power (20%). Confidence: HIGH.

**Vân Phong 1 (Van Phong 1)** — Khánh Hòa. Developed by Sumitomo Corporation, capacity of 1,320 MW, expected to begin operations in 2025. BOT project; recently completed commissioning. Confidence: HIGH.

**Na Dương 2 (Na Duong 2)** — Lạng Sơn. 1×110 MW expansion. As of July 2025, slated to be operational before December 31, 2026. Owner: Vinacomin. Confidence: HIGH.

**Long Phú 1 (Long Phu 1)** — Sóc Trăng. 2×600 MW subcritical plant developed by PVN. Suffered severe delays partly due to US sanctions on Russian equipment supplier (Power Machines/Silovye Mashiny). As of 2025, construction continues with significant schedule overruns. Confidence: MEDIUM (exact current status uncertain).

**Nam Định 1 (Nam Dinh 1)** — Nam Định. A joint venture between Taekwang Power (South Korea) and ACWA Power (Saudi Arabia), capacity of 1,200 MW, expected to begin operations by the late 2020s. Confidence: MEDIUM.

### Gas — Operating (Domestic Gas)

**Phú Mỹ Complex (Phu My)** — Bà Rịa-Vũng Tàu. Vietnam's largest gas power center with multiple CCGT plants: Phu My 1 (~1,090 MW), Phu My 2.1 (~729 MW), Phu My 2.1 Extension (~451 MW, BOT), Phu My 2.2 (~733 MW, BOT), Phu My 3 (~744 MW, BOT), Phu My 4 (~468 MW). Gas power plants in Vietnam are located in the southern region, including 5,705 MW in the southeast requiring 20–22 million cu m/d of gas. Since 2020, gas supply has been declining due to ageing gas fields; average supply stands at just 9.5 million cu m/d, less than half of demand. Confidence: HIGH for existence; MEDIUM for individual unit capacities.

**Cà Mau 1 & 2 (Ca Mau)** — Cà Mau. 1,500 MW of gas capacity in the southwest, with demand of 4–4.6 million cu m/d. Two CCGT plants (~750 MW each) operated by PV Power, commissioned 2008–2009. Use domestic gas from PM3-CAA basin. Confidence: HIGH.

**Nhơn Trạch 1 & 2 (Nhon Trach)** — Đồng Nai. CCGT plants operated by PV Power using domestic gas. Nhon Trach 1 (~450 MW, COD ~2009) and Nhon Trach 2 (~750 MW, COD ~2011). Confidence: HIGH.

### Gas — LNG (New)

**Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Nhon Trach 3 & 4)** — Đồng Nai. Vietnam's first LNG-fueled power plants. Inaugurated on December 14, 2025, with combined investment capital of about USD 1.4 billion and total capacity of 1,624 MW. PV Power serves as the investor, with Lilama and Samsung C&T as EPC contractor. Equipped with GE 9HA.02 gas turbines, achieving efficiency of 62–64%. Nhon Trach 3 connected to the national grid in February 2025; Nhon Trach 4 in June 2025. Confidence: HIGH.

### LNG — Planned

LNG power capacity is expected to reach 22,525 MW by 2030. Under the Plan, Vietnam has five years (until 2030) to develop 15 LNG power plants. Key projects include:

- **LNG Bạc Liêu** (3,200 MW), **LNG Sơn Mỹ I & II** (~4,500 MW total), **LNG Cà Ná** (1,500 MW), **LNG Quỳnh Lập** (1,500 MW), **LNG Nghi Sơn** (1,500 MW), and others.

New LNG projects in the Revised PDP8 include LNG Hải Phòng Phase I (1,600 MW) and LNG Hiệp Phước Phase II (1,500 MW) for 2025–2030. For 2031–2035, LNG Công Thanh (1,500 MW), LNG Hải Phòng Phase II (3,200 MW), and LNG Quảng Trạch III (1,500 MW) are updated.
