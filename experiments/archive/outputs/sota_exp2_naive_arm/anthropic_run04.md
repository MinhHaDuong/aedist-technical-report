## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Electricity Mix

Through 2020, total installed capacity of the power generation industry had reached 69,340 MW. Government data shows that gas-fired generation accounts for just under 10% of Vietnam's installed power generation capacity, while coal-fired power plants account for about 30% of the country's electricity production. As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction.

Under PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 15 May 2023, as amended by Decision 768/QĐ-TTg, 15 April 2025):

The thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas, LNG, and coal with a total capacity of 14,930 MW, 22,400 MW and 30,127 MW respectively. Under the Revised PDP8, the total capacity of power plants serving domestic demand is projected to reach 183,291–236,363 MW by 2030 and 774,503–838,681 MW by 2050.

Vietnam does not plan any new coal-fired power plants by 2030. Under Master Plan VIII, it is intended that by 2050, coal-fired power plants will be no longer used for power generation; the production of thermal energy using biomass and ammonia is considered as the alternative.

The two coal-fired power plants set to cease operation by 2030 are the 440 MW Pha Lai in Hai Duong province and 100 MW Ninh Binh in Ninh Binh province, if they cannot update new technology nor improve the emission.

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **Coal transition timeline**: Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years within this decade.
- **LNG infrastructure gap**: Domestic natural gas projects are currently behind schedule, which may result in the delay of domestic gas-to-power projects; to date there are no precedent LNG-to-power projects in Vietnam (note: Nhon Trach 3&4 inaugurated Dec 2025).
- **Blue Whale delays**: Vietnam has called on Exxon Mobil to accelerate the delayed Blue Whale gas field project. The project, originally slated to begin operations in 2023, has seen minimal progress, with the timeline now pushed to 2028.
- **LNG pricing**: There is still no specific LNG pricing framework for power projects (as of mid-2025, partially addressed by Decree 100/2025).
- **Regional supply imbalance**: The North of Vietnam faced great challenges in 2023 for power shortage. MOIT forecasted that localized power shortages in Ho Chi Minh City are expected by 2030.

By 2050, the target is to develop 3,335 MW of clean energy to replace six plants of 600 MW Uong Bi, 600 MW Hai Phong 1, 1,200 MW Quang Ninh, 115 MW Cao Ngan, 600 MW Cam Pha 1&2, and 220 MW Son Dong. By 2045, the target is to replace Pha Lai 2 (600 MW), Na Duong (110 MW), and Formosa Dong Nai (450 MW).

**LNG cross-check**: According to the adjusted PDP VIII, Vietnam is projected to have 22,524 MW of installed LNG power capacity by 2030. Our LNG pipeline total (commissioning + planned) = 1,624 + 2,700 (Thai Binh LNG + Hiep Phuoc) + 17,700 (Decision 1509 list plants) ≈ 22,024 MW. The small gap (~500 MW) may reflect capacity rounding or reserve projects.
