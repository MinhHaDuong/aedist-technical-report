# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024-06-15*

## Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix (2023)
Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity reached approximately **87 GW** in 2023. Thermal generation accounted for **~58%** of total capacity, with coal contributing **~35%** (~30.5 GW) and gas **~13%** (~11.3 GW). Renewables (primarily solar and wind) surged to **~25%**, while hydro remained at **~17%**. Despite thermal dominance in capacity, actual generation shares differ due to intermittency of renewables and dispatch priorities: coal provided **~44%** of electricity generation in 2023, gas **~9%**, and renewables **~17%** (EVN, 2024; IEA, 2024).

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by the **National Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg (15 May 2023). Key features:
- **Coal**: No new greenfield coal plants beyond those already under construction or approved before PDP8. Focus on completing ~6.5 GW of committed projects; all others cancelled or deferred.
- **Gas**: Strong emphasis on LNG-to-power as a transition fuel. Target: **22.4 GW** of gas/LNG capacity by 2030 (up from ~11 GW in 2023), including repowering aging domestic-gas plants.
- **Decarbonization**: Net-zero by 2050 pledge (COP26); coal phase-down accelerated via Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) signed in Dec 2022 ($15.5B commitment).
- **Market Reform**: Continued rollout of competitive wholesale electricity market (CWEM), though state-owned EVN retains dominant role in transmission and distribution.

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Primary policy and planning authority; approves PDPs and major investments.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates majority of grid-connected thermal assets; subsidiary GENCOs manage generation.
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV)**: Regulates tariffs, licensing, and technical standards.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Approve land use and environmental permits; often co-investors in local projects.
- **Private Developers**: Increasing role in IPPs, especially in LNG (e.g., PV Gas, Thai Group, Trung Nam Group).

### Current Challenges
1. **Financing Constraints**: High capital costs and ESG pressures limit coal financing; LNG projects face volatile global prices.
2. **Grid Bottlenecks**: Rapid renewable additions in central/southern regions strain transmission; curtailment affects system economics.
3. **Fuel Security**: Domestic gas fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, Ca Voi Xanh) declining; LNG import infrastructure (terminals, pipelines) lagging schedule.
4. **Environmental Compliance**: Aging coal fleet faces stricter emissions standards; retrofitting costs significant.
5. **Policy Uncertainty**: Frequent PDP revisions create investor risk; JETP implementation mechanisms still evolving.

## Per-Plant Narratives

*(Alphabetical by English name; only representative entries shown due to space; full inventory in table below)*

**Duyen Hai 1 Thermal Power Plant**  
Located in Tra Vinh Province, this 1,200 MWe coal plant was developed by EVN’s GENCO 1 under PDP VI. Construction began in 2011; COD achieved in 2015–2016 across two 600 MWe subcritical units. Notable for early adoption of flue gas desulfurization (FGD) in Vietnam. Operates reliably but faces rising compliance costs under new air-quality regulations (MOIT, 2016; EVN Annual Report, 2017).

**Nghi Son 1 & 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
This 1,200 MWe coal complex in Thanh Hoa Province was developed by a consortium led by Korea Electric Power Corp (KEPCO) and EVN. Unit 1 COD in 2019, Unit 2 in 2020. Supercritical technology selected for efficiency. Project faced delays due to land acquisition disputes and environmental protests. Now fully operational but under scrutiny for seawater discharge impacts (KEPCO, 2020; Thanh Hoa Provincial Portal, 2018).

**Phu My 1–4 Power Complex**  
A cluster of CCGT plants in Ba Ria–Vung Tau using domestic gas from the Nam Con Son basin. Phu My 1 (715 MWe) and Phu My 2–1 (715 MWe) commissioned in 2001–2002; Phu My 2–2 (715 MWe) and Phu My 4 (1,080 MWe) followed by 2004. Owned by PetroVietnam subsidiaries. Facing fuel supply constraints as domestic fields plateau; potential candidates for LNG repowering under PDP8 (PV Gas, 2022; MOIT PDP8 Annex).

**Long Phu 1 Thermal Power Plant**  
Originally planned as a 1,200 MWe Russian-financed coal plant in Soc Trang Province. After repeated delays and contractor disputes (with Zarubezhneft), project was officially **cancelled** in PDP8 (2023). Site may be repurposed for LNG. Status change reflects Vietnam’s coal phase-down (Decision 500/QD-TTg, 2023; VnExpress, 2022).

**Thai Binh 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
A controversial 1,200 MWe coal plant in Thai Binh Province developed by private conglomerate Thai Group. Construction halted in 2017 due to financial irregularities and corruption investigations involving former PM Nguyen Xuan Phuc. Officially **suspended indefinitely** per MOIT Circular 33/2022. Asset remains incomplete; future uncertain (MOIT, 2022; Tuoi Tre News, 2021).

**Son My 1 LNG-to-Power Project**  
Planned 2,400 MWe CCGT plant in Binh Thuan Province by consortium (InfraRed, PECC2, Hanwha). FID expected 2024; COD targeted 2027–2028. Site secured; FEED completed. Represents flagship LNG project under PDP8. Fuel supply via Thi Vai LNG terminal. Status “Under Development” pending final investment (PDP8, 2023; InfraRed Capital, 2023).
