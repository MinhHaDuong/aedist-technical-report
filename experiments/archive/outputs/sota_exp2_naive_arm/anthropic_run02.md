## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Mix

Vietnam's electricity mix as of 2025 includes approximately 48% coal, 34% hydropower, and 8% solar. In 2024, total electricity generation reached 308 TWh, with coal contributing the largest share at 148 TWh (48%). Hydropower followed at 98 TWh (32%), solar and wind together produced 39 TWh (13%), and gas-fired power accounted for 23.4 TWh (7%).

While historically hydroelectric was the dominant source, coal has been the key power generation source since 2018. Vietnam has become the world's fifth-largest coal importer in 2025 and the leading coal importer in Southeast Asia.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

On May 15, 2023, the Vietnamese government released the Eighth Power Development Plan (PDP8) for the period 2021–2030, with a vision to 2050. On April 15, 2025, the Prime Minister approved updates to the PDP8 (the "Amended PDP8").

Key thermal-sector provisions of PDP8 and its 2025 amendment:

- **Coal**: Both plans restrict coal to projects under construction, capping 2030 capacity at ~31 GW. The 2025 plan sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050.
- **Domestic gas**: Thermal sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas with a total capacity of 14,930 MW. The focus is on developing the Block B and Ca Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) gas-electricity chains, with a total capacity of 6,900 MW.
- **LNG**: The 2025 PDP8 includes an increase from 0.8 GW to 22.5 GW of imported LNG capacity by 2030. The revised PDP8 clears the way for natural gas to replace coal as the country's primary source of baseload power by 2030.

### 1.3 Key Institutional Actors

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)** | Regulatory authority, power planning, tariff policy |
| **Vietnam Electricity (EVN)** | National utility; owns EVNGENCO 1, 2, 3 generation subsidiaries; sole grid offtaker |
| **PetroVietnam (PVN)** | State-owned entity controlling fossil fuel infrastructure; parent of PV Power (POW), PV Gas |
| **Vietnam Coal & Mineral Industries Group (Vinacomin / TKV)** | Coal mining and mine-mouth power generation |
| **BOT Developers** | Foreign investors (Mitsubishi, KEPCO, Marubeni, Sumitomo, AES/OneEnergy, Sembcorp, CGNPC, Teknik Janakuasa, Jaks Resources, etc.) |

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **LNG delays**: As of 2025, Vietnam has only completed two LNG power plants — Nhon Trach 3 and 4 — out of 16 projects outlined in PDP8. Delays in building infrastructure and global shortages of gas turbines have led to significant delays in installed capacity for LNG-fired generation.
- **Gas supply decline**: Gas plants in the southeast require 20–22 million cu m/d but average supply stands at just 9.5 million cu m/d due to ageing gas fields.
- **PPA framework**: The government has still not given EVN approval to sign power purchase agreements for LNG-based electricity generation (as of late 2024).
- **Coal import reliance**: Vietnam imported 42 million tonnes of thermal coal in 2024, a 27% y-o-y increase.
- **Ageing fleet**: Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed 40 years. The Pha Lai Power Complex started in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Power Plants

#### A. Operating Plants

**Uông Bí (Uong Bi) — Quảng Ninh**
Vietnam's oldest coal-fired power station, originally built with Soviet assistance in the 1960s. It has passed a lifespan of 40 years. The original small units (12–50 MW) are largely retired. An expansion phase (2 × 150 MW) was commissioned around 2009–2010 using subcritical technology. Owner: EVNGENCO 1 (formerly). Total operating capacity approximately 300 MW (expansion only). Confidence: MEDIUM — unit-level data varies across sources.

**Phả Lại 1 (Pha Lai 1) — Hải Dương**
Soviet-built plant with 4 × 110 MW units commissioned during 1983–1986, totaling 440 MW. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. Uses domestic anthracite coal. Owner: Pha Lai Thermal Power JSC (under EVNGENCO 2). Subcritical pulverized coal. Confidence: HIGH.

**Phả Lại 2 (Pha Lai 2) — Hải Dương**
Expansion of the Pha Lai complex, 2 × 300 MW (600 MW total), commissioned 2001–2002. Subcritical. Owner: Pha Lai Thermal Power JSC. Confidence: HIGH.
