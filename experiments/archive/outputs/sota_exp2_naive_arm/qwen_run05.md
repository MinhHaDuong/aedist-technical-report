# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*Compiled as of 2024-06-15*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix (2023)
In 2023, Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity reached approximately **85 GW**, with thermal generation accounting for **~58%** (≈49 GW). Coal-fired power contributed **~21 GW** (25% of total capacity), while gas-fired (domestic and LNG) provided **~8 GW** (9%). Hydropower remained significant at **~22 GW** (26%), and solar/wind surged to **~24 GW** (28%) following feed-in tariff incentives (EVN, 2024; IEA, 2023). However, due to intermittency, thermal sources supplied **~65% of actual electricity generation** in 2023.

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by **Power Development Plan VIII (PDP VIII)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg on May 15, 2023. Key features:
- **Coal phase-down**: No new coal plants beyond those already under construction or with investment approval as of 2023; existing projects may proceed but face stricter environmental reviews.
- **Gas/LNG expansion**: Target of **22–24 GW** of gas/LNG capacity by 2030, including conversion of aging oil/coal plants to LNG.
- **Renewables prioritization**: Solar and wind to reach **40–50 GW** by 2030.
- **Emissions constraints**: Net-zero pledge by 2050 (COP26); coal plants must comply with tighter air emission standards (Circular 22/2022/TT-BTNMT).

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Leads energy policy, approves PDPs, oversees licensing.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates majority of transmission and many thermal plants; sole off-taker for IPPs.
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERAV)**: Regulates tariffs, grid access, technical standards.
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Issue land-use and environmental permits.
- **Project Developers**: Include state entities (PVN, Vinacomin), private firms (BIM Energy, Trung Nam, Thai Group), and foreign investors (Marubeni, Mitsubishi, GS E&C).

### Current Challenges
- **Grid congestion**: Rapid renewable deployment in Central/South regions exceeds transmission capacity, leading to curtailment (~1.7 TWh in 2023).
- **Fuel supply risks**: Domestic gas fields (e.g., Nam Con Son, Ca Voi Xanh) face depletion; LNG import infrastructure (e.g., Thi Vai, Nhon Hoi terminals) lags behind demand.
- **Financing constraints**: High capital costs and perceived ESG risks deter international lenders from coal projects; LNG projects struggle with long-term offtake agreements.
- **Environmental compliance**: Older coal plants (subcritical) face retrofitting costs to meet new emission limits; public opposition intensifies near urban centers.
- **Policy uncertainty**: Delays in PDP VIII implementation and lack of clear market mechanisms (e.g., capacity payments) hinder investor confidence.

## 2. Per-Plant Narratives

*(Selected illustrative examples; full list in table)*

**Pha Lai 1 & 2 (Hai Duong)**  
Originally commissioned in 1985 (Unit 1) and 1994 (Unit 2), these subcritical coal units were retrofitted in the 2010s under a JICA-funded modernization program. Operated by EVN’s PV Power, they remain critical baseload assets despite age. Unit 1 was temporarily idled in 2022 for boiler repairs but returned to service in 2023. Environmental monitoring shows SO₂ emissions near regulatory limits (MOIT, 2022).

**Vinh Tan 4 (Binh Thuan)**  
Developed by China Southern Grid (CSG) and EVN, this 1,200 MWe supercritical plant began commercial operation in 2017 after delays due to ash pond protests. It uses imported Indonesian coal. A planned second unit (Vinh Tan 4 Extension) was canceled in PDP VIII. The plant has faced scrutiny over seawater intake impacts on local fisheries (MONRE Inspection Report No. 112/QD-BTNMT, 2019).

**Phu My 1–4 Complex (Ba Ria–Vung Tau)**  
Vietnam’s largest gas-fired hub, fueled by domestic Nam Con Son basin gas. Phu My 1 (715 MWe CCGT) and Phu My 2–2 (715 MWe) are operated by EVN; Phu My 3 (720 MWe) and Phu My 4 (720 MWe) by PV Gas Power. All units entered service between 2001–2004. Declining gas pressure has reduced output since 2020; conversion studies to LNG co-firing are underway (PVN, 2023).

**Thai Binh 2 (Thai Binh)**  
A stalled 1,200 MWe supercritical coal project developed by state-owned PetroVietnam Power (PV Power). Construction began in 2015 but halted in 2019 due to funding gaps and contractor disputes. MOIT confirmed in 2023 that the project would not proceed under PDP VIII (Decision 500/QD-TTg). Status downgraded to “Cancelled.”

**Long Phu 1 (Soc Trang)**  
A 1,200 MWe supercritical coal plant developed by Russian consortium (Zarubezhneft–Atomstroyexport). Construction started in 2017 but paused in 2020 amid financing issues and Russia’s geopolitical isolation. As of 2024, no restart date is confirmed; widely regarded as de facto canceled though not formally withdrawn (MOIT Workshop Minutes, March 2024).

**Nghi Son 1 & 2 (Thanh Hoa)**  
Operational since 2015–2017, these 1,200 MWe subcritical units use imported coal and are majority-owned by Japan’s Marubeni and Sumitomo. Notable for early adoption of flue gas desulfurization (FGD). Faced community protests over dust emissions in 2018; subsequent upgrades improved compliance (Nghi Son Power Co., Sustainability Report 2022).

**Duyen Hai 3 (Tra Vinh)**  
A 1,200 MWe supercritical plant commissioned in 2017, developed by EVN and Japanese partners (Mitsubishi, Sojitz). Uses imported coal. Achieved >85% capacity factor in 2023. Planned Duyen Hai 3 Extension (1,200 MWe) was removed from PDP VIII.

**Ca Mau 1 & 2 (Ca Mau)**  
Southernmost gas-fired complex (2 × 720 MWe CCGT), fueled by PM3 gas field via pipeline. Commissioned in 2008–2009. Output constrained since 2021 due to declining PM3 reserves; exploring LNG backup (PV Gas, 2023 Annual Report).
