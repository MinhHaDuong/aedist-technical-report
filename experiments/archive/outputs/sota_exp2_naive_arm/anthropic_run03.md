# Vietnam Thermal Generation Asset Inventory (> 30 MWe)

*Compiled: May 2026 | Best-effort as-of date for most status fields: Q1 2026*

---

## 1 — Sector Overview

### 1.1 Electricity Generation Mix

In 2024, Vietnam's total electricity generation reached 308 TWh, with coal-fired power contributing the largest share at 148 TWh—48% of the total. Hydropower followed at 98 TWh (32%), although its output was constrained by drought. Solar and wind generation together produced 39 TWh (13%), reflecting steady growth despite ongoing grid bottlenecks. Gas-fired power accounted for 23.4 TWh, or 7% of total generation.

On the installed-capacity side, coal-fired power plants remain dominant, accounting for approximately 43% of total installed capacity. Hydropower is the next largest contributor, and the share of non-hydro renewables — including solar and wind — has risen to 21%, signaling momentum in the clean energy transition.

Coal accounted for a record 64.6% of Vietnam's electricity in April 2024, and even though Vietnam has pledged not to develop new coal-fired plants after 2030, its current fleet is the youngest in Asia.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

**PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 15 May 2023):** Vietnam cancelled around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline while keeping a pipeline of new coal projects to around 13 GW — allowing the completion of eleven coal-fired power plants already in PDP7 Revised. The PDP8 allows only ongoing coal-fired thermal projects currently under construction and under investment promoted for operation during the 2021–2025 period; no additional new coal-fired thermal power is to be developed.

The thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas, LNG, and coal with a total capacity of 14,930 MW, 22,400 MW and 30,127 MW respectively.

**Amended PDP8 (Decision 768/QĐ-TTg, 15 April 2025):** Deputy Prime Minister Bùi Thanh Sơn signed Decision No. 768/QĐ-TTg, officially approving the revised version of PDP8. The updated plan projects 22,524 MW of LNG capacity by 2030, while domestic natural gas power is expected to remain stable at 10,861–14,930 MW. Coal-fired power capacity will be capped at 31,055 MW through 2030, with no new coal projects to be approved thereafter. The Amended PDP8 also sets out clear goals for nuclear power: between 2030 and 2035, the Ninh Thuan 1 & 2 Nuclear Power Plants will be put into operation with a capacity of 4,000–6,400 MW.

**Implementation Plan (Decision 262/QĐ-TTg):** The Implementation Plan sets out in Annex III details of domestic gas, LNG and coal power projects to be developed until 2030, including name, planned capacity, location, required year of operation, and additional notes on the project status.

**JETP Agreement (December 2022):** Vietnam and a coalition of countries led by the EU and the UK concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership agreement with US$15.5 billion. The agreement proposed reducing Vietnam's coal fleet capacity from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030. As of July 2025, three clean-energy investment projects have officially penned financing agreements under the JETP.

**BOT Tax Exemptions (September 2025):** Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting the foreign owners of seven coal power plants from the requirement to pay a 15% global minimum corporate tax rate set by the OECD. The seven BOT coal plants were Mông Dương-2, Vĩnh Tân-1, Nghi Sơn-2, Vân Phong-1, Vũng Áng-2, Hai Duong, and Duyên Hải-2.

### 1.4 Current Challenges

- **Coal import dependency:** Vietnam imported 42 million tonnes of thermal coal in 2024, a 27% year-on-year increase, primarily from Indonesia. Vietnam ranked among the world's top five coal importers in 2024.
- **LNG infrastructure delays:** As of late 2025, delays in building infrastructure and global shortages of gas turbines had led to significant delays in installed capacity for gas, likely delaying 2030 LNG-fired gas plant activation goals.
- **Coal fleet age divergence:** Vietnam's oldest power station (Uông Bí) has passed a lifespan of 40 years. The power units of the Phả Lại Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade.
- **Grid integration:** Vietnam has struck challenges with further expansion, including a grid that is unprepared for adding large-scale wind and solar projects.

## 2 — Per-Plant Narratives

> **Scope note.** The narratives below cover all identified thermal (coal and gas) plants > 30 MWe. They are organized by fuel, then by geography (north → south). For brevity, well-sourced plants receive concise narratives; poorly-sourced or minor assets receive stub entries with confidence flags.

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Plants

#### 2.1.1 Ninh Bình Thermal Power Plant (Nhiệt điện Ninh Bình)
**Province:** Ninh Bình | **Units:** 4 × 25 MW = 100 MW | **Status:** Operating (units aging)
One of Vietnam's oldest coal plants (COD 1974), using subcritical pulverized-coal boilers. Individual units are 25 MW each—below the 30 MW unit threshold but the plant total qualifies. Owned by EVNGENCO 1. Uses domestic anthracite. Candidate for early retirement under JETP frameworks. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — total capacity widely reported; individual unit configuration from secondary sources.

#### 2.1.2 Uông Bí Thermal Power Plant (Nhiệt điện Uông Bí)
**Province:** Quảng Ninh | **Status:** Operating
Vietnam's oldest power station, with a lifespan exceeding 40 years. The original units date from the 1960s–1970s (Soviet-era). An expansion unit of ~300 MW (subcritical) was commissioned around 2014–2015, owned by TKV. Older units may be partially decommissioned. Total operating capacity estimated at ~300–450 MW depending on retirement status of legacy units. **Confidence: LOW** — legacy unit status uncertain; expansion COD approximate.

#### 2.1.3 Phả Lại 1 (Nhiệt điện Phả Lại 1)
**Province:** Hải Dương | **Units:** 4 × 110 MW = 440 MW | **Status:** Operating | **COD:** 1983–1986
Phả Lại 1 is managed by Pha Lai Thermal Power Joint Stock Company with a total of 4 units of 110 MW each and a total of 440 MW, and was put into operation in 1986. Soviet-built subcritical units burning domestic anthracite from Quảng Ninh. The Phả Lại Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. **Confidence: HIGH.**
