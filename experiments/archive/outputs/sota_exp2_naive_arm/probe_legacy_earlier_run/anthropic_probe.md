## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Evolution of the National Electricity Generation Mix

Vietnam has experienced remarkable electricity demand growth, closely tracking its sustained GDP expansion of 6–7% annually over recent decades. The country's electricity supply has historically been dominated by hydro, gas and coal, with generous feed-in tariffs kicking off a boom in solar and wind, resulting in the installation of 19.8 GW and 3.8 GW of capacity, respectively, in just four years.

**Installed capacity milestones:**
- Through 2020, total installed capacity of the power generation industry had reached 69,340 MW.
- As of 2022, Vietnam's total installed capacity stood at 80 GW, 32% of which comes from coal-fired power plants, followed by hydro power (29%) and solar power facilities (21%).
- With total installed capacity projected to reach 180 GW by 2030 and up to 800 GW by 2050, Vietnam is positioning itself as one of Asia's most dynamic emerging energy markets.

**Generation mix (2021):**
- In 2021, coal accounted for 45% of electricity generation, and fossil gas for 10%.
- Among renewables, hydropower supplied 31% of electricity, solar PV accounted for 11%, and the remaining capacity was covered by wind power and bioenergy, with a combined 2.3% of the total.
- While historically hydroelectric has been the dominant source of energy generation, coal has been the key power generation source since 2018. Coal accounted for about 30% of installed capacity and 47% of electricity generation in 2021.

**Coal capacity:**
- As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction.
- Coal-fired power plants remain dominant, accounting for approximately 43 percent of total installed capacity.

**Demand projections:**
- Commercial electricity is expected to reach about 335.0 billion kWh by 2025; about 505.2 billion kWh by 2030; about 1,114.1–1,254.6 billion kWh by 2050.

### 1.2 Policy Framework

Vietnam's power sector is governed through centralized national power development plans (PDPs), issued by the Prime Minister and implemented by the Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT).

**Plan evolution:**
- **PDP 6** (2001, Decision 40/2003/QD-TTg): Covered 2001–2010, emphasized hydropower development
- **PDP 7** (2011, Decision 1208/QD-TTg): Covered 2011–2020, introduced significant coal expansion
- **PDP 7 Revised** (2016, Decision 428/QD-TTg): Updated in response to evolving conditions, introduced wind/solar FITs
- On May 15, 2023, the Vietnamese government released Vietnam's Eighth Power Development Plan (PDP8) for the period of 2021–2030, with a vision to 2050. The Prime Minister issued Decision 500, which took effect on May 15, 2023, and covers a term of 27 years (2023 to 2050).
- On 15 April 2025, the Prime Minister of Vietnam issued Decision 768/QD-TTg approving the revision to the National Power Development Plan ("Revised PDP8"). This decision supersedes Decision 500/QD-TTg dated 15 May 2023 approving the original PDP8.
- Subsequently, on 30 May 2025, the Ministry of Industry and Trade issued Decision 1509/QD-BCT approving the implementation plan for the Revised PDP8.

**Key PDP8 provisions for thermal power:**
- By 2030, coal thermal power will contribute to only 20% of total planned energy resources for local use (30,127 MW out of 150,489 MW).
- Vietnam cancels around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline, while keeping a pipeline of new coal projects to around 13 GW. The PDP8 still allows the completion of eleven coal-fired power plants already included in the revised PDP7.
- Gas-fired thermal power capacity will reach 37,330 MW out of 150,489 MW, including domestic and imported sources.
- By 2050, all coal plants must be converted to alternative fuels or cease operation.
- Vietnam does not have a plan to develop new coal-fired plants after 2030.
- After 2035, no new LNG power plants will be developed.

**Revised PDP8 (2025) further adjustments:**
- To match higher GDP growth targets, the capacity targets increased from 90.5 GW to 89.655 GW–99.934 GW by 2030 and from 185.19 GW–208.56 GW to 205.7 GW–228.6 GW by 2050.
- The Amended PDP8 sets a more stringent objective limiting emissions to between 197–199 million tons by 2030, and a fixed target of 27 million tons by 2050.
- Changes in the energy mix and policy include an increase from 0.8 GW to 22.5 GW of imported LNG capacity by 2030.
- The 2025 plan sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050, with a total converted capacity of 25,798 MW.

**JETP Commitment:**
- In December 2022, Vietnam and a coalition of countries led by the European Union and the UK concluded a Just Energy Transition Partnership agreement with US$15.5 billion to fund the development and implementation of a plan to cap and then phase out unabated coal generation. The agreement proposed reducing Vietnam's coal fleet capacity from 37 GW to 30.2 GW by 2030.

### 1.3 Energy Supply Landscape

**Coal:**
- Vietnam has significant domestic anthracite coal reserves concentrated in Quang Ninh province (Quang Ninh coalfield). However, domestic production has plateaued.
- Vietnam increasingly relies on coal imports because coal-fired power plants have been used to meet rapidly increasing electricity demand; more than half of Vietnam's electricity generation came from coal in 2020.
- Imported coal (sub-bituminous) from Indonesia and Australia supplies plants in central and southern Vietnam (Vinh Tan, Duyen Hai, Van Phong complexes).
- PDP8 encourages measures to step up the search and exploration of new reserves of domestic coal.

**Natural Gas:**
- Domestic gas production comes from offshore fields in the Cuu Long, Nam Con Son, and Malay-Tho Chu basins, feeding plants in the southeast (Phu My, Nhon Trach, Ba Ria) and southwest (Ca Mau, O Mon).
- Gas power plants in Vietnam are located in the southern region, including 5,705 MW of capacity in the southeast, which requires 20–22 million cu m/d of gas, and 1,500 MW in the southwest. Since 2020, supply for these power plants has been on the decline due to ageing gas fields.
- Currently, average gas supply for the plants in the southeast stands at just 9.5 million cu m/d, less than half of demand. From 2025 onwards, gas supply is expected to continue this downward trend.
- Key future gas field: Block B (O Mon cluster), Ca Voi Xanh/Blue Whale (Dung Quat cluster)

**LNG:**
- Vietnam currently has only one operational LNG terminal, located at Thi Vai, with a capacity of 1 million metric tons per year.
- Hai Linh/Cap Mep terminal (Ba Ria-Vung Tau): 3 Mtpa, nearing operational status.
- Son My LNG terminal (Binh Thuan): planned
- The Government of Vietnam plans to import eight million tons per annum (MMTPA) of LNG between 2024 and 2030, and up to 15 MMTPA of LNG after 2030 for power purposes.

**Pipelines & Infrastructure:**
- Nam Con Son pipeline system: offshore to Phu My/Nhon Trach
- PM3-CAA pipeline: to O Mon (Ca Mau/O Mon)
- Block B – O Mon gas pipeline: under development, MOECO has taken a final investment decision (FID) for the Block B Project, with first gas expected by the end of 2026.
- Thi Vai LNG Terminal – Nhon Trach gas pipeline

### 1.5 Current Challenges

- **Energy trilemma:** Balancing energy security (rising demand, declining domestic gas), affordability (low regulated tariffs vs. high LNG import costs), and sustainability (net-zero 2050 commitment)
- The gas bet is risky; the international markets are volatile and domestic supplies uncertain. Projects could face delays or become stranded assets due to a shortage of affordable LNG.
- Energy analysts note there has not been support for long-term financially viable natural gas markets; it could be more cost effective to install the capacity with renewables.
- Regulatory uncertainties and delays in approving key projects have hindered the timely implementation of planned expansions. The lack of a clear pricing mechanism for gas and electricity tariffs can also complicate project financing.
- Grid congestion: Some of the country's transmission lines are operating at full load or are overloaded, especially in the region where solar capacity is concentrated. Electricity producers have reduced generation from renewables because of grid limitations.
- Coal import dependency creates exposure to international market fluctuations
- Aging coal fleet: Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years within this decade.

### 2.1 Coal-Fired Power Plants

#### A. NORTHERN REGION — Legacy Plants

**Ninh Bình Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Ninh Bình)
- **Location:** Ninh Binh province
- **Capacity:** 4 × 25 MW = 100 MW (subcritical, Soviet-design)
- **History:** Commissioned 1974 with Soviet assistance. Among Vietnam's earliest coal-fired plants. Uses domestic Quang Ninh anthracite.
- **Status:** Operational but aging (50+ years). Likely candidate for early retirement under PDP8 fuel conversion provisions.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM — plant is widely documented as operational but no recent primary source confirms current operational status.

**Uông Bí Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Uông Bí)
- **Location:** Uong Bi city, Quang Ninh province
- **Capacity:** Complex with multiple phases; earliest units from 1960s (4 × 12 MW, likely retired), 1970s units (2 × 55 MW), 1980s units (2 × 110 MW), and Uong Bi Extension (1 × 330 MW, 2014). Estimated active capacity: ~630 MW.
- **History:** Vietnam's oldest thermal power station, originally commissioned in 1963. Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. Expanded in multiple phases over six decades. Uses local anthracite coal from Quang Ninh mines.
- **Issues:** Extreme age of original units raises environmental and efficiency concerns. Extension unit represents a significant technology improvement.
- **Confidence:** HIGH for existence, MEDIUM for exact current capacity (old units may be partially decommissioned).

**Phả Lại Power Complex** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Phả Lại)
- **Location:** Pha Lai, Chi Linh, Hai Duong province
- **Pha Lai 1:** 4 × 110 MW = 440 MW, Soviet-built, COD 1983–1986
- **Pha Lai 2:** 2 × 300 MW = 600 MW, Japanese equipment (Sumitomo/IHI), COD 2001–2002
- **History:** Pha Lai 1 was built with Soviet ODA and represents Vietnam's first major coal-fired complex. Pha Lai 2 was financed by JBIC (Japan). Operated by EVNGENCO 2.
- The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — well-documented, government-operated.

**Na Dương 1 Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Na Dương 1)
- **Location:** Na Duong, Loc Binh district, Lang Son province
- **Capacity:** 2 × 50 MW = 100 MW (CFB technology)
- Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002, and was completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — confirmed by GEM, Vinacomin reports.

**Na Dương 2 Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Na Dương 2)
- **Location:** Lang Son province
- **Capacity:** 1 × 110 MW (CFB)
- In October 2015, work started on the over US$192 million thermal power plant in Lang Son.
- **History:** Vinacomin project with significant delays. In December 2023, Construction Corporation No. 1 and DR.AZ Group Co., Ltd. were awarded the 3,315 billion VND EPC contract. The contract's implementation period was 30 months.
- **Status:** Under construction (expected COD ~2026–2027)
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM — construction confirmed but timeline uncertain.

**Cao Ngạn Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện Cao Ngạn)
- **Location:** Thai Nguyen province
- **Capacity:** 2 × 50 MW = 100 MW
- **History:** Commissioned 2005–2006. Operated by EVNGENCO 1. Uses domestic coal.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM — documented in MOIT reports.

**An Khánh Thermal Power Plant** (Nhà máy Nhiệt điện An Khánh)
- **Location:** Thai Nguyen province
- **Capacity:** ~110 MW (2 × ~50–55 MW)
- **History:** Privately developed. Uses local coal. COD ~2013.
- **Confidence:** LOW — limited primary source documentation.
