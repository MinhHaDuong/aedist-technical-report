# Vietnam Thermal Generation Assets >30 MWe — Coal, Domestic Gas, Imported LNG  
**Status date of inventory:** 23 May 2026  
**Scope:** utility, BOT/IPP, captive/CHP, under-construction, planned, suspended, cancelled/removed, and formal planning-cycle pre-FID thermal assets in Vietnam using **Coal**, **Domestic gas**, or **Imported LNG**.  
**Capacity basis:** nameplate MWe unless noted; gross/net often unspecified in Vietnamese planning documents.  
**Confidence rule:** HIGH = two concordant admissible sources, at least one primary; MEDIUM = one primary or primary plus unresolved freshness/attribute issue; LOW = secondary-only, stale, uncertain, cancelled/removed with limited primary trail, or unresolved conflict.

---

## 1. Sector overview

Vietnam’s thermal generation fleet is dominated by coal and domestic gas, with LNG entering commercial operation in 2025 through Nhơn Trạch 3–4. PDP8 and the PDP8 implementation plan identify continued coal projects already committed or delayed, new domestic-gas projects tied mainly to Block B–Ô Môn and Cá Voi Xanh, and a large LNG pipeline intended to offset declining domestic gas and support system adequacy. Decision 262/QĐ-TTg of 1 April 2024 is the principal official source for 2023–2030 implementation lists, including domestic gas, LNG, coal, delayed coal, and cogeneration / industrial by-product sources. The adjusted PDP8 in 2025 preserved the planning continuity of projects already included under Decisions 500, 262 and 1682, while setting revised aggregate capacity orientations.

Operationally, EVN reported 2023 coal-fired installed capacity of 26,756 MW. Domestic gas generation is concentrated in the Southeast and Southwest gas systems: Phú Mỹ, Bà Rịa, Nhơn Trạch, Cà Mau and Ô Môn. Gas supply decline in the Southeast has reduced utilization of existing gas plants, while LNG infrastructure at Thị Vải underpins Nhơn Trạch 3–4 and future LNG projects.

Coal development is now largely limited to projects already in implementation or difficult/delayed legacy projects. Decision 262 identifies Vân Phong 1 as in operation; Vũng Áng 2, Na Dương 2, Quảng Trạch 1, An Khánh–Bắc Giang and Long Phú 1 as scheduled coal projects; and Quảng Trị, Công Thanh, Nam Định 1, Vĩnh Tân 3 and Sông Hậu 2 as delayed/difficulty projects. Several older PDP7 coal projects are treated here as cancelled/removed or suspended where they lack PDP8 implementation continuity.

Imported LNG projects include operating Nhơn Trạch 3–4 and planned / pre-FID projects such as Hiệp Phước LNG, Long An LNG, Bạc Liêu LNG, Sơn Mỹ LNG, Quảng Ninh LNG, Thái Bình LNG, Cà Ná LNG, Quảng Trạch II LNG and Quỳnh Lập LNG. Many LNG rows remain MEDIUM or LOW because inclusion in PDP8 implementation is official, but investment approval, PPA, fuel supply, land, financing and COD timing vary materially.

## 2. Concise narratives for important, disputed or ambiguous assets

### Nhơn Trạch 3–4 LNG
Nhơn Trạch 3–4 are Vietnam’s first LNG-fired power plants. PV GAS reported a 25-year LNG supply contract for commercial operation of the two PV Power plants, effective from 2025. Trade and contractor reports state the complex was inaugurated on 14 December 2025. The inventory treats both units as **Operating** as of 14 December 2025, with separate rows because they are distinct plants but share owner, fuel chain and EPC context.

### Ô Môn complex
Decision 262 lists Ô Môn I as 660 MW and already operating, using Block B gas, and lists Ô Môn II, IV and III as domestic-gas projects with planned operation in 2027, 2028 and 2030 respectively. Ô Môn I has historically burned oil/gas depending fuel availability; for this inventory it is classified as **Domestic gas** because the current PDP8 implementation treatment assigns it to Block B gas.

### Quảng Trạch I / Quảng Trạch II
Quảng Trạch I is retained as a coal project under construction in Decision 262, while Quảng Trạch II appears in the LNG project list as a 1,500 MW LNG project. Older planning sources treated Quảng Trạch II as coal; this inventory follows the later PDP8 implementation identity and classifies Quảng Trạch II as **Imported LNG**, with notes preserving the fuel-transition ambiguity.

### Vĩnh Tân 3
Vĩnh Tân 3 remains a difficult/delayed coal project in Decision 262, listed at 1,980 MW. Earlier sponsor arrangements changed and international developer participation weakened. Because Decision 262 still lists it in the difficult-project table rather than an operating/construction list, the status is **Suspended / delayed**, not cancelled.

### Sông Hậu 2
Sông Hậu 2 is listed in Decision 262’s delayed/difficulty coal table at 2,120 MW. Earlier BOT development has faced financing and implementation constraints. It is retained as **Suspended / delayed** with MEDIUM confidence because the latest official planning source confirms unresolved project status but not a final cancellation.

### Long Phú 1
Long Phú 1 is a long-delayed coal project. Decision 262 lists it in the coal project table with a 2027 target, but other public history shows significant delay. It is classified as **Planned / delayed**, not operating or under construction, pending fresh project execution evidence.

### Phú Mỹ 2.2 and Phú Mỹ 3 BOT
Phú Mỹ 2.2 and Phú Mỹ 3 are domestic-gas BOT CCGT assets in Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu. EVNGENCO3’s 2024 reporting notes O&M activity for Phú Mỹ 3 and preparation for O&M handover for Phú Mỹ 2.2, confirming continued operational relevance and transition of BOT arrangements.

### Retired / legacy plants: Ninh Bình, Thủ Đức, old gas turbines
Ninh Bình and Thủ Đức are old thermal assets with limited recent official plant-level confirmation. They are included because they exceed 30 MWe and are historically significant, but confidence is LOW/MEDIUM depending source freshness. Where retirement/standby status cannot be freshly confirmed, notes mark the ambiguity.

### Captive / CHP assets
Large industrial captive/CHP assets are less consistently reported than grid power plants. Formosa Hà Tĩnh captive coal power and Vedan Vietnam cogeneration are included because they are identifiable >30 MWe industrial thermal assets. Several steel, cement and refinery by-product plants are listed only where evidence supports electrical capacity; otherwise they are excluded or marked LOW.
