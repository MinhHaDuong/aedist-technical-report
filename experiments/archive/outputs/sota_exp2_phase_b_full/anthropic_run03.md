# VIETNAM THERMAL POWER GENERATION INVENTORY (>30 MWe)
**Compiled: May 2026 | Analyst report — primary-sourced**

---

## 1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

Vietnam's electricity system has grown rapidly, with installed capacity exceeding 80 GW by 2025. Thermal generation—coal, domestic gas, and LNG—remains the backbone, supplying roughly 50% of output.

The regulatory framework is anchored by Decision 500/QĐ-TTg (15 May 2023), the Power Development Plan VIII (PDP8), approved by the Prime Minister for the period 2021–2030 with a vision to 2050. By 2030, Vietnam targets total gas thermal power capacity of 14,930 MW, LNG capacity of 22,400 MW, and coal capacity of 30,127 MW. PDP8 was amended by Decision 768/QĐ-TTg (15 April 2025), signed by Deputy PM Bùi Thanh Sơn, with emphasis on feasibility, energy security, regional balance, and supporting GDP growth targets of 8%+ in 2025 and double digits through 2030.

PDP8 cancelled 11 coal projects totalling 13,220 MW (including Quảng Ninh III, Cẩm Phả III, Hải Phòng III, Quỳnh Lập I & II, Vũng Áng III, Long Phú II & III, Tân Phước I & II) and directed Quảng Trạch II to convert from coal to LNG before 2030. By 2050, Vietnam aims to eliminate unabated coal for electricity, shifting fully to biomass and ammonia co-firing.

Key actors include **EVN** (state utility, operates via Genco 1/2/3), **PVN/Petrovietnam** (through PV Power), **Vinacomin/TKV** (coal mining company with mine-mouth plants), **MOIT** (Ministry of Industry and Trade, regulator), and several BOT developers (Sumitomo, Marubeni/KEPCO, AES, COPI/China consortia). Of 13 LNG projects in PDP8, only Nhơn Trạch 3&4 was deployed on schedule as of early 2026, underscoring the LNG transition's implementation challenges.

## 2. KEY PLANT NARRATIVES

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 LNG Complex (Đồng Nai)
The project has a design capacity of 1,624 MW and total investment of $1.4 billion, making it Vietnam's first LNG-fired power complex, invested by PV Power (Petrovietnam subsidiary). PV Power reported that Nhơn Trạch 3 was ready for commercial operation on 20 November 2025, while Nhơn Trạch 4 was ready in December 2025. The complex entered commercial operation from 1 January 2026, with both plants completing acceptance testing and receiving market participation licences. This was the first power project in Vietnam to arrange international financing (>$1 billion) without government guarantees.

### Vĩnh Tân Complex (Bình Thuận)
The Vĩnh Tân power centre in Tuy Phong district, Bình Thuận hosts four coal-fired stations totalling ~4,244 MW. Vĩnh Tân 2's first unit generated power to the national grid in January 2014. Vĩnh Tân 1 (BOT, COPI consortium) commissioned in 2019; Vĩnh Tân 4 and 4MR (EVN/Genco3) commissioned 2017–2019. In September 2025, the National Assembly monitoring delegation heard that "hundreds of households" were impacted by pollution from the complex. Vĩnh Tân 3 (originally ~1,980 MW coal BOT) remains uncertain—a January 2026 Thanh Niên article questioned whether to continue or halt the project.

### Long Phú 1 (Sóc Trăng)
A 1,200 MW (2×600) coal plant developed by PVN. Construction has been stalled due to contractor sanctions (Power Machines, Russia) and financing difficulties. Listed in PDP8 as one of six coal projects to be "expeditiously completed," but progress remains minimal. Status: Under Construction (Stalled).

### Na Dương (Lạng Sơn)
Na Dương-1 is a two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired plant owned by Vinacomin, completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine. As of August 2024, electricity generation was reportedly declining due to limited coal supply. Na Dương 2 was slated to be operational before 31 December 2026. In April 2021 a consortium of Dongfang Electric and Vietnam Baiteng Construction signed the construction contract.

### Quảng Trạch (Quảng Bình)
Quảng Trạch I is a 1,200 MW (2×600) coal plant under construction by EVN. Quảng Trạch II was originally planned as coal but PDP8 directed it to shift to LNG before 2030, with planned capacity of 1,500 MW (2×750 CCGT).
