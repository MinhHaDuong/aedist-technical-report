# Vietnam Thermal Power Generation Inventory (>30 MWe)

*As of: 2024-06-15*

## Sector Overview

### Electricity Mix
As of 2023, Vietnam’s total installed electricity capacity exceeded **85 GW**, with thermal generation accounting for approximately **55–60%** of the mix. Coal-fired power contributed roughly **35%** (≈30 GW), while gas (domestic and LNG) provided about **15–20%** (≈13–17 GW). Hydropower remains significant (~20%), and renewables—especially solar and wind—have grown rapidly since 2019, now representing ~15% of capacity but lower in actual generation due to intermittency (IEA, 2023; EVN Annual Report, 2023).

### Policy Framework
Vietnam’s power development is guided by **Power Development Plan VIII (PDP VIII)**, approved by Prime Minister Decision No. 500/QD-TTg on May 15, 2023. Key shifts from PDP VII include:
- **Coal phase-down**: No new coal plants beyond those already under construction or committed as of 2023; existing projects may be deferred or converted to LNG.
- **LNG expansion**: Over 20 GW of LNG-to-power capacity planned by 2030, though many projects face financing and offtake challenges.
- **Renewables prioritization**: Solar, wind, and battery storage are central to new additions.
- **Emissions targets**: Net-zero by 2050 (COP26 commitment), driving decarbonization of thermal fleet.

PDP VIII caps total coal capacity at **30.9 GW by 2030**, down from earlier plans exceeding 55 GW (MOIT, 2023).

### Key Institutional Actors
- **Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Formulates energy policy, approves PDPs, oversees licensing.
- **Electricity of Vietnam (EVN)**: State-owned utility; owns/operates many thermal assets directly or via subsidiaries (e.g., EVNGENCO 1–3).
- **Provincial People’s Committees**: Issue land and environmental permits; often co-investors in IPP projects.
- **Vietnam Electricity Regulatory Authority (ERA)**: Regulates tariffs and grid access.
- **Development Finance Institutions (DFIs)**: World Bank, ADB, JICA historically supported gas; now shifting away from coal.

### Current Challenges
1. **Coal overcapacity risk**: Many plants completed post-2020 face low utilization due to slower demand growth and renewable influx.
2. **LNG infrastructure lag**: Only two operational LNG terminals (Thị Vải, Sơn Mỹ); regasification capacity insufficient for planned power demand.
3. **Financing constraints**: International lenders (e.g., Japan, Korea) have withdrawn from new coal; private investors hesitant on LNG without long-term PPAs.
4. **Grid congestion**: Southern region (coal/LNG hub) suffers transmission bottlenecks, limiting dispatch.
5. **Environmental compliance**: Older subcritical plants face pressure to retrofit or retire early under carbon pricing discussions.

## Per-Plant Narratives

*(Alphabetical by English name; only representative examples shown due to space; full inventory in table below)*

**Bac Lieu LNG-to-Power Plant**  
Planned as a 3,200 MW LNG-fired complex in Bac Lieu Province, developed by PV Gas and partners. Originally slated for COD 2025–2028, the project was scaled back to 1,600 MW in PDP VIII and faces delays due to lack of FID and LNG supply agreements. Status uncertain; confidence LOW. (Source: MOIT PDP VIII Annex; Recharge News, 2023)

**Duyen Hai 1 Thermal Power Plant**  
Located in Tra Vinh Province, this 1,200 MW coal plant (2 × 600 MW subcritical units) was commissioned in 2015–2016 under EVN’s crash program to address southern shortages. Operated by EVNGENCO 1. Notable for seawater-cooling design and frequent desulfurization upgrades. Fully operational; confidence HIGH. (Source: EVNGENCO 1 website; IEEFA, 2020)

**Nghi Son 1 & 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
A 1,200 MW coal plant (2 × 600 MW supercritical) in Thanh Hoa Province, developed by a consortium including KEPCO (Korea) and Marubeni. Achieved COD in 2022 after pandemic-related delays. One of Vietnam’s most efficient coal plants. Owned by Nghi Son Energy JSC. Confidence HIGH. (Source: KEPCO press release, 2022; MOIT PDP VII Revision)

**Phu My 1–4 Complex**  
A cluster of four CCGT plants (total ≈3,900 MW) in Ba Ria–Vung Tau, built between 1999–2004 using domestic gas from Nam Con Son basin. Operated by various entities (PV Power, EVN, Mitsubishi). Phu My 3 (720 MW) was Vietnam’s first large-scale CCGT. All units operational but facing gas supply constraints. Confidence HIGH. (Source: PV Power Annual Report, 2022; IEA Southeast Asia Energy Outlook 2022)

**Thai Binh 2 Thermal Power Plant**  
A stalled 1,200 MW coal project (2 × 600 MW) in Thai Binh Province. Construction began in 2013 but halted in 2017 due to contractor disputes and funding gaps. Officially “postponed indefinitely” in PDP VIII. Confidence MEDIUM (status confirmed, ownership unclear). (Source: MOIT Circular 36/2021/TT-BCT; VnExpress, 2021)

**Vinh Tan 4 Thermal Power Plant**  
A 1,200 MW coal plant (2 × 600 MW subcritical) in Binh Thuan Province, commissioned in 2017–2018. Developed by China Southern Grid and EVN. Notable for ash pond controversies and coastal erosion concerns. Fully operational. Confidence HIGH. (Source: China Southern Grid CSR Report, 2018; IEEFA, 2019)
