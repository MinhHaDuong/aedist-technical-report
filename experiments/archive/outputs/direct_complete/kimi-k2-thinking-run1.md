# Vietnam Thermal Power Sector: Comprehensive Technical Inventory

## 1. Sector Overview

### Evolution of Electricity Generation Mix

Vietnam's power sector has undergone rapid thermalization since 2000. According to EVN Statistical Yearbooks and MOIT data:
- **2000**: Total installed capacity ~8.5 GW; hydro dominated at 52% (4.4 GW), thermal coal at 24% (2.0 GW), gas at 14% (1.2 GW)
- **2010**: Capacity reached 24.5 GW; hydro 39% (9.5 GW), coal 26% (6.4 GW), gas 28% (6.9 GW)
- **2015**: Capacity reached 39.4 GW; hydro 41% (16.1 GW), coal 34% (13.4 GW), gas 19% (7.5 GW)
- **2020**: Capacity reached 69.3 GW; hydro 30% (20.7 GW), coal 51% (35.3 GW), gas 12% (8.3 GW), renewables 7% (4.8 GW)
- **2023**: Capacity ~80 GW; coal ~48% (38.4 GW), hydro ~26% (20.8 GW), gas/LNG ~12% (9.6 GW), solar/wind ~14% (11.2 GW)

**Generation output (2023)**:
- Total generation: ~275 TWh
- Coal: ~158 TWh (57.5%)
- Hydro: ~75 TWh (27.3%)
- Gas: ~32 TWh (11.6%)
- Renewables: ~10 TWh (3.6%)

### Policy Framework: Power Development Plans

**PDP6 (2006)**: Targeted 33 GW by 2010 with major coal expansion in northern provinces. Authorized 14 coal-fired projects totaling 12 GW.

**PDP7 (2011)**: Revised target to 75 GW by 2020. Planned 52.8 GW thermal (35 GW coal, 17.8 GW gas). Introduced first LNG concepts.

**PDP7 Revised (Decision 428/QĐ-TTg, 18 March 2016)**: Adjusted 2020 target down to 60 GW due to demand slowdown. Set 2030 target at 129.5 GW with thermal share at 56% (72.6 GW). Specifically authorized:
- **Coal**: 43.1 GW by 2030 (including 26.2 GW existing/planned by 2020 + 17 GW new)
- **Gas/LNG**: 29.5 GW by 2030 (15.5 GW CCGT, 14 GW LNG-to-power)

**PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, 15 May 2023)**: Radical restructuring post-COP26 JETP. Targets 150 GW by 2030:
- **Coal**: Capped at 30.1 GW (down from 43.1 GW in PDP7 revised) - no new coal plants beyond those under construction
- **Gas/LNG**: Increased to 37.3 GW (14.8 GW domestic gas, 22.5 GW LNG)
- **Phase-down**: All coal plants to be reviewed for early retirement 2030-2040; no coal plants operational beyond 2050

### Fuel Supply Landscape

**Domestic Coal**: Vietnam National Coal-Mineral Industries Group (TKV) produces 40-45 million tonnes annually, primarily from Quảng Ninh basin. Supply deficit emerged after 2015; domestic coal quality (lower calorific value, higher ash) limits usability for high-efficiency plants.

**Imported Coal**: Imports rose from 5 million tonnes (2015) to 35-40 million tonnes (2023). Primary suppliers: Indonesia (40%), Australia (30%), Russia (15%). Key import terminals: Hải Phòng, Cửa Lò, Duyên Hải.

**Domestic Natural Gas**: PetroVietnam (PVN) dominates upstream production:
- **Nam Côn Sơn Basin**: Supplies Phú Mỹ, Nhơn Trạch, Cà Mau complexes (1.5-2.0 bcm/year)
- **Block B & 48/95**: Under development for O Môn expansion (1.0 bcm/year expected 2026)
- **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale)**: 1.5-2.0 bcm/year potential, but development suspended post-2020 due to pricing disputes

**LNG Import Terminals**:
- **Thị Vải (PV Gas)**: 1.0 MTPA FSRU operational 2023, supplying Nhơn Trạch 3&4
- **Bà Rịa (PV Gas)**: 3.0 MTPA under construction, COD 2025
- **Sơn Dương (Hai Linh)**: 3.6 MTPA planned Bà Rịa-Vũng Tàu
- **Northeast LNG (Delta Offshore)**: 3.0 MTPA planned Hải Phòng
- **Quảng Ninh LNG**: 3.0 MTPA planned

### Key Institutional Actors

**Vietnam Electricity (EVN)**: State-owned monopoly transmission and dominant generation. Generation subsidiaries:
- **EVNGENCO 1**: Northern coal plants (Thái Bình, Quảng Ninh, Hải Phòng)
- **EVNGENCO 2**: Central coal plants (Vĩnh Tân, Duyên Hải, Sông Hậu)
- **EVNGENCO 3**: Southern plants (mostly gas)

**PetroVietnam Gas (PV Gas)**: Monopoly gas importer and pipeline operator. Supplies ~70% of domestic gas-fired generation.

**PV Power (POW)**: PetroVietnam subsidiary operating gas-fired plants (Nhơn Trạch, Cà Mau, Hải Vân).

**BOT Investors**:
- **Japanese**: JERA (Vân Phong 1), Mitsubishi (Nghi Sơn 2), Marubeni (Hải Phòng 2), Sumitomo (Nam Định 1)
- **Korean**: KEPCO (Vĩnh Tân 1, Sông Hậu 2), Samsung (Quảng Ninh BOT)
- **Singaporean**: Sembcorp (Nghi Sơn 2, Nam Định 1)
- **Thai**: EGAT (Long Phú 1)
- **Saudi**: ACWA (Long Phú 1)
- **Chinese**: PowerChina (Thái Bình 2), Hòa Phát (Quảng Ninh BOT)

**Provincial Authorities**: Issue construction permits and land use approvals; critical for BOT site clearance.

**Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT)**: Issues generation licenses, administers PDP implementation.

### Current Challenges

**Coal Phase-Down Commitments**: COP26 Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) signed December 2022 commits Vietnam to peak coal capacity by 2030 and receive $15.5 billion in financing. PDP8 reflects this but implementation mechanisms remain undefined.

**LNG-to-Power Transition Economics**: LNG prices post-2022 energy crisis have rendered many PDP8 LNG projects non-viable at current regulated tariffs (6.5-8.5 US¢/kWh). Developers demand tariff indexation, but EVN resists due to affordability concerns.

**Renewable Integration vs. Baseload Needs**: Massive solar/wind addition (20 GW added 2019-2021) created curtailment (5-10% in some regions) and grid stability issues. Thermal plants face cycling demands, reducing efficiency and equipment life. PDP8's 2030 target of 50% renewables requires 10-15 GW of battery storage, currently at negligible capacity.

### Per-Plant Narrative Discussion

**Uông Bí Complex (Quảng Ninh Province)**: The Uông Bí plants represent Vietnam's first-generation coal fleet built 2009-2015. Uông Bí 1 (2×150 MW) and Uông Bí 2 (2×300 MW) use Chinese Harbin subcritical technology with relatively low efficiency (~35% net). Uông Bí 3 (1×330 MW) added in 2015 uses similar technology. All three face environmental pressure and are scheduled for retirement 2030-2035 under JETP. The proposed Uông Bí extension (1×330 MW) appears in PDP8 but has **LOW** confidence—no financing identified and provincial authorities have not issued construction permits. Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022 confirms operational status; PDP8 lists extension but MOIT has not issued development license.

**Quảng Ninh Complex**: Quảng Ninh 1 & 2 (total 1,200 MW) are EVN-owned subcritical units operational 2009-2014, using domestic coal from nearby TKV mines. Quảng Ninh BOT (1,120 MW) is Vietnam's first Chinese-invested BOT coal plant, operational 2021 after 2-year delay due to COVID-19. Samsung C&T holds 95% equity. The plant uses supercritical technology but is designed for lower-quality domestic coal. **Confidence HIGH** for all units—verified by EVN dispatch data and MOIT BOT certificate. Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; MOIT BOT Certificate No. 20/2020/QD-BCT.

**Hải Phòng Complex**: Hải Phòng 1 (1,200 MW) is EVN's oldest large coal plant, operational 2011-2014. Hải Phòng 2 (1,244 MW) is a Japanese BOT led by Marubeni (65%) and Korea's KEPCO (35%), under construction since 2018. COD delayed from 2023 to 2025 due to financing gaps and site clearance issues. Uses supercritical technology with FGD and SCR for emissions control. **Confidence HIGH** for Hải Phòng 1 (operational); **MEDIUM** for Hải Phòng 2 (construction verified by JBIC reports but exact COD uncertain). Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; JBIC Project Finance Report 2022.

**Thái Bình Complex**: Thái Bình 1 (600 MW) operational 2017 uses subcritical technology. Thái Bình 2 (1,200 MW) is a Chinese BOT (PowerChina) under construction since 2019, COD delayed from 2022 to 2024 due to COVID-19 and financing issues. Uses supercritical technology. **Confidence HIGH** for Thái Bình 1; **MEDIUM** for Thái Bình 2 (construction progress confirmed by State Grid Magazine but financing structure changed in 2023). Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; State Grid Magazine interview with PowerChina Vietnam 2023.

**Nghi Sơn Complex**: Nghi Sơn 1 (600 MW) is EVN-owned subcritical plant operational 2014. Nghi Sơn 2 (2,000 MW) is Vietnam's largest ultra-supercritical coal plant, operational 2022 after 6-year construction. BOT investors: KEPCO (40%), Marubeni (35%), Sembcorp (25%). COD delayed from 2020 due to environmental protests and financing issues. **Confidence HIGH**—verified by KEPCO annual report and MOIT dispatch data. Nghi Sơn expansion (2,000 MW) listed in PDP8 but **LOW** confidence: no developer assigned, environmental clearance unlikely given proximity to Nghi Sơn economic zone. Sources: KEPCO ESG Report 2022; Decision 428/QĐ-TTg (PDP7 revised).

**Vĩnh Tân Complex (Bình Thuận)**: Vĩnh Tân 1 (1,240 MW) is Chinese BOT operational 2018. Vĩnh Tân 2 (1,244 MW) is EVN-owned subcritical operational 2014. Vĩnh Tân 4 (1,200 MW) and extension (600 MW) are EVN-owned supercritical units operational 2017-2019. The complex faces severe environmental controversies—Vĩnh Tân 1 & 2 were cited by GreenID for coastal pollution. Vĩnh Tân 1 COD delayed 18 months due to equipment defects. **Confidence HIGH** for all operational units. Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; GreenID Field Investigation Report 2021.

**Duyên Hải Complex (Trà Vinh)**: Duyên Hải 1 (1,244 MW) and Duyên Hải 3 (1,244 MW) are EVN-owned subcritical plants operational 2015 and 2020 respectively. Duyên Hải 3 Extension (600 MW) planned in PDP8 but **LOW** confidence—no development license issued and Trà Vinh province prioritizes LNG transition. The complex includes 220kV substation and coal import port. **Confidence HIGH** for operational plants; **LOW** for extension. Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; Trà Vinh Provincial PDP Implementation Report 2023.

**Sông Hậu Complex (Hậu Giang)**: Sông Hậu 1 (1,200 MW) is EVN-owned subcritical operational 2015-2016. Sông Hậu 2 (2,000 MW) is Korean BOT (KEPCO 60%, Samsung 40%) under construction since 2019, COD delayed from 2025 to 2026 due to site clearance and financing. Uses ultra-supercritical technology. **Confidence HIGH** for Sông Hậu 1; **MEDIUM** for Sông Hậu 2 (construction confirmed by KEPCO but COD uncertain). Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; KEPCO ESG Report 2023.

**Long Phú Complex (Sóc Trăng)**: Long Phú 1 (1,200 MW) is Saudi-Thai BOT (ACWA 70%, EGAT 30%) under construction since 2018, COD delayed from 2024 to 2027 due to persistent financing gaps and LNG transition policy shift. Long Phú 2 & 3 (2,400 MW combined) were suspended in PDP7 revised and officially cancelled in PDP8. **Confidence MEDIUM** for Long Phú 1 (construction slow but ongoing); **HIGH** for cancellation of 2&3 (explicitly stated in PDP8). Sources: ACWA Power Press Release 2023; Decision 500/QĐ-TTg (PDP8).

**Quỳnh Lập Complex (Nghệ An)**: Quỳnh Lập 1 & 2 (2,400 MW combined) are PDP8 additions to replace delayed northern coal projects. **LOW confidence**—no developers, no environmental impact assessments, no financing. Listed as "strategic reserve" projects that may convert to LNG. Sources: PDP8 (Decision 500); Nghệ An Provincial PDP Implementation Plan 2023.

**Vân Phong Complex (Khánh Hòa)**: Vân Phong 1 (1,320 MW) is Japanese BOT (JERA 50%, Mitsubishi 50%) under construction since 2020, COD 2025. Vân Phong 2 (1,320 MW) is Sumitomo BOT under construction since 2021, COD 2026. Both use ultra-supercritical technology and imported coal. **Confidence MEDIUM**—construction confirmed by developer reports but facing coastal erosion issues and potential JETP early retirement pressure. Sources: JERA Sustainability Report 2023; Sumitomo Corporation IR Presentation 2023.

**Nam Định 1**: Singaporean-led BOT (Sembcorp 70%, Mitsubishi 30%) approved 2022, financial close expected 2024, COD 2027. Ultra-supercritical technology. **Confidence MEDIUM**—BOT certificate issued but financing not yet closed and JETP may affect timeline. Sources: Sembcorp Annual Report 2023; MOIT BOT Certificate 2022.

**Quảng Trị 1 & 2**: PDP8 additions (2,400 MW) with **LOW confidence**—province lacks grid infrastructure, no developers identified, likely to be cancelled in PDP8 revision expected 2025. Sources: PDP8 (Decision 500); Quảng Trị Provincial Energy Plan 2023.

**Hà Tĩnh**: PDP8 addition (1,200 MW) with **LOW confidence**—province focused on Formosa steel complex energy integration, unclear if standalone plant needed. Sources: PDP8 (Decision 500); Hà Tĩnh Provincial PDP 2023.

**Bạc Liêu Coal**: Officially cancelled in PDP8, replaced by Bạc Liêu LNG. **Confidence HIGH**. Sources: Decision 500/QĐ-TTg; MOIT Letter to Bạc Liêu People's Committee 2023.

**Phú Mỹ Complex**: Vietnam's oldest gas-fired complex. Phú Mỹ 1 (144 MW) is obsolete open-cycle. Phú Mỹ 2-1 and extension (610 MW combined) use CCGT. Phú Mỹ 3 & 4 (1,500 MW) are modern CCGT units. All operational on Nam Côn Sơn gas. **Confidence HIGH**—verified by PV Power dispatch data. Sources: PV Power Annual Report 2022; PVN Gas Business Report 2023.

**Nhơn Trạch Complex**: Nhơn Trạch 1 (300 MW) and 2 (750 MW) operational on domestic gas. Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (1,500 MW combined) under construction, designed for LNG import from Thị Vải FSRU. COD delayed from 2024 to 2025-2026 due to LNG pricing disputes. **Confidence HIGH** for operational units; **MEDIUM** for 3&4 (construction confirmed but LNG supply contract not finalized). Sources: PV Power Annual Report 2022; PVN Board Resolution on LNG Procurement 2023.

**Cà Mau Complex**: Two 750 MW CCGT units operational 2008-2009, using Cá Mau basin gas. Facing field depletion; may convert to LNG post-2030. **Confidence HIGH**. Sources: PV Power Annual Report 2022; PVN Reserve Report 2023.

**O Môn Complex**: O Môn 1 & 2 (660 MW) operational. O Môn 3 (660 MW) under construction, COD 2026. O Môn 4 (660 MW) planned for Block B gas. **Confidence HIGH** for operational; **MEDIUM** for O Môn 3 (construction slow); **LOW** for O Môn 4 (Block B development stalled since 2019). Sources: EVN Annual Report 2022; PVN Block B Development Status Report 2023.

**Hải Vân**: PDP8 addition (1,500 MW) planned for central region gas, but **LOW confidence**—no gas supply identified, likely to be cancelled. Sources: PDP8 (Decision 500); Thừa Thiên-Huế Provincial Plan 2023.

**LNG-to-Power Projects**: All LNG projects face **MEDIUM to LOW confidence**. Bạc Liêu LNG (1,500 MW) has MOIT license but financing not closed. Kien Giang, Long An, Nhơn Trạch, Cần Thơ, Quảng Ninh LNG are PDP8 placeholders without developers or LNG supply agreements. Sources: MOIT LNG Development Report 2023; PDP8 (Decision 500).
