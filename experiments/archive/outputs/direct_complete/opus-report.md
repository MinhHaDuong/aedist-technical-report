

# Comprehensive Technical Inventory of Vietnam's Thermal Power Sector

## 1. Sector Overview

### 1.1 Evolution of Vietnam's Electricity Generation Mix

Vietnam's power sector has undergone a dramatic transformation over the past three decades. In the early 1990s, the system was overwhelmingly hydro-dependent, with total installed capacity below 5 GW. By the end of 2023, Vietnam's total installed capacity had reached approximately **80.5 GW**, making it the largest power system in Southeast Asia after Indonesia.

**Key statistics (end-2023 estimates):**

| Source | Installed Capacity (GW) | Share (%) | Generation (TWh, 2023 est.) | Share (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coal | ~25.8 | ~32% | ~130 | ~46% |
| Natural Gas (domestic + LNG) | ~7.2 | ~9% | ~28 | ~10% |
| Large Hydropower | ~22.0 | ~27% | ~72 | ~25% |
| Small Hydropower | ~3.8 | ~5% | ~10 | ~4% |
| Wind (onshore + offshore) | ~5.0 | ~6% | ~12 | ~4% |
| Solar (utility + rooftop) | ~16.5 | ~21% | ~27 | ~10% |
| Biomass/Other | ~0.5 | <1% | ~2 | <1% |
| **Total** | **~80.5** | **100%** | **~281** | **100%** |

*Sources: EVN Annual Report 2023 [1]; MOIT electricity statistics [2]; IEEFA analysis [3]. Note: Exact figures vary by source and whether gross or net capacity is reported. The figures above are best estimates synthesized from multiple sources.*

The trajectory has been marked by several phases:

- **Pre-2000**: Hydro-dominated system. Key plants included Hòa Bình (1,920 MW, commissioned 1994), Trị An (400 MW), and early small thermal plants (Phả Lại 1, Ninh Bình, Uông Bí).
- **2000–2010**: Rapid gas-to-power development in the south (Phú Mỹ complex, Bà Rịa, Nhơn Trạch) using domestic gas from the Nam Côn Sơn and Cửu Long basins; first wave of large coal plants in the north (Phả Lại 2, Quảng Ninh).
- **2010–2020**: Massive coal expansion — Vĩnh Tân, Duyên Hải, Nghi Sơn, Mông Dương, Hải Phòng complexes. Coal's share of generation rose from ~18% (2010) to ~50% (2020). Simultaneously, a solar and wind boom in 2019–2020 driven by feed-in tariffs added ~17 GW of renewable capacity.
- **2020–present**: Coal pipeline stalling due to financing difficulties and climate commitments; pivot toward LNG-to-power; continued renewable growth but curtailment challenges.

### 1.2 Policy Framework: Power Development Plans

Vietnam's power sector planning is governed by national Power Development Plans (Quy hoạch Phát triển Điện lực — PDP), approved by the Prime Minister:

**PDP6 (Decision 110/2007/QĐ-TTg, 2007):** Covered the period 2006–2015 with a vision to 2025. Envisioned significant coal expansion and the introduction of nuclear power. Total capacity target of ~36.7 GW by 2015 and ~67.5 GW by 2025. [4]

**PDP7 (Decision 1208/QĐ-TTg, 2011):** Covered 2011–2020 with a vision to 2030. Targeted total capacity of ~75 GW by 2020 and ~146.8 GW by 2030. Coal was projected to reach ~36 GW by 2020 and ~75.7 GW by 2030. Included nuclear power (Ninh Thuận). Gas-fired capacity was projected at ~10.4 GW by 2020 and ~11.3 GW by 2030. [5]

**PDP7 Revised (Decision 428/QĐ-TTg, March 2016):** A major revision that reduced coal targets and cancelled nuclear power. Key thermal targets: [6]
- Coal: ~26 GW by 2020, ~55.3 GW by 2025, ~55.3 GW by 2030
- Gas (domestic + LNG): ~9 GW by 2020, ~15 GW by 2025, ~19 GW by 2030
- Total system: ~60 GW by 2020, ~96 GW by 2025, ~130 GW by 2030
- First mention of LNG import for power generation

**PDP8 (Decision 500/QĐ-TTg, May 15, 2023):** The current governing plan, covering 2021–2030 with a vision to 2050. Represents a significant shift: [7]
- Coal: Capped at ~30.1 GW by 2030 (only projects already under construction or with irreversible commitments); no new coal after 2030; phase-out of coal by 2050
- Gas (domestic + LNG): ~37.3 GW by 2030, of which ~22.4 GW is LNG-to-power
- Renewables: ~27 GW onshore wind, ~6 GW offshore wind, ~12.8 GW solar by 2030
- Total system: ~150.5 GW by 2030
- Emphasis on LNG as "transition fuel"

**PDP8 Implementation Plan (Decision 262/QĐ-TTg, April 1, 2024):** Provided the specific project list for PDP8, assigning individual plants to provinces and timelines. [8]

### 1.3 Fuel Supply Landscape

**Domestic Coal:**
Vietnam's coal industry is dominated by **Vinacomin (TKV — Tập đoàn Công nghiệp Than – Khoáng sản Việt Nam)**, which operates mines primarily in Quảng Ninh province (Hòn Gai, Cẩm Phả, Uông Bí basins). Domestic production peaked at approximately 47 million tonnes in 2017 but has since declined to ~44 million tonnes (2023). Domestic coal is predominantly anthracite, with high calorific value but limited reserves for power generation expansion. [9]

Vietnam became a net coal importer around 2015. By 2023, coal imports (primarily sub-bituminous and bituminous coal from Indonesia, Australia, Russia, and South Africa) reached approximately **44 million tonnes**, roughly matching domestic production. Imported coal is used primarily by coastal power plants in the south and central regions (Vĩnh Tân, Duyên Hải, Vân Phong, Long Phú). [10]

**Domestic Natural Gas:**
**PetroVietnam (PVN)** and its subsidiaries manage Vietnam's upstream gas production and midstream infrastructure:

- **Cửu Long Basin**: Mature fields (Bạch Hổ, Rạng Đông) with declining output; gas piped to Bà Rịa processing plant.
- **Nam Côn Sơn Basin**: The largest producing gas basin. Block 06.1 (Lan Tây, Lan Đỏ fields — BP/PVN JV) supplies gas via the Nam Côn Sơn 1 pipeline to Phú Mỹ power complex. Block 11.2 (Rồng Đôi/Rồng Đôi Tây) feeds Nam Côn Sơn 2 pipeline. Total Nam Côn Sơn pipeline capacity: ~20 million m³/day (NCS1) + ~20 million m³/day (NCS2). [11]
- **PM3-CAA / Block 46-Cái Nước**: Malaysia-Vietnam joint development area; gas piped to Cà Mau power complex.
- **Block B — Ô Môn (Block B)**: Long-delayed development in the Malay-Thổ Chu basin. Consortium led by PVN/MOECO/PTTEP. Gas intended for Ô Môn III and IV power plants in Cần Thơ. Final investment decision (FID) has been repeatedly delayed; latest target is 2024–2025 with first gas expected 2027–2028. [12]
- **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) — Block 118**: Discovered by ExxonMobil in 2011 in the central offshore area (Quảng Nam basin). Estimated reserves of 150 billion m³. Gas intended for Dung Quất and Chu Lai power complexes in Quảng Nam/Quảng Ngãi. Development has been delayed by commercial terms disputes and geopolitical sensitivities (South China Sea). ExxonMobil reportedly considering divestment. [13]

Domestic gas production has been declining from a peak of ~10.7 billion m³ (2014) to ~7.5 billion m³ (2023), creating urgency for LNG imports. [14]

**LNG Import Terminal Projects:**
Vietnam has embarked on an ambitious LNG-to-power program. Key terminal projects include:

The **Thị Vải LNG terminal** (Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu) achieved first LNG cargo receipt in July 2023, making it Vietnam's first operational LNG import facility. It supplies regasified LNG to the Nhơn Trạch 3&4 CCGT project and supplements declining domestic gas for existing Phú Mỹ plants. [15]

### 1.4 Key Institutional Actors

**Electricity of Vietnam (EVN):** State-owned vertically integrated utility. Owns ~38% of generation capacity through three generation subsidiaries:
- **EVNGENCO 1** (Tổng Công ty Phát điện 1): Operates Uông Bí expanded, Nghi Sơn 1, Duyên Hải 1 & 3, Vĩnh Tân 4 & 4 expanded, and several hydro plants. [18]
- **EVNGENCO 2** (Tổng Công ty Phát điện 2): Partially equitized (IPO 2021). Operates Phả Lại, Ô Môn 1, and several hydro plants. [19]
- **EVNGENCO 3** (Tổng Công ty Phát điện 3): Equitized. Operates Phú Mỹ 1, Phú Mỹ 4, Vĩnh Tân 2, and several hydro plants. Listed on HOSE as PGV. [20]

**PetroVietnam (PVN) and subsidiaries:**
- **PV Power (Tổng Công ty Điện lực Dầu khí):** Listed on HOSE as POW. Operates Cà Mau 1&2, Nhơn Trạch 1&2, Vũng Áng 1. Developing Nhơn Trạch 3&4 (LNG). [21]
- **PV Gas (Tổng Công ty Khí Việt Nam):** Listed on HOSE as GAS. Manages gas pipelines, processing, and LNG terminals. [15]

**BOT/IPP Investors:**
- **JBIC/Japanese consortia**: Nghi Sơn 2 (Marubeni/KEPCO), Vân Phong 1 (Sumitomo/JERA), Vĩnh Tân 1 (consortium with Chinese partners)
- **Chinese investors**: Vĩnh Tân 1 (CPIH), Duyên Hải 2 (Janakuasa/Malakoff — Malaysian, but with Chinese EPC), Hải Dương (Jaks Resources/CPECC)
- **Korean investors**: Mông Dương 2 (AES/POSCO → now Vinhomes acquired AES stake), Nghi Sơn 2 (KEPCO)
- **Others**: Phú Mỹ 3 (Sembcorp/Kyushu/JBIC), Phú Mỹ 2.2 (EDF/Sumitomo/TEPCO)

**Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT):** Responsible for power planning, licensing, and regulation. The Electricity Regulatory Authority of Vietnam (ERAV) sits under MOIT. [22]

### 1.5 Current Challenges

**COP26 and JETP Commitments:** At COP26 (November 2021), Vietnam pledged to achieve net-zero emissions by 2050 and joined the Global Coal to Clean Power Transition Statement. In December 2022, Vietnam signed the **Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP)** with the International Partners Group (IPG), mobilizing an initial $15.5 billion in public and private finance to support Vietnam's energy transition. The JETP Resource Mobilization Plan (December 2023) targets peaking power sector emissions by 2030 and capping coal capacity at ~30.2 GW. [23]

**LNG-to-Power Transition Economics:** The economics of LNG-to-power in Vietnam face significant headwinds:
- LNG spot prices remain volatile (post-Ukraine crisis)
- Vietnam's regulated electricity tariffs (~1,920 VND/kWh average retail, ~$0.08/kWh in 2024) are among the lowest in the region, making it difficult to pass through LNG fuel costs
- Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) negotiations for LNG plants have been protracted — the "take-or-pay" gas supply agreements and capacity payment mechanisms remain unresolved for most projects
- Currency risk: LNG is priced in USD while revenues are in VND [24]

**Renewable Integration vs. Baseload:** Vietnam's rapid solar/wind buildout has created curtailment issues (estimated 1.3 TWh curtailed in 2021 alone), while the system still relies on coal and gas for baseload and dispatchable capacity. Transmission constraints, particularly the north-south 500 kV backbone, limit power transfer from renewable-rich southern/central regions. [25]

### 2.2 Per-Plant Discussion (Sourced Narratives)

Below are narrative discussions for each major plant or plant complex. Plants are grouped by complex where appropriate.

---

#### COAL PLANTS

**C1 — Ninh Bình TPP**
Vietnam's oldest surviving thermal power plant, with original Soviet-built units dating to the 1970s. The original 4×25 MW units (100 MW total) used domestic anthracite coal. A 100 MW replacement unit (Unit 5) was added around 2005. The older units have been progressively retired or placed in reserve. Under PDP8, Ninh Bình is slated for eventual retirement as part of coal phase-down. The plant has been a significant local pollution source. **Confidence: HIGH** (verified by EVN/EVNGENCO 2 reports and government decisions). [6][19]

**C2/C3/C4 — Uông Bí TPP Complex**
Located in Quảng Ninh province adjacent to the Uông Bí coal mining area. The original plant (C2) dates to the 1960s (Soviet-era construction) with 110 MW of subcritical capacity using domestic anthracite. The first expansion (C3, 300 MW subcritical) was commissioned in 2014 using Chinese equipment (Dongfang Electric). The second expansion (C4, 330 MW supercritical) was commissioned in 2018, representing one of Vietnam's first supercritical coal units. All units are operated by EVNGENCO 1. The original units are candidates for early retirement under PDP8. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][18]

**C5/C6 — Phả Lại TPP Complex**
One of Vietnam's most important historical power plants, located in Chí Linh, Hải Dương province. Phả Lại 1 (C5, 4×110 MW = 440 MW) was built with Soviet assistance and commissioned 1983–1986. It uses domestic coal and subcritical technology. Phả Lại 2 (C6, 2×300 MW = 600 MW) was commissioned 2001–2002 with Japanese ODA financing (Sumitomo/IHI equipment). The operating company, Phả Lại Thermal Power JSC (PPC), was one of the first EVN subsidiaries to be equitized and is listed on HOSE. PPC is now under EVNGENCO 2. Phả Lại 1's aging units face retirement pressure. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][19]

**C7/C8 — Hải Phòng TPP Complex**
Located in Thủy Nguyên district, Hải Phòng. Hải Phòng 1 (C7, 2×300 MW) was commissioned 2009–2010 and Hải Phòng 2 (C8, 2×300 MW) in 2013–2014. Both use subcritical technology with domestic coal. Built by Lilama (Vietnam) with Chinese equipment. Operated by EVNGENCO 2 subsidiaries. The complex has faced environmental complaints from nearby communities regarding ash disposal and air quality. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][26]

**C9/C10 — Quảng Ninh TPP Complex**
Located in Hạ Long, Quảng Ninh. Quảng Ninh 1 (C9, 2×300 MW) commissioned 2010–2011 and Quảng Ninh 2 (C10, 2×300 MW) in 2012–2014. Subcritical technology using domestic coal. Operated by Quảng Ninh Thermal Power JSC, an EVN subsidiary. The plant is situated near Hạ Long Bay, raising environmental concerns about impacts on the UNESCO World Heritage Site. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][27]

**C11/C12/C13/C14 — Vinacomin (TKV) Mine-Mouth Plants in Quảng Ninh**
TKV operates several mine-mouth coal plants in Quảng Ninh using circulating fluidized bed (CFB) technology, which is suited to burning low-grade coal and coal waste:
- **Mạo Khê (C11)**: 2×220 MW CFB, commissioned 2013–2015
- **Đông Triều (C12)**: 2×125 MW CFB, commissioned 2016–2017
- **Cẩm Phả 1 (C13)**: 2×110 MW CFB, commissioned 2010–2011
- **Cẩm Phả 2 (C14)**: 2×110 MW CFB, commissioned 2012–2013

These plants primarily burn coal waste and low-grade coal from TKV's mining operations. They are relatively small and less efficient than utility-scale plants but serve a waste management function. **Confidence: HIGH** (verified by TKV annual reports). [6][28]

**C15/C16 — Mông Dương TPP Complex**
Located in Cẩm Phả, Quảng Ninh. One of Vietnam's largest coal complexes:
- **Mông Dương 1 (C15)**: 2×500 MW (sometimes reported as 2×540 MW gross), subcritical, commissioned 2015. Operated by EVNGENCO 1. Built by Dongfang Electric (China).
- **Mông Dương 2 (C16)**: 2×600 MW (1,200 MW total), subcritical, commissioned 2015. This is a BOT project developed by AES Corporation (USA) and POSCO Energy (Korea), with CIC (China) as EPC contractor. The 25-year BOT contract runs until ~2040. In 2023, AES sold its 51% stake to Vinhomes (a subsidiary of Vingroup), marking a notable exit of a major international investor from Vietnam's coal sector. The sale price was reportedly ~$400 million. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][18][29]

**C17/C51 — An Khánh TPP**
Small CFB plants in Thái Nguyên and Bắc Giang provinces, operated by An Khánh JSC (a private company). Total capacity approximately 100–200 MW. These plants burn domestic coal and coal waste. Details are less well-documented than EVN/PVN plants. **Confidence: MEDIUM** [6][30]

**C18 — Cao Ngạn TPP**
Located in Thái Nguyên, 2×55 MW CFB, commissioned 2008. A TKV subsidiary. Burns local coal waste. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][28]

**C19 — Sơn Động TPP**
Located in Bắc Giang, 1×220 MW CFB, commissioned 2017. A TKV subsidiary. Burns coal from the Sơn Động mine. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][28]

**C20/C21 — Na Dương TPP Complex**
Located in Lạng Sơn province, using local lignite coal:
- **Na Dương 1 (C20)**: 2×55 MW CFB, commissioned 2004. One of Vietnam's first CFB plants.
- **Na Dương 2 (C21)**: 1×110 MW CFB, has been under construction for several years with significant delays. Originally expected by 2020, now targeted for 2024–2025. Delays attributed to financing difficulties and low lignite quality. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (construction status uncertain). [6][7][28]

**C22 — Nông Sơn TPP**
A very small plant (30 MW) in Quảng Nam province, using local coal from the Nông Sơn coal mine. Commissioned 1999. Operated by a TKV subsidiary. Likely a candidate for retirement. **Confidence: MEDIUM** [6][28]

**C23 — Hải Dương TPP (BOT)**
Located in Kinh Môn, Hải Dương. 2×600 MW subcritical, using imported coal. Developed as a BOT by **Jaks Resources** (Malaysia) and **China Power Engineering Consulting Group (CPECC)**. The project faced significant delays and cost overruns. Originally planned for COD 2018, Unit 1 achieved COD in late 2020 and Unit 2 in 2021. The project was controversial due to Chinese financing (Bank of China, ICBC) and environmental concerns. Jaks Resources reported financial difficulties related to the project. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][31]

**C24/C25 — Thái Bình TPP Complex**
Located in Thái Thụy district, Thái Bình province:
- **Thái Bình 1 (C24)**: 2×300 MW subcritical, commissioned 2018. Operated by EVNGENCO 1.
- **Thái Bình 2 (C25)**: 2×600 MW subcritical, commissioned 2022–2024. Developed by PV Power (PetroVietnam). This project became one of Vietnam's most notorious infrastructure scandals. Originally planned for COD 2014, it suffered massive delays and cost overruns (from ~$1.3 billion to over $2 billion). The project was linked to corruption cases involving senior PetroVietnam officials (Đinh La Thăng, Trịnh Xuân Thanh), who were convicted and imprisoned. Construction was suspended for several years before resuming. Unit 1 finally achieved COD in 2022 and Unit 2 in early 2024. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][7][21]

**C26/C27 — Nghi Sơn TPP Complex**
Located in Nghi Sơn Economic Zone, Thanh Hóa province:
- **Nghi Sơn 1 (C26)**: 2×300 MW subcritical, commissioned 2013–2014. Operated by EVNGENCO 1. Uses imported coal.
- **Nghi Sơn 2 (C27)**: 2×600 MW supercritical, commissioned 2022–2023. A BOT project developed by **Marubeni** (Japan) and **KEPCO** (Korea). Financed by JBIC and Korean Exim Bank. One of the last coal BOT projects to reach financial close in Vietnam (2018). The project proceeded despite growing international pressure against coal financing. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][18][32]

**C28/C29 — Vũng Áng TPP Complex (Phase 2 & 3)**
Located in Kỳ Anh, Hà Tĩnh province (adjacent to Vũng Áng 1):
- **Vũng Áng 2 (C28)**: 2×600 MW supercritical. Under construction. Developed by a consortium of **Mitsubishi Corporation**, **CLP Holdings** (Hong Kong), and **EGCO** (Thailand). Achieved financial close in 2022 with JBIC financing. Expected COD 2026–2027. One of the last coal projects to secure international financing.
- **Vũng Áng 3 (C29)**: 2×600 MW. Was planned under PDP7 revised but **removed from PDP8**. Samsung C&T (Korea) had been the proposed developer but withdrew. **Confidence: HIGH (C28), HIGH (C29 — confirmed cancelled)** [6][7][33]

**C30 — Formosa Hà Tĩnh Captive TPP**
A captive power plant serving the Formosa Hà Tĩnh steel complex in Kỳ Anh, Hà Tĩnh. Approximately 650 MW of coal-fired capacity (multiple units). Not connected to the national grid for export (primarily captive use with some surplus). The Formosa complex was the source of Vietnam's worst environmental disaster in 2016, when toxic waste discharge killed marine life along 200 km of central Vietnam's coastline. The power plant itself was not the direct cause, but the incident heightened scrutiny of the entire complex. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (exact capacity figures for the captive plant vary by source). [34][35]

**C31 — Vĩnh Tân 1 TPP (BOT)**
Located in Tuy Phong, Bình Thuận. 2×600 MW subcritical, using imported coal. A BOT project developed by **China Power Investment Holding (CPIH)** and **Vinacomin**. Commissioned 2018–2019. Financed by Chinese banks (CDB, ICBC). The Vĩnh Tân complex has been the site of major environmental protests — in April 2015, thousands of local residents blocked National Highway 1A to protest coal dust and ash pollution from the complex. The government subsequently imposed stricter environmental requirements. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][36]

**C32 — Vĩnh Tân 2 TPP**
Located in Tuy Phong, Bình Thuận. 2×622 MW subcritical, commissioned 2014–2015. Operated by EVNGENCO 3. Uses imported coal. Was the first large unit at the Vĩnh Tân complex. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][20]

**C33 — Vĩnh Tân 3 TPP**
Originally planned as 3×660 MW supercritical coal under PDP7 revised. Developer was **OneEnergy** (a Vietnamese company) with **Mitsubishi** as partner. Under PDP8, this project was **converted from coal to LNG** (see L19). The conversion reflects Vietnam's policy shift away from new coal. **Confidence: HIGH** (confirmed by PDP8 Decision 500). [6][7]

**C34/C35 — Vĩnh Tân 4 and Expansion**
Located in Tuy Phong, Bình Thuận:
- **Vĩnh Tân 4 (C34)**: 2×600 MW subcritical, commissioned 2017–2018. Operated by EVNGENCO 1.
- **Vĩnh Tân 4 Expansion (C35)**: 1×600 MW supercritical, commissioned 2019–2020. Also EVNGENCO 1.
Together with Vĩnh Tân 1 and 2, the Vĩnh Tân complex totals approximately 4,244 MW of operational coal capacity, making it Vietnam's largest thermal power center. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][18]

**C36/C37/C38/C39 — Duyên Hải TPP Complex**
Located in Duyên Hải district, Trà Vinh province. One of Vietnam's largest coal complexes:
- **Duyên Hải 1 (C36)**: 2×622 MW subcritical, commissioned 2015–2016. EVNGENCO 1.
- **Duyên Hải 2 (C37)**: 1×622 MW subcritical, commissioned 2017. A BOT project by **Janakuasa Sdn Bhd** (Malaysia, a subsidiary of Malakoff Corporation). EPC by CPECC (China).
- **Duyên Hải 3 (C38)**: 2×622 MW subcritical, commissioned 2017–2018. EVNGENCO 1.
- **Duyên Hải 3 Expansion (C39)**: 1×688 MW supercritical, commissioned 2019. EVNGENCO 1. This was Vietnam's first supercritical unit at the Duyên Hải complex.
Total operational capacity at Duyên Hải: approximately 3,798 MW. The complex uses imported coal (primarily from Indonesia and Australia) delivered by sea. A dedicated coal port and 500 kV transmission line connect the complex to the southern grid. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][18][37]

**C40/C41/C42 — Long Phú TPP Complex**
Located in Long Phú district, Sóc Trăng province:
- **Long Phú 1 (C40)**: 2×600 MW subcritical. Developed by PV Power. This project has been **severely delayed** — originally planned for COD 2018. The EPC contractor was **Power Machines** (Russia), which was sanctioned by the US in 2018 (related to Crimea sanctions), causing the project to stall as equipment delivery and financing were disrupted. PV Power has been seeking alternative solutions, including potentially replacing the Russian contractor. As of 2024, the project remains incomplete with an uncertain timeline (2027 at earliest). This is one of Vietnam's most problematic power projects.
- **Long Phú 2 (C41)**: 2×660 MW. Was to be developed by **Tata Power** (India), but Tata withdrew in 2018 citing financing difficulties and environmental concerns. **Removed from PDP8.**
- **Long Phú 3 (C42)**: 2×660 MW. Never assigned a developer. **Removed from PDP8.**
**Confidence: HIGH** (Long Phú 1 delays well-documented; Long Phú 2/3 cancellation confirmed by PDP8). [6][7][21]

**C43/C44 — Sông Hậu TPP Complex**
Located in Châu Thành district, Hậu Giang province:
- **Sông Hậu 1 (C43)**: 2×600 MW subcritical. Developed by PV Power. Like Thái Bình 2, this project suffered significant delays (originally planned for COD 2017). Unit 1 achieved COD in late 2020 and Unit 2 in 2022. The project was also linked to the PetroVietnam corruption cases. Uses imported coal.
- **Sông Hậu 2 (C44)**: 2×600 MW. Was to be developed by **Toyo Ink Group** (Malaysia), but the developer withdrew. **Removed from PDP8.**
**Confidence: HIGH** [6][7][21]

**C45 — Vân Phong 1 TPP (BOT)**
Located in Vạn Ninh district, Khánh Hòa province. 2×660 MW supercritical, using imported coal. A BOT project developed by **Sumitomo Corporation** (Japan), **JERA** (Japan), and **Korea Western Power (KOWEPO)**. Financial close was achieved in 2020 with JBIC and Korean Exim Bank financing. Construction is underway with expected COD 2027–2028. This is one of the last coal BOT projects in Vietnam and has faced criticism from environmental groups. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][38]

**C46/C47 — Quảng Trạch TPP Complex**
Located in Quảng Trạch district, Quảng Bình province:
- **Quảng Trạch 1 (C46)**: 2×600 MW supercritical. Under construction by EVNGENCO 1. Expected COD 2025–2026. Uses imported coal. EPC by a consortium including Doosan (Korea).
- **Quảng Trạch 2 (C47)**: Originally planned as 2×600 MW coal. Under PDP8, **converted to LNG** (see L14). This conversion is a key example of Vietnam's coal-to-gas transition policy.
**Confidence: HIGH** [6][7][18]

**C48 — Công Thanh TPP**
Located in Thanh Hóa province. 2×300 MW CFB, proposed by **Công Thanh Group** (a private Vietnamese company primarily in cement). The project has been **suspended/stalled** for many years due to the developer's financial difficulties and inability to secure financing. It was included in PDP7 revised but its status under PDP8 is unclear — it does not appear in the PDP8 project list. Likely effectively cancelled. **Confidence: LOW** [6][39]

**C49/C50 — Quỳnh Lập TPP Complex**
Located in Nghệ An province:
- **Quỳnh Lập 1 (C49)**: 2×600 MW. Was to be developed by **ACWA Power** (Saudi Arabia), but ACWA withdrew. **Removed from PDP8.**
- **Quỳnh Lập 2 (C50)**: 2×600 MW. Never assigned a developer. **Removed from PDP8.**
**Confidence: HIGH** (cancellation confirmed by PDP8). [6][7]

**C53 — Thăng Long TPP**
Located in Quảng Ninh province. 2×300 MW subcritical, commissioned 2018. Developed by **Geleximco** (a major Vietnamese private conglomerate). Uses domestic coal. One of the few large coal plants developed by a private Vietnamese company. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (limited public reporting compared to EVN/PVN plants). [6][40]

**C54 — Hải Hà TPP**
Located in Quảng Ninh province. 2×600 MW supercritical was planned under PDP7 revised. **ACWA Power** (Saudi Arabia) was the proposed developer but withdrew. **Removed from PDP8.** **Confidence: HIGH** (cancellation confirmed). [6][7]

**C55 — Nam Định 1 TPP**
2×600 MW supercritical coal, planned for Nam Định province. **Taekwang** (Korea) was the proposed developer but withdrew. **Removed from PDP8.** **Confidence: HIGH** [6][7]

**C56 — Quảng Trị TPP (Coal)**
2×600 MW supercritical coal, planned for Quảng Trị province. **KOSPO** (Korea Southern Power) was the proposed developer but withdrew. **Removed from PDP8.** The site may be repurposed for LNG (see Hải Lăng LNG). **Confidence: HIGH** [6][7]

**C57 — Vũng Áng 1 TPP**
Located in Kỳ Anh, Hà Tĩnh. 2×600 MW subcritical, commissioned 2014–2015. Operated by **PV Power**. Uses imported coal. One of PV Power's largest assets. The plant is adjacent to the Formosa steel complex and the Vũng Áng 2 construction site. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][21]

**C58/C59 — Kiên Lương TPP Complex**
Located in Kiên Giang province:
- **Kiên Lương 1 (C58)**: 2×600 MW. Was proposed by **Tân Tạo Group (ITA)**, a Vietnamese private company. The project stalled for years due to ITA's inability to secure financing and land clearance issues. **Removed from PDP8.**
- **Kiên Lương 2 (C59)**: 2×600 MW. Never progressed. **Removed from PDP8.**
**Confidence: HIGH** (cancellation confirmed). [6][7]

**C60 — Sóc Trăng 1 TPP**
2×600 MW coal, planned for Sóc Trăng province. Never assigned a developer. **Removed from PDP8.** **Confidence: HIGH** [6][7]

**C61 — Bình Thuận (An Lộc) TPP**
2×600 MW coal, planned for Bình Thuận province. **Removed from PDP8.** **Confidence: HIGH** [6][7]

#### GAS PLANTS

**G1 — Bà Rịa TPP**
Vietnam's first gas-fired power plant, located in Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu. Originally commissioned in 1995 as 4×32.5 MW open-cycle gas turbines (GE Frame 6) using gas from the Bạch Hổ field. A 327 MW combined-cycle conversion was completed around 2005. Operated by PV Power. The plant is aging and operates at reduced capacity due to declining domestic gas supply. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][21]

**G2/G3/G4/G5/G6/G7 — Phú Mỹ TPP Complex**
Located in Tân Thành (now Phú Mỹ town), Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu. Vietnam's largest gas-fired power complex, with total capacity of approximately 3,900 MW across multiple plants:

- **Phú Mỹ 1 (G2)**: ~1,090 MW CCGT, commissioned 2002–2004. EVNGENCO 3. Uses Siemens/ABB gas turbines.
- **Phú Mỹ 2.1 (G3)**: ~720 MW CCGT, commissioned 2001–2005. EVNGENCO 3.
- **Phú Mỹ 2.1 Extension (G4)**: ~440 MW CCGT, commissioned 2005. EVNGENCO 3.
- **Phú Mỹ 2.2 (G5)**: ~733 MW CCGT BOT, commissioned 2005. Developed by **Mekong Energy Company** (EDF 56.25%, Sumitomo 28.125%, TEPCO 15.625%). Vietnam's first gas-fired BOT. 20-year PPA with EVN.
- **Phú Mỹ 3 (G6)**: ~733 MW CCGT BOT, commissioned 2004. Developed by **Phu My 3 BOT Power Company** (Sembcorp 66.7%, Kyushu Electric 13.3%, JBIC-related 20%). Another early BOT.
- **Phú Mỹ 4 (G7)**: ~450 MW CCGT, commissioned 2004–2005. EVNGENCO 3.

All plants use domestic gas from the Nam Côn Sơn basin via the NCS1 pipeline. As domestic gas production declines, these plants have been operating below capacity. The Thị Vải LNG terminal (commissioned 2023) is intended to supplement gas supply to the Phú Mỹ complex. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][20][41][42]

**G8/G9 — Nhơn Trạch TPP Complex (Gas)**
Located in Nhơn Trạch district, Đồng Nai province:
- **Nhơn Trạch 1 (G8)**: ~450 MW CCGT, commissioned 2008–2009. PV Power.
- **Nhơn Trạch 2 (G9)**: ~750 MW CCGT, commissioned 2011. PV Power.
Both use domestic gas from the Nam Côn Sơn pipeline system. The Nhơn Trạch site is also the location for the LNG-fired Nhơn Trạch 3&4 (see L1/L2). **Confidence: HIGH** [6][21]

**G10/G11 — Cà Mau TPP Complex**
Located in Cà Mau province (southernmost tip of Vietnam):
- **Cà Mau 1 (G10)**: ~750 MW CCGT, commissioned 2007–2008. PV Power.
- **Cà Mau 2 (G11)**: ~750 MW CCGT, commissioned 2008. PV Power.
Both use domestic gas from the PM3-CAA joint development area (Malaysia-Vietnam) via the PM3-Cà Mau pipeline. The gas supply contract with Petronas is a key commercial arrangement. As PM3-CAA gas declines, the plants face fuel supply uncertainty. **Confidence: HIGH** [6][21]

**G12/G13/G14/G15 — Ô Môn TPP Complex**
Located in Ô Môn district, Cần Thơ city:
- **Ô Môn 1 (G12)**: ~660 MW CCGT, commissioned in phases (OCGT 2009, CCGT conversion 2015). EVNGENCO 2. Currently uses gas from PM3-CAA pipeline.
- **Ô Môn 2 (G13)**: ~750 MW CCGT. Was assigned to **Vietracimex** (a private Vietnamese company) but development has stalled. The project is under review and may be reassigned. Intended to use gas from Block B (Ô Môn gas field). Expected COD has slipped repeatedly — now 2026–2028 at earliest.
- **Ô Môn 3 (G14)**: ~750 MW CCGT. To be developed by EVNGENCO 2. Intended to use Block B gas. Expected COD 2027–2028.
- **Ô Môn 4 (G15)**: ~750 MW CCGT. Supported by **JICA** (Japan) financing. Intended to use Block B gas. Expected COD 2028–2029.

The entire Ô Môn 2/3/4 expansion is contingent on the **Block B gas development**, which has been delayed for over a decade. Without Block B gas, these plants cannot operate as planned. PDP8 retains all three plants but acknowledges the Block B timeline risk. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (Ô Môn 1 operational — HIGH; Ô Môn 2/3/4 — MEDIUM due to Block B uncertainty). [6][7][8][19]

**G16 — Thủ Đức TPP**
An aging plant in Ho Chi Minh City, originally built in the 1960s–1970s. Various small gas turbine and steam units totaling approximately 160 MW. Operates primarily as peaking/reserve capacity. Uses a mix of gas and diesel. Likely candidate for retirement. **Confidence: LOW** (limited recent data). [6]

#### LNG PLANTS

**L1/L2 — Nhơn Trạch 3&4 LNG**
Located at the existing Nhơn Trạch site in Đồng Nai province. Vietnam's **first LNG-to-power project**:
- **Nhơn Trạch 3 (L1)**: ~750 MW CCGT
- **Nhơn Trạch 4 (L2)**: ~750 MW CCGT
Developer: **PV Power**. EPC by Samsung C&T and Lilama. LNG supplied from the **Thị Vải LNG terminal** (PV Gas) via pipeline. Construction began in 2021. Nhơn Trạch 3 was expected to achieve COD in late 2024 or early 2025, with Nhơn Trạch 4 following 6–12 months later. However, PPA negotiations with EVN have been protracted — the key issue is the gas price pass-through mechanism, as LNG costs are significantly higher than domestic gas. As of mid-2024, the PPA had not been finalized, creating uncertainty about commercial operations even as construction nears completion. **Confidence: HIGH** (construction progress well-documented; PPA uncertainty is the main risk). [7][21]

**L3 — Hiệp Phước LNG TPP**
Located in Nhà Bè district, Ho Chi Minh City. ~1,500 MW CCGT using imported LNG. The existing Hiệp Phước site has oil-fired peaking capacity (D3). The LNG conversion/expansion is included in PDP8. Developer arrangements are still being finalized. **Confidence: MEDIUM** [7][8][46]

**L4/L5 — Long An LNG TPP**
Located in Long An province (Mekong Delta, adjacent to HCMC):
- **Long An 1 (L4)**: ~1,500 MW CCGT. Various developers have been proposed, including **VinaCapital** and Singapore-based partners.
- **Long An 2 (L5)**: ~1,500 MW CCGT. Planned for later phase.
A dedicated LNG terminal would be required. The project is in early planning stages. **Confidence: LOW** (no FID, developer arrangements uncertain). [7][8]

**L6 — Bạc Liêu LNG TPP**
Located in Bạc Liêu province (Mekong Delta). ~3,200 MW CCGT (one of the largest proposed LNG plants in Vietnam). Developer: **Delta Offshore Energy (DOE)**, a Singapore-based company. DOE proposed an integrated LNG terminal + power plant using a floating storage regasification unit (FSRU). The project received an investment registration certificate in 2020 but has **stalled** due to:
- Inability to finalize PPA with EVN
- Questions about DOE's financial capacity
- Regulatory uncertainty around LNG pricing
- Competition from other LNG projects
As of 2024, the project's viability is in serious doubt. PDP8 includes it but with caveats. **Confidence: LOW** [7][43]

**L7/L8 — Sơn Mỹ LNG Complex**
Located in Hàm Tân district, Bình Thuận province. An integrated LNG terminal + power complex:
- **Sơn Mỹ LNG Terminal**: Originally a JV between **PV Gas** and **AES Corporation** (USA). AES divested its stake in 2023 as part of its global portfolio restructuring. PV Gas is now the lead developer.
- **Sơn Mỹ 1 (L7)**: ~2,250 MW CCGT. Developer restructuring underway.
- **Sơn Mỹ 2 (L8)**: ~2,250 MW CCGT. Originally developed by **EDF** (France), **Sojitz** (Japan), and **Kyushu Electric** (Japan). The consortium has faced difficulties with PPA negotiations.
The Sơn Mỹ complex is one of the most important LNG projects in PDP8 but faces significant delays. **Confidence: MEDIUM** [7][44]

**L9/L10 — Hải Lăng LNG Complex**
Located in Hải Lăng district, Quảng Trị province:
- **Phase 1 (L9)**: ~1,500 MW CCGT
- **Phase 2 (L10)**: ~1,500 MW CCGT
Developer: **T&T Group** (Vietnam) in partnership with **Hanwha Energy** (Korea) and **KOGAS** (Korea). An integrated LNG terminal is planned. The project has received provincial support and some preliminary approvals. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (early development stage; no FID). [7][45]

**L11 — Quảng Ninh LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Quảng Ninh province. Included in PDP8. Developer not yet finalized. PV Gas has expressed interest in developing the LNG terminal. **Confidence: LOW** (planning stage only). [7][8]

**L12 — Thái Bình LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Thái Bình province. Included in PDP8. Developer not yet finalized. **Confidence: LOW** [7][8]

**L13 — Nghi Sơn LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Thanh Hóa province (Nghi Sơn Economic Zone). Included in PDP8. Developer not yet finalized. **Confidence: LOW** [7][8]

**L14 — Quảng Trạch 2 LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Quảng Bình province. This is the **converted** Quảng Trạch 2 project (originally coal — see C47). EVNGENCO 1 is the likely developer. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (conversion confirmed by PDP8; implementation timeline uncertain). [7][8]

**L15 — Cà Ná LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Ninh Thuận province. Included in PDP8. Various developers have expressed interest. **Confidence: LOW** [7][8]

**L16 — Dung Quất LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Quảng Ngãi province (Dung Quất Economic Zone). Originally intended to use gas from the **Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale)** field, but given delays in that project, may use imported LNG. **Sembcorp** (Singapore) has been mentioned as a potential developer. **Confidence: LOW** (dependent on Cá Voi Xanh or alternative LNG supply). [7][8]

**L17 — Mỹ Giang (Vân Phong) LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Khánh Hòa province (near the Vân Phong deep-water port). Included in PDP8. **Confidence: LOW** [7][8]

**L18 — Chân Mây LNG TPP**
~1,500 MW CCGT planned for Thừa Thiên Huế province. Included in PDP8. **Confidence: LOW** [7][8]

**L19 — Vĩnh Tân 3 (Converted to LNG)**
Originally a 1,980 MW coal project (see C33). Under PDP8, converted to LNG-fired CCGT. Capacity may be adjusted (~1,500–2,000 MW). OneEnergy and Mitsubishi were the original developers; restructuring is underway. **Confidence: MEDIUM** [7][8]

## Concluding Notes

### Limitations and Caveats

1. **Completeness**: This inventory captures approximately 83 plant entries across ~55+ complexes. Some very small plants (industrial captive, provincial diesel generators) may be omitted. The inventory is believed to be substantially complete for utility-scale thermal plants ≥50 MW.

2. **Capacity figures**: Nameplate/gross capacity is used throughout. Net capacity is typically 3–7% lower for coal plants and 2–5% lower for CCGT plants. Some sources report different capacity figures for the same plant (e.g., Phú Mỹ 1 is variously reported as 1,090 MW, 1,100 MW, or 1,130 MW depending on the source and whether gross or net).

3. **Fuel classification**: Some plants classified as "domestic coal" may use a blend of domestic and imported coal, particularly northern plants that supplement domestic anthracite with imported sub-bituminous coal. The classification reflects the primary design fuel.

4. **LNG project pipeline**: The PDP8 LNG project list is highly aspirational. As of mid-2024, only Nhơn Trạch 3&4 is under construction. Most other LNG projects lack FID, finalized PPAs, or confirmed LNG supply contracts. The actual LNG capacity delivered by 2030 is likely to be significantly below the ~22.4 GW target.

5. **Source verification**: All source references are to documents and publications that are believed to exist based on the analyst's knowledge. However, specific URLs have not been verified and are therefore not provided. Readers should consult the Vietnamese government's official gazette (Công báo), EVN/PVN corporate websites, and financial market disclosures for primary verification.

6. **Data currency**: This inventory reflects information available through approximately mid-2024. The Vietnamese power sector is evolving rapidly, and project statuses may have changed since this document was prepared.
