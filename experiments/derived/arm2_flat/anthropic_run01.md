## REPORT — VIETNAM THERMAL GENERATION INVENTORY (>30 MWe)

*Compiled 25 May 2026 | All lifecycle stages | Nameplate MWe*

---

### METHODOLOGY & SCOPE

This inventory covers grid-connected thermal generation assets in Vietnam with nameplate capacity >30 MWe across all lifecycle stages (operating through cancelled/retired). Captive industrial plants (e.g., Formosa Hà Tĩnh steel complex) are excluded. Sources are ranked per the hierarchy in the operating rules; confidence is calibrated accordingly.

**Status vocabulary:** Operating · Commissioning · Construction · Pre-construction · Planned · Cancelled · Shelved · Retired · Mothballed

### ASSET-SPECIFIC NOTES

**A1 — Ninh Binh:** Vietnam's oldest coal plant (1974). Individual units (4×25 MW) each below the 30 MWe threshold; included because plant total is 100 MWe. Likely mothballed or operating at minimal load; no confirmed retirement order found.

**A2 — Uong Bi:** Original Units 1–6 (various small capacities from the 1960s–80s) are believed retired as of December 2023. Only Phase II (330 MW, COD ~2007) and Phase III (300 MW, COD ~2013) remain active. The FES study identifies Uong Bi as having passed 40 years of operation. PDP8 requires conversion to biomass/ammonia or closure by 2050.

**A3/A4 — Pha Lai 1 & 2:** The Pha Lai Power Complex will exceed 40 years within this decade. Pha Lai 1 (Soviet-built, 4×110 subcritical units) is among the least efficient in the fleet. Retirement/conversion likely before 2035.

**A7 — Cam Pha:** Attributed to TKV at 220 MW (2×110 subcritical). Capacity and unit configuration carry LOW confidence; some sources suggest a larger complex (~320 MW) including older Cửa Ông units. Older units, if extant, are likely below 30 MWe individually and may be retired.

**A14 — Thai Binh 2:** Major PVN project that experienced severe delays and cost overruns. PVN referenced Thai Binh 2 among "spectacular successes in implementing and completing key national energy projects." COD ~2022; exact date unverified — MED.

**A15 — Mong Duong 1:** Total capacity 1,080 MW comprised of 2 units of 540 MW each, using CFB technology. Operated by EVNGENCO1.

**A16 — Mong Duong 2:** Developed by AES-VCM Mong Duong Power Company Limited as a BOT project. The project is jointly developed by AES (51%), POSCO Energy (30%), and China Investment (19%). One of 7 foreign-owned BOT coal plants subject to the September 2025 MoF global minimum tax exemption proposal.

**A24 — Van Phong 1:** Vietnam's first supercritical coal plant, with an installed capacity of 1,432 MW (net 1,320 MW). COD January 2024. Sumitomo announced the transfer of 50% equity in VPCL, with AboitizPower acquiring a 25% stake for ~$220M.

**A26 — Duyen Hai 2:** 1,320 MW BOT plant owned by Teknik Janakuasa Sdn Bhd (100%), a subsidiary of Malakoff Corporation (Malaysia). Equipped with 2× Dongfang Electric 660 MW supercritical units. COD June 2021.

**A30 — Hai Duong BOT:** Unit 1 achieved COD on November 24, 2020; Unit 2 commenced operations in January 2021. JAKS Resources (Malaysia) and CEEC (China) are the BOT investors.

**A33 — Vung Ang 2:** One of 7 BOT coal plants listed for tax exemption. Mitsubishi/KEPCO/Chugoku EP consortium. Under active construction, targeting COD 2026–27.

**A34 — Long Phu 1:** Construction stalled after Russian EPC contractor Power Machines withdrew in 2018 due to US sanctions, when the project was over 70% complete. PVN is now targeting completion in 2027, with Unit 1 in Q2 and Unit 2 in Q3.

**A35 — Nam Dinh 1:** Joint venture between Taekwang Power (South Korea) and ACWA Power (Saudi Arabia), with anticipated operations by the late 2020s. NOT cancelled in PDP8; remains in pre-construction pipeline.

**B2–B6 — Phu My Complex:** Vietnam's largest gas-fired cluster (~4,017 MWe total). EVNGENCO3 (Phu My Thermal Power Company) owns 2,540 MW across Phu My 1, 2.1, and 4. Phu My 2.2 and 3 were BOT projects now transferred to EVN. Phu My 3 was officially handed over to EVN on March 1, 2024.

**C1/C2 — Nhon Trach 3 & 4:** Vietnam's first LNG-to-power project. PV Power started initial operation of the first 812 MW unit (Nhon Trach 3) in early 2025. Nhon Trach 4 (identical 812 MW H-class CCGT) under construction, expected to follow in 2025–26.

**C3 — O Mon IV:** 1,155 MW CCGT with Doosan Enerbility and PECC2 as EPC contractors. Target COD December 2028. Fed by Block B – Ô Môn gas pipeline.

**C6–C8 — Dung Quat I–III:** Linked to ExxonMobil's Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) gas field. Long-delayed due to gas field development timeline and commercial disputes. Total ~2,250 MW planned.

**C11 — Bac Lieu LNG:** At 3,200 MW, the largest single LNG project proposed. Delta Offshore Energy (Singapore/US) is the developer. Has faced persistent delays in commercial and regulatory approvals.

### CAVEATS & LIMITATIONS

- **Capacity figures**: Nameplate (gross) MWe used throughout. Net capacity is typically 3–8% lower for coal, 1–3% lower for gas CCGT. Where only net capacity was found, this is noted.
- **COD dates marked with ~**: Estimated from parametric knowledge or secondary sources; not verified against MOIT commissioning certificates.
- **Cam Pha (A7), Son Dong (A9), Hiep Phuoc (B12)**: LOW confidence on capacity and current status. May warrant field verification.
- **Cancelled coal projects (A36–A50)**: Capacities are approximate (typically 1,200 MW modules); several were never advanced beyond QĐ-level planning.
- **LNG pre-construction/planned (C4–C23)**: Many lack signed PPAs, FIDs, or confirmed investors. Delivery timelines are highly uncertain. The revised PDP8 targets ~22.5 GW of LNG by 2030, but industry consensus considers <10 GW achievable by that date.
- **Possible omissions**: Small TKV/Vinacomin plants (e.g., Cửa Ông, Nông Sơn 30 MW), older oil-fired peakers (Thủ Đức, Cần Thơ), and captive industrial power are excluded or listed at LOW confidence.
