### ASSET NOTES

**#1 Uong Bi** — Vietnam's oldest thermal plant. Multiple phases from 1963 (Soviet-built 4×12 MW) through expansions to ~630 MW. Uong Bi has "passed a lifespan of 40 years" and is a candidate for early retirement/repurposing. Exact current dispatchable capacity uncertain.

**#2 Pha Lai 1** — Pha Lai TPP 1 is the oldest of the thermal power plants, operated for approximately 40 years, with a capacity of 440 MW. Earmarked for coal transition under World Bank/JETP pathways.

**#4 Ninh Binh** — The World Bank shared initial research results for repurposing of Ninh Binh coal-fired power plant. Among the first candidates for coal-to-clean conversion.

**#5 Na Duong 1** — A two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002, and was completed in 2005.

**#12 Mong Duong 2** — Prior to the transaction, AES Mong Duong Power Company was jointly owned by AES (51%), Posco International (30%), and China Investment Corporation (19%). AES sold its stake in Dec 2020; current majority owner is a consortium.

**#16 Thai Binh 2** — Long-delayed PVN flagship. On 27-Apr-2023, PVN held a ceremony to inaugurate Thai Binh 2 with 2 units having a total capacity of 1,200 MW put into commercial operation.

**#18 Nghi Son 2** — The Nghi Son 2 plant in Thanh Hoa province has a capacity of 1,200 MW. BOT operated by Marubeni/KEPCO consortium.

**#20 Hai Duong BOT** — Hai Duong Thermal Power Plant is a 1,200 MW coal fired power project; China Power Engineering Consulting Group and JAKS Resources own 70% and 30% respectively.

**#29 Song Hau 1** — Plagued by cost overruns. PVN invested; the project was completed and put into commercial operation from May 6, 2022.

**#30 Van Phong 1** — In October 2023, Unit 1 was approved for commercial operation. In December 2023, Unit 2 was approved for commercial operation. The last new coal plant expected to reach COD in Vietnam under PDP8.

**#34 Long Phu 1** — Stalled at 78% completion after US sanctions on Russia's Power Machines. In October 2025, Qingda Environmental Protection disclosed it had signed an EPC contract for the project. Petrovietnam aims to restart the idling Long Phu 1 and complete it in 2027.

**#37–41 Shelved coal** — Quang Tri, Vinh Tan III, and Song Hau II have stopped or do not have any investor yet. These five "troubled" projects totalling ~7,220 MW are effectively moribund but not formally cancelled in PDP8.

**#42–52 Cancelled coal (PDP8)** — PDP8 cancelled 13,220 MW of coal-fired power including Quang Ninh III, Cam Pha III, Hai Phong III, Quynh Lap I & II, Vung Ang III, Quang Trach II, Long Phu II & III, Tan Phuoc I & II. Individual MW for each project are estimated at ~1,200 MW based on typical 2×600 configuration.

**#55–60 Phu My Complex** — Phu My 1 is a 1,118 MW capacity power plant built by MHI. The 468 MW Phu My 2-1 extension is an extension to the existing 477 MW Phu My 2.1 power plant. Phu My 3 is a 715 MW combined-cycle power plant. BOT concessions for Phu My 2.2 and 3 have been transferred back to EVN.

**#63–64 Ca Mau** — The Ca Mau Combined Cycle Power Plant is a 1,500 MW thermal project owned by PetroVietnam Power, commissioned in 2008.

**#67–68 Nhon Trach 3 & 4** — Vietnam's first LNG-to-power plants. Vietnam launched its first LNG-to-power project as the Prime Minister ordered inauguration of Nhon Trach 3 and 4 in Dong Nai on December 14. Commercial operation scheduled Aug 2025 (Unit 3) and Nov 2025 (Unit 4).

**#77 Thai Binh LNG** — Jointly invested by Tokyo Gas, Kyuden Group (Japan), and Truong Thanh Vietnam Group with respective shares of 40%, 30%, and 30%.

**#90 Bac Lieu LNG** — 3,200 MW proposed by Delta Offshore Energy (Singapore). Project viability is widely questioned; no confirmed FID or financing.
