## Per-asset explanatory notes

### Nhiệt điện Thái Bình II (Thai Binh II TPP)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence and status); MEDIUM (exact COD date).** The 1.2 GW plant was inaugurated by the Vietnamese Government and consists of two 600 MW supercritical coal-fired units operated by PetroVietnam. PetroVietnam started work in 2011 but could not finish until 2023, largely because of violations that had been brought to light. Work resumed since August 2018. Two units completed commissioning and began generating electricity, supplying over 1 billion kWh, but the development was eventful, including periods when the project came to a grinding halt. The plant is located next to the Thái Bình 1 TPP, comprised of two 300 MW subcritical anthracite units, commissioned in 2017 and 2018.

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (LNG CCGT)

**Confidence: HIGH.** With a total capacity of 1,624 MW, these are Vietnam's first power plants using imported LNG. The project consists of two gas-fired power plants using LNG with total capacity of 1,624 MW, located adjacent to the existing Nhon Trach 1 and 2 combined cycle power plants. Nhon Trach 3 had its completion acceptance approved in October 2025 and was granted COD status on November 20, 2025. On December 3, 2025, the Nhon Trach 4 Project completed state-level acceptance testing with COD expected December 20, 2025. The plants are equipped with GE's 50Hz, 9HA.02 gas turbines fuelled by LNG. Total capacity is 2 × 812 MW. The planned COD had repeatedly slipped from original 2020–2021 (PDP7A) to Q4-2024/Q2-2025 (Mott MacDonald report) to the actual late-2025 dates achieved.

### Vũng Áng II BOT (Coal, USC)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence and near-completion); MEDIUM (exact COD).** The project commenced construction in 2021, has a total investment of US$2.2 billion and comprises two units with a total capacity of over 1,200 MW. The plant utilizes ultra-supercritical (USC) boiler technology, considered the most advanced for coal-fired power plants in Vietnam. Overall project progress has reached 99.7% as of September 2025. Both generating units are targeted for operation in Q3 2025. PDP8 lists capacity as 1,330 MW. The BOT entity is VAPCO, now 100% owned by OneEnergy (Mitsubishi/Chugoku Electric/KEPCO consortium after CLP withdrawal in 2019).

### Quảng Trạch I (Coal, USC)

**Confidence: HIGH.** The 1.2 GW coal-fired power plant is under construction in Quảng Bình province, developed by EVN with an estimated investment of $1.78 billion. PetroVietnam was the previous developer, which broke ground in July 2011; the Government assigned the project to EVN in October 2016 due to slow implementation. The plant comprises two ultra-supercritical coal-fired units of 600 MW capacity each. The plant is expected to undergo a test run in September 2025 and become operational in 2026. PDP8 lists capacity at 1,403 MW (reflecting net/gross difference). In December 2025, a contract was awarded for the supply of imported coal for the trial run. The adjacent Quảng Trạch II has been converted from coal to LNG. For Quảng Trạch 2 LNG, the contract was signed February 9, 2026.

### Long Phú I (Coal)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence); LOW (COD).** This 2 × 600 MW plant in Sóc Trăng province has been under construction by PVN but has been effectively stalled since 2018. In 2013, Power Machines (Russia) signed the EPC contract; in 2018, a third of the way to completion, Power Machines could not continue because the US put them on an embargoed list, making it impossible to procure equipment. PVN did not accept the US sanction as force majeure, and Power Machines took PVN to Singapore International Arbitration. PDP8 still lists it under construction (1,200 MW, 2021–2030), and the implementation plan targets COD 2027, but this remains highly uncertain. Report 58 assessed possible 48-month delay "with potential for further slippage."

### Sông Hậu II BOT (Coal — Cancelled)

**Confidence: HIGH (cancellation).** Originally planned as a 2 × 1,000 MW coal BOT by Toyo Ink/Toyo Ventures (Malaysia). PDP8 Table 3 listed it as troubled (2,120 MW). From GEM wiki sources, the BOT contract was terminated by MOIT in July 2024 after years of financing difficulties. The developer filed a dispute notice in January 2024, and in April 2025 the lead arranger (EXIM Bank) withdrew.

### Vĩnh Tân III BOT (Coal — Shelved)

**Confidence: HIGH (shelved status).** Originally planned as a 3 × 660 MW coal BOT. PDP8 Table 3 lists 1,980 MW as troubled. Mitsubishi withdrew in February 2021, citing climate targets. From GEM wiki, Adani was reportedly planning a $2.8 billion investment in August 2024, but no construction has commenced. The project's future as coal remains deeply uncertain.

### Quảng Trị Coal TPP (Shelved/Announced)

**Confidence: LOW.** Originally planned as a 2 × 660 MW coal BOT by EGATi (Thailand). EGATi terminated the project in October 2022. As of February 2025, EVNGENCO 1 was seeking to invest in the cancelled project, though its preferred fuel was unclear. In March 2025, EVN stated that if they were the investor it would be developed using coal; if a different investor, it would be proposed using a different fuel. Status is ambiguous between cancelled-as-coal and announced-as-potentially-revived.

### Vân Phong I BOT (Coal)

**Confidence: HIGH.** Van Phong power station is an operating power station of at least 1,432 MW. Developed by Sumitomo under a BOT contract. PDP8 implementation plan lists Van Phong 1 as "in operation." Acceptance was reported in December 2023. In October 2025, Aboitiz Power agreed to buy a 25% stake from Sumitomo for about US$220 million. Capacity figure varies: PDP7A listed 2 × 660 MW; PDP8 lists 1,432 MW; GEM/E-542 state 2 × 716 MW. The 1,432 MW figure from PDP8 is adopted.

### Ô Môn Gas Power Center

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The Ô Môn complex is planned to house four generating units using gas from the Block B (Lô B) offshore field. Ô Môn I (660 MW) currently operates on fuel oil and is planned for conversion per PDP8 Table 5. Ô Môn II, III, and IV are new CCGT plants. Report 58 notes that Ô Môn III is delayed by 5 years and Ô Môn IV by 3 years, both synchronized with the Lô B gas field development schedule. PDP8 increases capacity of new units from 750 MW (PDP7A) to 1,050 MW each. All depend on successful development of the Block B gas pipeline, which remains uncertain.

### Mông Dương II BOT (Coal)

**Confidence: HIGH.** The 1,240 MW coal-fired thermal power plant was developed by AES-VCM Mong Duong Power Company, a joint venture between AES Corporation (51%), Posco Energy (30%) and CIC subsidiary (19%). In April 2015, the plant entered full commercial operation, approximately six months ahead of schedule. Constructed on a BOT basis, the plant comprises two generating units of 620 MW gross capacity each. Vietnam's first major coal BOT project.

### Duyên Hải Power Complex

**Confidence: HIGH.** The Duyen Hai complex is an operating power station of at least 4,378 MW in Trà Vinh. It comprises: Duyên Hải I (1,245 MW, GENCO 1), Duyên Hải III (1,245 MW, GENCO 1), Duyên Hải III Extension (660 MW, EVN), and Duyên Hải II BOT (1,200 MW, Janakuasa). As of February 2025, the complex reportedly employed over 1,000 local workers and supplied 116 billion kWh of power to the national grid over ten years.

### LNG Thái Bình

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The investor consortium comprises Tokyo Gas, Kyuden International, and Truong Thanh Group JSC, with an investment of approximately $2 billion. Construction was required to kick off in September 2025. PDP8 Table 1 lists 1,500 MW for 2021–2030. This is a new LNG project distinct from the existing Thai Binh coal power center.
