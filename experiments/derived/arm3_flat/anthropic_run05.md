The inventory contains approximately 90 discrete asset records spanning the full lifecycle of Vietnam's thermal generation fleet. In aggregate, the operating coal fleet totals roughly 25,000–27,000 MWe (consistent with the approximately 28,000 MW reported at end-2025 by Vietnamese media, which includes some plants not individually confirmed here such as smaller TKV units). By the end of 2025, the country's total installed power capacity had reached about 88,000 MW, with coal-fired power accounting for nearly 28,000 MW, or more than 30 per cent of the total. The operating domestic-gas CCGT fleet totals approximately 7,500–8,000 MWe (Phú Mỹ complex, Nhơn Trạch 1-2, Cà Mau 1-2, Bà Rịa), and with a total capacity of 1,624 MW, the Nhơn Trạch 3 and Nhơn Trạch 4 power plants added Vietnam's first LNG capacity in late 2025. Under PDP8, approximately 22,400 MW of LNG-fired capacity and roughly 7,600 MW of domestic-gas CCGT capacity are planned for 2021–2030, though many projects lack confirmed developers or depend on upstream gas-field timelines (Lô B, Cá Voi Xanh, Báo Vàng). Regarding coal power, by 2030, the total capacity of operating plants and projects under construction is expected to be completed and put into operation at about 31,055 MW, with five coal projects (4,360 MW) to be urgently completed and 3 projects/5,300 MW (Nam Dinh I, Song Hau II, Vinh Tan 3) facing difficulties in arranging capital and changing investor structure. By 2050, the orientation is to no longer use coal for electricity generation, completely switching to using biomass/ammonia.

## Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (LNG CCGT, 2 × 812 MW, Đồng Nai)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence, status, capacity, COD).** Vietnam's first LNG-fired power complex. Invested by Vietnam Oil and Gas Power Corporation (PV Power), a member unit of Petrovietnam, with the Lilama–Samsung C&T consortium as the EPC general contractor. The Project consists of two gas-fired power plants using LNG with total capacity of 1624MW. The plants will be equipped with GE's 50Hz, 9HA.02 gas turbines fuelled by LNG. Construction began in 2022 after PM investment approval in 2019. Nhon Trach 3 Power Plant had its completion acceptance approved by the State Council in October 2025 and was granted COD status on November 20, 2025. On December 3, 2025, the Nhon Trach 4 Project completed state-level acceptance testing and is expected to complete the COD certification on December 20, 2025. PDP7A had planned the Nhơn Trạch 3&4 complex (as 2×750 MW) for 2020–2021; actual COD was delayed roughly 4–5 years.

### Thái Bình II (Coal, 2 × 600 MW, Thái Bình)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence, status); MEDIUM (technology).** The Thái Bình 2 thermal power plant, operated by PetroVietnam, consists in two 600 MW supercritical coal-fired units. The plant is located next to the Thái Bình 1 thermal power plant, comprised of two 300 MW subcritical anthracite units, commissioned in 2017 and 2018. PDP7A had planned COD 2017–2018 for Thái Bình II. The project was severely delayed due to legal/corruption issues and the COVID-19 pandemic. The project's development was eventful, especially at times when the project came to a grinding halt as past issues led to challenges in legal and funding matters. Inaugurated April 2023.

### Long Phú I (Coal, 2 × 600 MW, Sóc Trăng)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence, capacity); LOW (COD, status).** Long Phu 1 is a 1.2GW supercritical thermal power plant being developed in the Long Phu district of Soc Trang Province. EPC by consortium of Power Machines (Russia), BTG Holding (Slovakia), and PTSC. The project is expected to face a delay of 36 months compared with original plans after the US Treasury put Power Machines in an embargo list. Construction was approximately 78% complete as of 2020 per E-542 study, but has been largely suspended since. As of March 2024, the proposed loan financing arrangements appeared to not be going ahead. As of March 2025, there was no further news on financing the project. PDP8 revised lists COD 2027 but this remains highly uncertain.

### Vũng Áng II (Coal, 2 × 665 MW, Hà Tĩnh)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence, construction status); MEDIUM (COD).** BOT project. The Vung Ang II Thermal Power Plant (VAPCO) project, which commenced construction in 2021, has a total investment of US$2.2 billion and comprises two units with a total capacity of over 1,200 MW. The Vung Ang II Thermal Power Plant utilizes ultra-supercritical (USC) boiler technology – considered by experts to be the most advanced technology for coal-fired power plants in Vietnam. PDP8 lists 1,330 MW (nameplate higher than original 2×600 plan). PDP8 revised lists COD 2025–2026. Included in MoF September 2025 BOT tax exemption proposal, suggesting advanced commissioning.

### Quảng Trạch I (Coal, 2 × ~700 MW, Quảng Bình)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence, construction); MEDIUM (capacity, COD).** Originally planned as 2×600 MW under PVN (PDP7A). Transferred to EVN. PDP8 lists capacity as 1,403 MW—higher than earlier 1,200 MW plans, suggesting uprated turbines. As of February 2024, construction was expected to be 61% complete by the end of the year. As of March 2024, Unit 1 was expected to begin operating in May 2026, and Unit 2 in October 2026. Report 32 estimated COD 2024 (delayed 2.5 years from PDP7A plan); further delays have pushed to 2026.

### Vân Phong I (Coal BOT, 2 × 716 MW, Khánh Hòa)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (status, COD).** Sumitomo BOT project. PDP8 lists 1,432 MW under construction. E-542 study (Nov 2020) stated PPA signed, with COD estimated mid-2023 + early 2024. PDP8 revised lists COD 2024. Included in MoF September 2025 BOT tax exemption proposal alongside confirmed-operating BOTs (Nghi Sơn II, Mông Dương II, etc.), strongly implying the plant is now operating. Technology likely USC (modern large-scale BOT) but not confirmed in primary sources.

### Sông Hậu II (Coal BOT, 2 × 1,000+ MW, Hậu Giang)

**Confidence: HIGH (cancelled status).** Malaysian developer Toyo Ink/Toyo Ventures held the BOT contract. PDP8 Table 3 listed it as delayed (2,120 MW). In January 2025, Toyo Ventures officially disputed MOIT's contract termination. The BOT contract was terminated by MOIT in July 2024. The project's future is unclear amid ongoing legal dispute.

### Vĩnh Tân III (Coal BOT, 3 × 660 MW, Bình Thuận)

**Confidence: HIGH (shelved status).** Originally a BOT with VTEC (consortium including Mitsubishi, One Energy). In February 2021 Mitsubishi announced that it was withdrawing from Vinh Tan-3, the first time Mitsubishi has withdrawn from a coal plant project. Mitsubishi cited its climate targets as the main reason. PDP8 Table 3 lists it among delayed/troubled projects. PDP8 revised retains it among projects "facing difficulties in arranging capital."

### Ô Môn Complex (Gas CCGT, Cần Thơ)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (planned status); LOW (COD).** The Ô Môn power center is planned to host four gas-fired units using domestic gas from the Lô B offshore field (Block B, Ô Môn gas pipeline). Ô Môn I (660 MW, existing oil-fired) is to convert to gas; Ô Môn II, III, IV (each 1,050 MW CCGT per PDP8) are new-build. All timelines depend on the Lô B gas field development. Reports 32 and 58 note 3–5 year delays, with synchronization to Lô B as the binding constraint. PDP8 Table 5 lists all four as planned for 2021–2030 but without specific COD dates.

### Dung Quất / Miền Trung Complex (Domestic Gas CCGT, Quảng Ngãi / Quảng Trị)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (planned status); LOW (COD).** Five CCGT plants (Dung Quất I-III, Miền Trung I-II, each 750 MW per PDP8 Table 5) are planned to use gas from the Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) offshore field operated by ExxonMobil. All are contingent on the CVX field reaching final investment decision and gas delivery. Report 58 estimated COD 2024–2026 for various units but noted "đồng bộ khí CVX" (synchronized with CVX gas) as the key constraint.

### LNG Bạc Liêu (3,200 MW, Bạc Liêu)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence in plan); LOW (implementation feasibility).** PDP8 Table 1 lists 3,200 MW for 2021–2030. The project was first added to the revised PDP7 as a large LNG scheme, with pre-feasibility underway as of 2020 per E-542. No confirmed developer or EPC contract in primary sources.
