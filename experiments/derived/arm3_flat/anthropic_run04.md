## Per-asset explanatory notes

### NĐ Uông Bí (original plant)

**Existence/status: HIGH.** This Soviet-era plant (commissioned 1960s-1970s) was listed at 105 MW in both the EVN 2011 Annual Report and the EVN 2017 Annual Report under GENCO 1. It is absent from the EVN 2018 Annual Report, which lists only Extensions 1 (300 MW) and 2 (330 MW) for GENCO 1 at Uông Bí, implying retirement between 2017 and 2018. PDP7 Annex 2 had planned retirement for 2021. Status recorded as Retired ~2017-2018 based on its disappearance from EVN's roster.

### NĐ Thái Bình II

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** One of Vietnam's most delayed coal projects. Report 58 states: "Chậm tiến độ 3 năm và vẫn còn rủi ro chậm tiếp" (delayed 3 years with risk of further delay). Originally planned 2017-2018 per PDP7A, later reports estimated 2020-2021. Report 32 assessed: "Chưa xác định tiến độ hoàn thành" as of its date. PVN eventually completed both 600 MW units in 2022-2023 per industry press. It uses domestic coal (subcritical technology). Capacity 2×600 = 1,200 MW per both Report 32 and Report 58. Status is capped at MEDIUM because the COD is attested only by secondary trade press and not by an EVN or PVN annual report in the evidence pack.

### NĐ Vũng Áng II (BOT)

**Existence/status: HIGH.** The project "commenced construction in 2021, has a total investment of US$2.2 billion and comprises two units with a total capacity of over 1,200 MW." It "utilizes ultra-supercritical (USC) boiler technology – considered by experts to be the most advanced technology for coal-fired power plants in Vietnam." Unit 1, "with a designed capacity of approximately 600MW, officially commenced commercial operation at 0:00 on July 22, 2025." Unit 2 "is in the testing phase" and is "expected to be completed and put into commercial operation in the near future, aiming for the entire plant to generate electricity by the third quarter of 2025." Developer is VAPCO (Vung Ang II Thermal Power Company Limited), with Mitsubishi Corporation (40%) and Chugoku Electric Power Co. as principal shareholders. PDP8 Table 2 records 1,330 MW (slightly higher than 2×600 cited elsewhere). The capacity figure discrepancy (1,200 vs 1,330 MW) likely reflects nameplate vs gross rating; PDP8's 1,330 MW is used as the official planning capacity.

### NĐ Quảng Trạch I

**Existence/status: HIGH.** The plant "commenced construction in December 2021" and "comprises two generating units with a total installed capacity of 1,403 MW, utilizing supercritical fluid (USC) technology." The capacity of 1,403 MW from PDP8 Table 2 is higher than the 2×600 = 1,200 MW in earlier PDP7A documents, reflecting the actual turbine sizing. As of mid-2026, "The Quảng Trạch I thermal power plant is expected to connect its first unit to the national grid in April 2026 and begin commercial operation in May 2026." By February 2026, "the reverse power input for Unit 2 was successfully completed." Previously developed by PVN, transferred to EVN in October 2016 due to slow progress. EPC by CC1-Mitsubishi-Hyundai consortium. Note: sources alternate between describing the technology as "supercritical" and "USC"; treated as supercritical in the table pending clarification.

### NĐ Long Phú I

**Existence/status: LOW.** Long Phú-1 is "a proposed 'high efficiency' 1,200-MW coal-fired power station" by PVN. Construction was ~78% complete but suspended after the primary EPC contractor (Russia's Power Machines) was sanctioned by the US. "The proposed loan financing arrangements appeared to not be going ahead. As of March 2025, there was no further news on financing the project." Status is recorded as Construction (work paused) with confidence LOW for completion, as there is no clear path forward and the COD is highly uncertain.

### NĐ Quảng Trị I (coal BOT)

**Existence/status: LOW.** "In October 2022, the Thai Ministry of Energy announced that the project was cancelled following nearly a decade of delays." However, "in March 2025, the People's Committee of Quang Tri had a working session to discuss the revival of Quảng Trị-1. EVN stated that if they were the investor in the project, it would be developed using coal." "In June 2025, Quang Tri Provincial People's Committee proposed that MOIT formally request that EVN be assigned to the implementation of Quảng Trị-1." The project is in flux—previously cancelled, now being revived with potentially different fuel and developer. Status recorded as Announced (revived) with LOW confidence due to the uncertain investor, fuel choice, and regulatory approval.

### TBKHH Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4

**Existence/status: HIGH.** "On December 14, 2025, at Ong Keo Industrial Park, Dai Phuoc Commune, Dong Nai Province," the inauguration ceremony was held. "With a total designed capacity of 1,624 MW, the plants are expected to supply more than 9 billion kWh annually once operating stably." "The project is invested with cutting-edge technology, using GE's 9HA.02 gas turbines (USA)." "The plants achieve an efficiency of 62–64%, among the highest globally." Each unit is approximately 812 MW. The capacity split between NT3 and NT4 is based on the total 1,624 MW divided equally; exact per-unit nameplate not independently confirmed. Vietnam's first LNG-to-power project, supplied via Thị Vải LNG terminal (PV GAS).

### Ô Môn Power Center (Ô Môn I–IV)

Ô Môn is a power center in Cần Thơ with four planned phases. Ô Môn I is currently operating at 660 MW, listed under GENCO 2 as "Oil fired (FO)" in EVN 2018 AR. It was originally 330 MW per EVN 2011 AR, then expanded to 660 MW. PDP8 Table 5 plans to convert Ô Môn I to Lô B domestic gas. Ô Môn II (1,050 MW), III (1,050 MW), and IV (1,050 MW) are all planned CCGT plants using Lô B gas, but all are substantially delayed due to the Lô B gas pipeline development schedule. Report 58 notes Ô Môn III is "Chậm 5 năm" (5 years late) and Ô Môn IV is "Chậm 3 năm" (3 years late), both "đồng bộ tiến độ khí lô B" (synchronized with Lô B gas timeline). PDP8 Table 5 lists Ô Môn II at 1,050 MW (different from PDP7A's 750 MW), reflecting revised turbine specifications.

### LNG Bạc Liêu

**Status: MEDIUM.** PDP8 Table 1 lists LNG Bạc Liêu at 3,200 MW for the 2021-2030 period. The project is backed by Delta Offshore Energy (US). It has been in pre-feasibility stage for several years. At 3,200 MW, it would be one of Vietnam's largest single LNG projects. Progress has been slow, and the project's implementation timeline remains uncertain.

### NĐ Sông Hậu II and NĐ Vĩnh Tân III

Both are coal BOT projects listed in PDP8 Table 3 as projects facing financing and shareholder difficulties: Sông Hậu II at 2,120 MW and Vĩnh Tân III at 1,980 MW. The Revised PDP8 (April 2025) continues to list these among "3 projects/5,300 MW" (together with Nam Định I) that MOIT must negotiate with investors. "For 3 projects /5,300 MW (Nam Dinh I, Song Hau II, Vinh Tan 3)...facing difficulties in arranging capital and changing investor structure." Neither has started construction. For Vĩnh Tân III, CLP Holdings withdrew from coal in 2019. Both are effectively shelved pending resolution.

### Domestic gas projects (Cá Voi Xanh–dependent)

The Dung Quất I-III and Miền Trung I-II CCGT projects (each 750 MW) are all dependent on the development of the Cá Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) offshore gas field by ExxonMobil. PDP8 Table 5 confirms Dung Quất III uses "khí Cá Voi Xanh." All five projects have been delayed in sync with the gas field's development timeline. The Revised PDP8 continues to include them for the 2021-2030 period.

*Aggregate statistics (for reference, not a separate table):* The inventory records approximately 85 thermal asset entries across all lifecycle stages. Operating coal-fired capacity totals roughly 25,000–26,000 MWe across ~30 plants/plant-groups, with an additional ~4,700 MWe under construction (Vũng Áng II at 1,330 MW now partially commissioned, Quảng Trạch I at 1,403 MW, An Khánh–Bắc Giang at 650 MW, Na Dương II at 110 MW, Long Phú I at 1,200 MW stalled). Operating gas-fired capacity (domestic gas) totals roughly 7,500–8,000 MWe across ~12 plants, with the newly commissioned Nhơn Trạch 3&4 LNG complex adding 1,624 MWe as Vietnam's first imported-LNG capacity. PDP8 and the Revised PDP8 plan approximately 22,000–24,000 MWe of new LNG-fired CCGT capacity and 10,000–15,000 MWe of new domestic-gas CCGT capacity through 2030-2035, though nearly all projects are at Announced or Pre-permit stage. Roughly 7,000+ MWe of planned coal capacity from PDP7/PDP7A has been cancelled or shelved in PDP8, reflecting Vietnam's shift away from new coal investment. These aggregates are approximate, reflecting the inherent uncertainty in planned-project capacities and the varying nameplate figures reported across sources.
