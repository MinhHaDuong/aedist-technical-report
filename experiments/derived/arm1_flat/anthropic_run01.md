## Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

### Uông Bí (Uong Bi) Thermal Power Station

**Confidence: LOW (existence/operational status).** Vietnam's oldest power station (Uong Bi) has passed a lifespan of 40 years already, implying original commissioning before 1983. The station has undergone multiple expansions since the 1960s. The inventory row covers only the modern 300 MW extension (COD ~2014); original Soviet-era small units (12.5–50 MW) are presumed retired but no admissible source confirms retirement. The Green FDC analysis [20] references the station's age in the context of PDP8 decommissioning rules (40-year operational limit). **Source gap:** No admissible source confirms the extension's exact capacity or COD; values drawn from parametric knowledge (lead: Wikipedia).

### Phả Lại 1 (Pha Lai 1)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence), LOW (capacity).** The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade. This Soviet-built station (4 × 110 MW subcritical units, using domestically mined coal) is among Vietnam's oldest operational coal assets. Under PDP8 rules, it faces conversion or retirement after 40 years, putting a deadline around 2023–2026. The 440 MW total and unit configuration are from parametric knowledge; no admissible primary source was located for individual unit specifications.

### Na Dương 1 & 2 (Na Duong 1 & 2)

**Confidence: HIGH.** Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002, and was completed in 2005. The mine-mouth plant uses CFB technology. As of August 2024, electricity generation at Na Dương-1 was reportedly declining due to limited coal supply. As of October 2025, a biomass co-firing test was being conducted. Na Dương 2 (1 × 110 MW) received EPC contract in December 2023 and Vietcombank signed a credit contract to finance the Na Duong power plant for 2.63 billion VND in November 2024. The Na Dương mine's bank erosion poses long-term supply risks for both plants.

### Mông Dương 2 (Mong Duong 2)

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** In 2015, AES commissioned the 1,240 MW Mong Duong 2 coal-fired power plant in the Quang Ninh province. This is a BOT concession using circulating fluidized bed (CFB) technology, one of the largest CFB plants in the world. It was included in the Ministry of Finance's September 2025 proposal to exempt seven BOT coal plants from the OECD 15% global minimum tax, alongside Vĩnh Tân 1, Nghi Sơn 2, Vân Phong 1, Vũng Áng 2, Hải Dương, and Duyên Hải 2.

### Vũng Áng 2 (Vung Ang 2)

**Confidence: HIGH (existence/status), MEDIUM (USC technology).** A 1,200 MW BOT project being developed by a consortium of Mitsubishi's Diamond Generating Asia (40%), KEPCO (40%), and Chugoku Electric (20%). The GEM wiki cites a Project Finance International March 2019 report characterizing the project as "ultrasupercritical." Located in Ha Tinh province, it is designed to complement the existing Vung Ang 1 plant. Construction was confirmed accelerating as of November 2023. The project attracted significant NGO opposition and multiple banks withdrew financing between 2019 and 2021.

### Vân Phong 1 (Van Phong 1)

**Confidence: HIGH.** GEM lists Van Phong power station as an operating power station of at least 1,432 megawatts in Khánh Hòa. The design nameplate is 2 × 660 MW = 1,320 MW; the higher GEM figure likely reflects gross capacity including auxiliaries. Developed by Sumitomo Corporation under a BOT arrangement; with a capacity of 1,320 MW, this plant is one of the largest foreign-invested coal power projects in the country. Per GEM wiki references, Sumitomo transferred 50% of its stake in May 2025 and maintenance services were carried out in 2024, confirming operational status.

### Long Phú 1 (Long Phu 1)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence/construction status), LOW (COD).** This 2 × 600 MW plant in Sóc Trăng was originally contracted to Russia's Power Machines for EPC. US and EU sanctions following the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine severely disrupted procurement and construction. The Frasers Revised PDP8 analysis [23] lists it among behind-schedule coal projects still in construction. No admissible source confirms a firm revised COD; estimates range from 2026 to 2028.

### Công Thanh (Cong Thanh) — Coal-to-LNG Conversion

**Confidence: HIGH (coal cancellation); MEDIUM (LNG conversion).** In March 2011, land clearing began on the two-unit, 600-MW, $620 million Cong Thanh power station in the Nghi Son Economic Zone. Construction never substantively started. Cong Thanh is particularly noticeable. Originally planned as a coal-fired power plant, the project was approved for conversion to LNG with an expectation for early implementation during 2026–2030. This precedent-setting conversion from coal to LNG reflects the government's commitment under the Amended PDP8.

### Nam Định 1 (Nam Dinh 1) — Coal-to-LNG Conversion

**Confidence: HIGH (coal shelving); MEDIUM (LNG conversion).** Originally a 2 × 600 MW coal BOT project developed by Taekwang Power Holdings, with Saudi ACWA Power as co-investor. According to reports from May 2023, ACWA Power had withdrawn from the project. In November 2023, the power station proposal was described as "at risk" of termination. The proposed co-investment between Gulf Group and Taekwang Power would reportedly be favorable, as proactively pivoting to a gas-fired power station would be more attractive. The coal project is now effectively cancelled, with an LNG successor under discussion.

### Quảng Trị (Quang Tri) Thermal Power

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** A 2 × 600 MW coal project developed by Thailand's EGAT International (EGATi). The investor has applied for suspension of the project (according to Document EGATi 277/2023). PDP8 listed it among projects that "can no longer be developed" and excluded its capacity from 2030 coal totals. The project is classified as Shelved; a formal cancellation has not been confirmed from admissible sources.

### Phú Mỹ Complex (Phu My Power Centre)

**Confidence: HIGH (complex), MEDIUM–HIGH (individual plants).** The Phu My power complex is the largest group of power generation plants in Vietnam, with a combined capacity of up to 3,900 MW. It is located in the village of Phu My in the Ba Ria-Vung Tau province. The complex includes the Phu My 1, 2-1, 2-2, 3 and 4 plants. Phú Mỹ 2-2 (BOT, Mekong Energy/EDF consortium, 715 MW) and Phú Mỹ 3 (BOT, SembCorp consortium, 746 MW) were Vietnam's first foreign-invested IPPs. All power plants are fuelled by natural gas… Gas is supplied from Bạch Hổ oil field and Nam Con Son Basin's gas fields. The complex's capacity may be affected as domestic gas fields decline; transition to LNG or decommissioning will be a key future issue. Individual plant capacities for PM1 (1,090 MW), PM2-1 (900 MW), and PM4 (450 MW) are widely reported but sourced only from inadmissible compilations; combined totals are confirmed by admissible sources at ~3,859–3,900 MW ([8], [18]).

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Nhon Trach 3 & 4)

**Confidence: HIGH.** PetroVietnam Power Corporation's Nhon Trach 3 and 4 power plants have officially entered commercial operation. Located at Ong Keo Industrial Park in Dai Phuoc Commune, the twin plants represent Vietnam's first foray into LNG-fired electricity generation. With a combined installed capacity of 1.6 GW… Delivered by an EPC consortium comprising Samsung C&T and Vietnamese contractor Lilama, the project deploys GE Vernova's HA-class gas turbine technology. Earlier planning documents (2020) cited 1,760 MW combined; the 1.6 GW figure from GE Vernova's 2025 announcement likely reflects net capacity or updated specifications. LNG supplied from PV Gas's Thị Vải regasification terminal in Bà Rịa – Vũng Tàu. Nhon Trach 3 was connected to the national grid on February 5 [2025], with commercial operations scheduled in August (NT3) and November (NT4).

### Sơn Mỹ 2 (Son My 2)

**Confidence: HIGH (investment policy approval), MEDIUM (COD).** The Vietnamese government has approved the investment policy of the 2,250 MW Son My 2 CCGT power plant project. The power plant, developed by AES Vietnam, is estimated to require an investment of US$1.8 bn. Construction was planned to begin in 2023 and commissioning is scheduled for 2025. However, as of 2025–2026 these timelines have slipped considerably. Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for domestic gas and LNG-fired power capacity by a combined 25.2 GW per IEEFA [16], reflecting broader delays across LNG projects including turbine procurement bottlenecks.

### Bạc Liêu LNG (Bac Lieu LNG)

**Confidence: LOW.** Proposed by Singapore-based Delta Offshore Energy at 3,200 MW, this would be one of the largest single LNG power complexes in Southeast Asia. The Government has agreed to supplement Bac Lieu LNG CCGT project with a capacity of 3,200 MW as of 2020 planning documents, at an electricity price of 7 UScents/kWh. However, the project has faced persistent questions about developer capacity, bankability, and gas turbine procurement. IEEFA's 2025 analysis of turbine shortages [16] casts doubt on whether any LNG project without a disclosed turbine contract can meet 2030 deadlines. The project's status remains Announced with no confirmed progress toward construction.

## Aggregate Statistics

As of early 2026, the inventory contains approximately 75 distinct thermal asset records across all lifecycle stages. At the end of September 2022, Vietnam had 39 coal-fired power plants with a total capacity of 24,674 MW in operation, per MOIT data cited in a Baker McKenzie analysis. As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction. The operating coal plants in this inventory total approximately 25,500–26,000 MWe (including plants commissioned 2022–2024 such as Nghi Sơn 2, Thái Bình 2, Hải Dương, and Vân Phong 1), broadly consistent with these benchmarks plus recent additions.

For gas-fired generation, the existing domestic-gas fleet (Phú Mỹ complex, Bà Rịa, Cà Mau 1 & 2, Nhơn Trạch 1 & 2, Ô Môn 1) totals approximately 7,800–8,000 MWe. The first two LNG-fueled plants (Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4, ~1,600–1,760 MW combined) entered commercial operation in 2025. The thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas, LNG, and coal with a total capacity of 14,930 MW, 22,400 MW, and 30,127 MW, respectively, per the PDP8 Implementation Plan. Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for domestic gas and LNG-fired power capacity by a combined 25.2 GW. Two projects have disclosed turbine contracts but still face other significant barriers to financial closure, per IEEFA's October 2025 assessment.

A list of backup LNG thermal power plants with a combined capacity of 7,500 MW that are scheduled to be developed between 2031 and 2035 is also included in the Revised PDP8. The Amended PDP8 (Decision 768/QĐ-TTg, April 2025) also sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050, with a total converted capacity of 25,798 MW.

Several coal projects totaling approximately 6,240 MW have been shelved or cancelled (Vĩnh Tân 3, Quảng Trị, Sông Hậu 2, Công Thanh, Nam Định 1, Vũng Áng 3, Vân Phong 2), with two of these (Công Thanh and Nam Định 1) approved for conversion to LNG. Coal-fired power plants which can no longer be developed will be replaced by LNG or RE projects.

**Known gaps:** This inventory likely omits several smaller coal plants (< 100 MW) operated by TKV and industrial captive plants (e.g., Formosa Hà Tĩnh steel complex), older OCGT units in Hồ Chí Minh City (Thủ Đức), and early-stage LNG projects not yet appearing in official planning annexes. Most capacity figures are nameplate electrical output as commonly reported; gross vs. net is generally unspecified in Vietnamese energy planning documents.
