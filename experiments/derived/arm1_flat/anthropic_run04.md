## Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

### Công Thanh (Cong Thanh) – Thanh Hóa – 600 MW coal – Shelved

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence and status); LOW (future disposition).**

In March 2011, land clearing began on the two-unit, 600-MW, $620 million Cong Thanh power station in the Nghi Son Economic Zone. As of May 2015, construction was apparently still not underway, being held up for unspecified reasons. The massive Nghi Son Refinery & Petrochemical Complex was built at the location. Under PDP8, the project was proposed for conversion from coal to LNG. The coal project is effectively shelved, with the site's original purpose superseded. GEM wiki (Jan 2026) classifies units as pre-permit/cancelled. The Thanh Hoa Provincial People's Committee has requested conversion to an LNG plant, but no formal approval has been issued.

### Duyên Hải power center – Trà Vinh – multiple plants

**Confidence: HIGH.**

The Duyen Hai center co-locates four distinct plants. Duyen Hai Power Plant I & III is a 2,488-MW coal-fired facility in Tra Vinh that was commissioned in 2016. This figure represents the combined capacity of DH1 (~1,245 MW, 2 units) and DH3 (~1,243 MW, 2 units), both operated by EVNGENCO 1 with slightly different CODs (2015–2016 for DH1; 2017 for DH3). DH3 Extension (MR) added a single ~688 MW supercritical unit (COD 2019). Duyen Hai Power Plant II is a 1,320-MW coal-fired facility that came online in 2021, operated by Teknik Janakuasa under a BOT arrangement.

### Long Phú 1 – Sóc Trăng – 1,200 MW coal – Construction (stalled)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence); LOW (schedule).**

Long Phu 1 (2 × 600 MW subcritical coal) is an EVNGENCO 3 project that has been severely delayed. The original EPC contractor, Power Machines (Russia), was affected by international sanctions, disrupting equipment supply and construction. The plant is listed in PDP8 (2023) as under construction. No unit has been commissioned as of early 2026. The expected COD has slipped repeatedly; current estimates suggest 2025–2027, but on-the-ground progress has been minimal by many accounts. Confidence on the COD is LOW.

### Na Dương 2 – Lạng Sơn – 100 MW coal – Construction

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

In April 2021 a consortium formed by Dongfang Electric and Vietnam Baiteng Construction Co., Ltd. signed a contract with Vinacomin to build the plant. The September 2021 draft of PDP8 gives a commissioning date of 2021–2025. The project's feasibility study had been approved and capital arranged, however a contractor had not yet been selected as of mid-2022. As a CFB mine-mouth plant using Na Duong lignite, the project is small but strategically tied to Vinacomin's coal-mining operations. Construction progress has been slow.

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 – Đồng Nai – 1,624 MW LNG – Operating (2025)

**Confidence: HIGH.**

Vietnam's first purpose-built LNG-to-power complex. The "first fire" ceremony for Nhon Trach 3 occurred on January 11, 2025. Together, the two facilities comprise a total production capacity of 1,624 MW, providing roughly 8 billion kWh of electricity annually. The price tag for the complex is about $1.4 billion, with international banks Citi and ING leading the financing. The units use GE Vernova equipment. Nhon Trach 3 was slated for commercial operation in Q3 2025, with Nhon Trach 4 expected by end-2025. LNG is supplied from the Thi Vai terminal. When operational, the two plants will absorb all the LNG supply from Thi Vai. Capacity per unit: each with a capacity of 750 MW per S&P Global (Sep 2024), though the total of 1,624 MW implies ~812 MW each. The discrepancy likely reflects different rating conventions (ISO vs site conditions). I adopt the 1,624 MW total from the more recent IntelliNews/PV Power source.

### Ô Môn I – Cần Thơ – 660 MW – Operating (fuel-constrained)

**Confidence: LOW.**

O Mon I is listed in PDP8 as a 660 MW plant to be fueled by Block B gas. PV GAS's December 2025 customer list does not include O Mon, suggesting it may currently operate on alternative fuel or at reduced capacity. The Block B – O Mon gas pipeline has been subject to long delays. Current operational parameters are poorly documented in admissible sources. The 660 MW capacity figure is taken from the PDP8 document; the actual delivered capacity may be lower if operating in OCGT mode or on limited fuel supply.

### Quảng Trạch 1 – Quảng Bình – 1,200 MW coal – Construction

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

A PetroVietnam project (2 × 600 MW supercritical) at the Quang Trach power center. The PDP8 lists it among coal projects to be completed by 2025–2026. News from late 2025 described the project as nearing completion. GEM GCPT (Jan 2026) classifies it as under construction. COD has been delayed from original targets.

### Quảng Trị 1 – Quảng Trị – 1,320 MW coal – Cancelled

**Confidence: HIGH.**

Originally a BOT coal project by EGATi (Thailand, subsidiary of EGAT). In October 2022, the Thai Ministry of Energy announced that the project was cancelled following nearly a decade of delays. The decision was made following financial problems and Vietnam's net zero emissions commitments. The People's Committee of Quang Tri province issued a document applying for conversion to a gas-fired power plant. As of early 2025, EVNGENCO 1 was reportedly seeking to invest in the project as an LNG plant. The coal project is definitively cancelled; the LNG successor project has no formal approval yet.

### Sông Hậu 1 – Hậu Giang – 1,200 MW coal – Operating

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

Song Hau Thermal Power Plant is an operating power station of at least 1,200 MW in Hau Giang, also known as Hau River. The project experienced long delays during construction. Unit commissioning was staggered across 2020–2022. PetroVietnam and LILAMA were lead developers. In August 2023, Viet Phat signed a VND 6,023.7 billion coal supply agreement for Song Hau 1.

### Sông Hậu 2 – Hậu Giang – 2,000 MW coal – Shelved

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence); LOW (status).**

In April 2011, Toyo Ink Group Berhad announced it would pursue the two-unit, $3.5 billion, 2,000-MW Song Hau 2 plant. In March 2023, Toyo Ventures stated that a US$86 million operation and maintenance contract had been awarded, alongside a US$2.42 billion construction contract. However, the project has not demonstrably advanced to active construction. It is listed in PDP8 but its financing and timeline remain highly uncertain. Classification as "Shelved" reflects persistent delays without formal cancellation.

### Thái Bình 2 – Thái Bình – 1,200 MW coal – Operating

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

A flagship PetroVietnam project (2 × 600 MW subcritical) that was plagued by corruption scandals and very long construction delays. Original COD was planned around 2017–2018; actual commissioning occurred in 2022–2023. The project became a high-profile case in Vietnam's anti-corruption campaigns. Now operating under PVN.

### Vân Phong 1 – Khánh Hòa – 1,432 MW coal – Operating

**Confidence: HIGH.**

Van Phong power station is an operating power station of at least 1,432 MW in Ninh Hoa, Khanh Hoa. A BOT project developed by Sumitomo Corporation (Japan) and KEPCO (Korea). Construction commenced in August 2019. The plant uses supercritical technology with 2 × 716 MW units. Commissioned in 2024 after construction delays partly attributable to COVID-19. In September 2025, Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting the foreign owners of seven coal power plants from the 15% global minimum tax.

### Vũng Áng 2 – Hà Tĩnh – 1,200 MW coal – Construction

**Confidence: HIGH.**

A BOT project with Mitsubishi (Diamond Generating Asia, 40%), KEPCO (40%), and Chugoku Electric Power (20%). The project is planned as ultra-supercritical (USC). KEPCO's participation included a US$189 million investment for a 40% stake. Construction is underway with JBIC financing. Expected COD around 2027–2028. The project has attracted environmental criticism for continued coal investment.

### Bạc Liêu LNG – Bạc Liêu – 3,200 MW – Announced

**Confidence: LOW.**

Proposed by Delta Offshore Energy (Singapore). The project envisions a large LNG-to-power complex with floating storage. It is listed in the PDP8 Implementation Plan (2024) but has not advanced significantly. Financing, regulatory approvals, and gas supply arrangements remain unresolved. The 3,200 MW capacity figure should be treated as aspirational.

### Chân Mây LNG – Thừa Thiên Huế – 4,000 MW – Announced

**Confidence: LOW.**

Developed by Chan May LNG JSC, a US-Vietnam joint venture. The power plant's capacity is 4,000 MW; construction of the US$6 billion complex was scheduled to begin in 2021, with Phase 1 of 2,400 MW expected by 2024. Status: Memorandum of understanding signed. As of 2025, no construction has commenced. The project is significantly behind its original schedule.

### Kiên Giang 1 & 2 – Kiên Giang – 1,500 MW coal – Cancelled

**Confidence: MEDIUM.**

Kien Giang 1 and 2 projects (2×750 MW) were not implemented due to unidentified fuel sources, per the official PDP8 text. These projects were originally in the revised PDP7 pipeline but were formally dropped.
