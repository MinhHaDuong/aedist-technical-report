### Explanatory Notes

**Nhiệt điện Thái Bình 2**: Existence and operating status are HIGH confidence, supported by PV Power’s official April 2023 press release confirming Unit 1 COD and PDP8 inclusion. However, the *total* capacity attribution carries residual ambiguity: while 2×660 MW is universally cited, some EPC documents reference 2×660 gross / 2×600 net. This inventory records 1320 MW consistent with PV Power’s own operational reporting convention (which typically states gross for new plants), but users should note that dispatchable net capacity may be ~1200 MW. The prolonged development history (initial approval 2011, PVC mismanagement scandal, PETROVIETNAM takeover in 2018) makes this asset critical context for understanding Vietnam’s coal project execution risks.

**Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 LNG-to-Power**: This is the highest-confidence LNG project in Vietnam’s pipeline, being the only one with signed EPC contracts, confirmed LNG SPA (Shell), and active construction as of mid-2024. Status “Construction” is HIGH confidence per PV Power’s March 2024 investor briefing. However, COD date (H2 2025) remains MEDIUM confidence because LNG terminal commissioning and gas supply synchronization have historically slipped in Vietnamese projects. This asset is pivotal as the proof-of-concept for Vietnam’s LNG-to-power transition; its success or delay will directly influence the bankability of the dozen other LNG projects in pre-permit stages.

**Block B – Ô Môn Gas-to-Power Complex**: This 3,000 MW integrated project is the largest single gas development in Vietnam’s history and anchors the southwestern power system. Status “Permitted” is MEDIUM confidence: an investment certificate exists, but the project has missed multiple FID targets (originally 2020, now projected 2025+). The June 2024 shareholder minutes confirm continued commitment but acknowledge unresolved gas pricing negotiations between Petrovietnam and ExxonMobil. Capacity (4×750 MW) is drawn from PDP8 and PV Power filings but may be optimized downward at FID. Users should treat this as a program rather than a single plant; its realization depends entirely on upstream Block B gas field development, which carries its own regulatory and commercial uncertainties.

**Long Phú 1**: Status “Construction” is MEDIUM confidence and requires qualification. Physical completion is substantial (~70%), but the Russian EPC contractor (Power Machines) became unable to perform post-2022 sanctions. PV Power’s Q2/2024 update confirms active search for replacement contractors but provides no revised COD. The project is neither formally suspended nor progressing meaningfully. It is retained as “Construction” because equipment is installed and site security/maintenance continues, distinguishing it from “Shelved” greenfield sites. This asset exemplifies the geopolitical risk exposure of Vietnam’s coal fleet under construction.

**Vân Phong 1**: Cancellation is HIGH confidence, explicitly documented in PDP8 (May 2023) and acknowledged in JERA’s 2023 sustainability report with cost write-offs. This was among the most advanced pre-COD coal projects to be cancelled under Vietnam’s Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) commitments. Its removal signals the definitive end of new coal approvals in Vietnam. The site’s potential repurposing for LNG or hydrogen remains speculative (no PPA or EIA for alternative use); users should not assume automatic conversion.

**Bạc Liêu LNG**: Confidence is LOW across all attributes. While a provincial investment certificate was issued in 2021, there is no evidence of federal-level integration into EVN’s grid planning, no approved EIA, and no verifiable track record for the Singapore-based developer (Energy Capital Vietnam). The 3,200 MW capacity appears aspirational rather than engineered. This project is included because it appears in provincial planning documents and media reports, but it should be distinguished sharply from federally-backed LNG projects like Nhơn Trạch 3&4 or Block B. Absence of primary federal documentation is the basis for LOW confidence.
