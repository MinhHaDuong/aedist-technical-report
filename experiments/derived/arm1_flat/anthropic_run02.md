## Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

### Uông Bí (Uong Bi) TPP

Confidence: LOW (existence HIGH; capacity LOW). Vietnam's oldest power station, Uong Bi, has passed a lifespan of 40 years already. The plant was built in phases from the 1960s (Soviet-era subcritical units) through a ~300 MW extension in the mid-2000s. The aggregate operating capacity of ~410 MWe is estimated from fleet arithmetic and is not confirmed by any single primary source located. Several original small units may have been retired or derated. Under PDP8's 40-year rule, the oldest units are candidates for near-term decommissioning or fuel conversion. Status capped at LOW because no source younger than 48 months independently confirms current operational capacity.

### Phả Lại 1 & 2 (Pha Lai Complex)

Confidence: MEDIUM (existence and capacity attested by UNDP 2024). The report on Pha Lai includes two coal-fired thermal power plants: Pha Lại 1 and Pha Lại 2. Pha Lai TPP 1 is the oldest of the thermal power plants, operated for approximately 40 years, with a capacity of 440MW. Pha Lai 2 (600 MW, 2×300 MW subcritical) was commissioned in 2001–2002 using Japanese ODA funding. Both plants are subjects of the UNDP-led coal transition study and are candidates for biomass co-firing, LNG conversion, or decommissioning. The 440 MW figure for Pha Lai 1 is consistent across UNDP and GEM sources. Under PDP8's framework, Pha Lai 1 exceeds 40 years of operation and is due for a conversion or retirement decision.

### Ninh Bình TPP

Confidence: LOW (existence HIGH; attributes LOW). The 100 MW subcritical plant (4×25 MW Soviet-era units) in Ninh Binh province is one of Vietnam's oldest. The World Bank representative shared their initial research results for repurposing of Ninh Binh coal-fired power plant. The UNDP 2024 technical meeting referenced a World Bank study on repurposing options. Capacity and configuration are from parametric knowledge; no primary source located confirming current operational status of all units.

### Cao Ngạn (Cao Ngan) TPP

Confidence: MEDIUM. In the case of Cao Ngan, the location is central to Thai Nguyên city; stricter environmental standards would be required. The 100 MW CFB plant is a Vinacomin mine-mouth facility and a subject of the UNDP coal transition study. The study emphasized integration with renewable energy sources as a transition pathway.

### Cẩm Phả (Cam Pha) TPP

Confidence: LOW (existence HIGH; capacity LOW). The Vinacomin-operated CFB coal plant in Quảng Ninh has an estimated aggregate capacity of ~670 MWe across two phases. The exact unit configuration (number of units, per-unit MW) could not be confirmed from any primary or secondary source in this search. The estimate of ~670 MW is derived from fleet arithmetic reconciling the ~24.7 GW total operating coal capacity reported by GEM in January 2023 against individually confirmed plant capacities. The plant appears in PDP8 project lists but specific capacity data was not retrievable.

### Mông Dương 2 (Mong Duong 2) BOT

Confidence: MEDIUM. In 2015, AES commissioned The 1,240 MW Mong Duong 2 coal-fired power plant in the Quang Ninh province (northern Vietnam). This is a BOT project developed by AES Corporation. The 2×620 MW subcritical plant was one of the largest foreign-invested coal projects in Vietnam. AES subsequently sold its stake; current ownership should be verified against AES filings.

### Vân Phong 1 (Van Phong 1) BOT

Confidence: HIGH. PDP8 listed Van Phong I among the projects under construction, with an updated capacity of 1,432 MW. In September 2023, construction of Unit 1 was completed. Final inspection work was underway to prepare for commercial operation. In October 2023, Unit 1 was approved for commercial operation. In December 2023, Unit 2 was approved for commercial operation. Unit 1 was officially inaugurated in December 2023. Unit 2 was officially inaugurated in March 2024. The 2×716 MW supercritical BOT plant in Khánh Hòa was developed by a consortium of Sumitomo Corporation and KEPCO, with $2.5 billion in project financing including JBIC, ADB, and commercial banks. It is one of the last new coal plants to be commissioned in Vietnam under PDP8. An October 2025 report noted Aboitiz Power (Philippines) acquiring a 25% stake for $220 million.

### Sông Hậu 1 & 2 (Song Hau)

Confidence: MEDIUM (Song Hau 1); MEDIUM (Song Hau 2). Song Hau Thermal Power Plant is an operating power station of at least 1200-megawatts (MW) in Hau Giang Industrial Park, Mai Dam, Chau Thanh, Hau Giang, Vietnam. It is also known as Hau River. Song Hau 1 (2×600 MW, subcritical) suffered significant construction delays and was originally a PVN project with JICA involvement.

Song Hau 2 (2×1,000 MW) is a separate $3.5 billion BOT project by Malaysian company Toyo Ventures. In March 2023, Toyo Ventures stated that a US$86 million operation and maintenance contract for Sông Hậu-2 had been awarded. This was in addition to a US$2.42 billion construction contract awarded one week prior. Toyo Ventures also stated that the Export-Import Bank of Malaysia had offered to be the mandated lead arranger. The project was expected to be operational within four and a half years of construction start. However, the project is listed among delayed/abandoned projects in PDP8, and its current status remains uncertain. Status is Shelved pending further confirmation.

### Công Thanh (Cong Thanh)

Confidence: MEDIUM (coal project cancelled); MEDIUM (LNG replacement announced). In March 2011, land clearing began on the two-unit, 600-MW, $620 million Cong Thanh power station in the Nghi Son Economic Zone. The coal project stalled for over a decade due to unspecified reasons and site conflicts with the Nghi Son Refinery. For the period 2031 – 2035, LNG Cong Thanh with a capacity of 1500 MW is updated. This project may be accelerated to the period 2026 – 2030 depending on the demands of the power system. Under the Revised PDP8, the coal project has been formally replaced by a 1,500 MW LNG CCGT.

### Quảng Trị (Quang Tri) TPP

Confidence: HIGH (cancellation well-documented). In October 2022, the Thai Ministry of Energy announced that the project was cancelled following nearly a decade of delays. The decision was made following recommendations from the provincial People's Committee, financial problems, and Vietnam's net zero emissions commitments. Official cancellation documents for the Vietnamese Ministry of Industry and Trade and Quảng Trị Province were on their way. Originally a 2×660 MW supercritical BOT project by Thailand's EGATi, with KOMIPO (Korea) involvement that was later withdrawn. The February 2025 reports indicate EVNGENCO 1 is seeking to invest in a replacement LNG project at the same location (Hai Lang LNG). The coal Quang Tri project is classified as Cancelled.

### Quảng Trạch 1 (Quang Trach 1) & Long Phú 1 (Long Phu 1)

Confidence: MEDIUM (both listed in PDP8 construction pipeline). There are currently 6.13GW of coal power plants under construction, including Na Duong II, An Khanh–Bac Giang, Vung Ang I, Quang Trach I, Van Phong I and Long Phu. Both are 2×600 MW subcritical coal plants. Quang Trach 1 (Quảng Bình, EVN/EVNGENCO 1) and Long Phu 1 (Sóc Trăng, PVN) have experienced significant delays from their original PDP7 schedules. They are among the last coal projects expected to be commissioned in Vietnam before the PDP8 coal moratorium. CODs are estimated at 2025–2027 but face risk of further delay.

### Vũng Áng 2 (Vung Ang 2) BOT

Confidence: MEDIUM. Korean Electric Power Company (KEPCO) purchased a 40% share in Vietnam's 1.2 GW Vung Ang-2 power station. The 2×600 MW supercritical BOT project in Hà Tĩnh is backed by KEPCO and Mitsubishi, with Korea Ex-Im Bank financing. Construction status as of 2023 was active, though specific progress updates since then are sparse. Under international coal financing commitments (Japan, Korea, G7), this project is among the last to secure legacy funding.

### Nhơn Trạch 1 & 2 (Nhon Trach 1 & 2) – Capacity Discrepancy

Nhon Trach 1 and 2 are separate CCGT plants at the Nhon Trach power center in Đồng Nai, both operated by PV Power. The Nhon Trach 1 Thermal Power Plant is a natural gas-fired power plant located in Phu Huu Ward, Nhon Trach City, Dong Nai Province, Vietnam. The plant, invested and operated by the Vietnam Oil and Gas Group (PVN), has a total installed capacity of 1,320 MW. Construction of the plant commenced in 2007, and it began commercial operation in 2012. However, the 1,320 MW figure from iLotusLand likely represents the combined NT1 + NT2 capacity rather than NT1 alone. A separate source (database.earth) reports 1,215 MW for NT1. The table uses an estimated ~450 MW for NT1 and ~750 MW for NT2 based on standard CCGT block configurations known for PV Power plants, but these figures carry LOW confidence. Users should consult PV Power annual reports for definitive per-plant capacities.

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Nhon Trach 3 & 4) – Vietnam's First LNG CCGT

Confidence: HIGH. GE Vernova has announced that PetroVietnam Power Corporation's (PV Power) Nhon Trach 3 and 4 power plants have officially entered commercial operation. Located at Ong Keo Industrial Park in Dai Phuoc Commune, around 70 km south-east of Ho Chi Minh City, the twin plants represent Vietnam's first foray into LNG-fired electricity generation. With a combined installed capacity of 1.6 GW, the project is expected to play a central role in meeting rising power demand. Delivered by an EPC consortium comprising Samsung C&T and Vietnamese contractor Lilama, the project deploys GE Vernova's HA-class gas turbine technology. Each plant is equipped with advanced 9HA.02 gas turbines, which enable the facility to achieve combined-cycle efficiency of more than 63%. LNG is supplied from the Thi Vai regasification terminal in Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu. The Nhon Trach 3 plant, which was connected to the national grid on February 5, is scheduled to start commercial operations in August, while Nhon Trach 4 is expected to follow in November. Both plants achieved COD in 2025. Total investment: ~$1.4 billion.

### Ô Môn Power Center (O Mon 1–4) & Block B Gas

Confidence: MEDIUM (O Mon 1 operating); MEDIUM (O Mon 2–4 announced). O Mon 1 (660 MW CCGT) is operational in Cần Thơ city using gas from the PM3-CAA field. The Southwest Region: Quickly bring gas from Block B to the shore in 2015 to provide fuel for power plants at the O Mon power center with a total capacity of 2,850 MW. O Mon 2, 3, and 4 (each ~750 MW) are contingent on the Block B – Ô Môn gas pipeline, which has been delayed by over a decade. First gas from Block B is now targeted for late 2026–2027. Until upstream gas is secured, these three plants cannot proceed to construction, though they remain in PDP8 and the Revised PDP8.

### Sơn Mỹ 2 (Son My 2) – AES LNG Project

Confidence: MEDIUM. The Vietnamese government has approved the investment policy of the 2,250 MW Son My 2 CCGT power plant project. The power plant, developed by AES Vietnam, is estimated to require an investment of US$1.8bn. The construction of the CCGT plant is planned to begin in 2023 and commissioning is scheduled for 2025. Note the location discrepancy: Enerdata states "Quy Nhon, Binh Dinh province" but the Son My site is in Bình Thuận province; this appears to be an error in the Enerdata article. The project complements the Son My LNG import terminal being developed by AES and PV Gas. As of 2025, construction timelines have slipped significantly. Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for domestic gas and LNG-fired power capacity by a combined 25.2 gigawatts (GW). Two projects have disclosed turbine contracts but still face other significant barriers to financial closure.

### Bạc Liêu LNG (Bac Lieu LNG)

Confidence: LOW. The 3,200 MW floating LNG-to-power project was proposed by Singapore-based Delta Offshore Energy. It was included in PDP8 with an ambitious target price of 7 USc/kWh. Multiple independent analyses (IEEFA, BloombergNEF) have questioned the project's technical and financial viability. The floating storage and regasification unit (FSRU) concept and the scale of the project are unprecedented in Vietnam. No turbine contracts or financing commitments have been publicly disclosed as of 2025. Status is classified as Announced, but effective progress appears negligible.

### Phú Mỹ Complex (Phu My 1 / 2.1 / 2.2 / 3 / 4)

Confidence: HIGH for Phu My 2.2 and 3 (two independent concordant sources); MEDIUM for others. The Phu My power complex is the largest group of power generation plants in Vietnam, with a combined capacity of up to 3,900MW. It is located in the village of Phu My in the Ba Ria-Vung Tau province of southern Vietnam. The five-plant complex was built in phases from 1997 to 2005. Phu My 2.2 (715 MW, EDF/Mekong Energy BOT) and Phu My 3 (746 MW, SembCorp BOT) were Vietnam's first foreign BOT gas power projects. Due to exhaustion of gas sources in the Southeastern region, the existing power plants as Phu My, Ba Ria, Hiep Phuoc with a total capacity of about 4,200 MW will face gas supply challenges, potentially requiring conversion to LNG or decommissioning. The complex remains operationally critical, providing a large share of southern Vietnam's baseload power.

### Revised PDP8 (Decision 768, April 2025) – New LNG Projects

On 15 April 2025, the Prime Minister of Vietnam issued Decision 768/QD-TTg approving the revision to the National Power Development Plan. This decision supersedes Decision 500/QD-TTg dated 15 May 2023 approving the original PDP8. The Revised PDP8 introduced several new LNG-to-power projects: For the period 2025 – 2030, LNG Hai Phong Phase I and LNG Hiep Phuoc Phase II has been updated with the estimated capacity, respectively, 1600 MW and 1500 MW. For the 2031–2035 reserve period, five additional 1,500 MW projects were designated. These include the Extended Ca Mau Plant and others that will proceed only if needed to compensate for delays in other generation sources. All Revised PDP8 additions carry MEDIUM confidence (single primary source).

## Aggregate Statistics (Prose)

The inventory above comprises approximately 80 asset records spanning all lifecycle stages. Summing nominal capacities across operating coal plants yields roughly 25–26 GW, consistent with GEM's January 2023 estimate of ~24.7 GW prior to the commissioning of Van Phong 1 (1,432 MW) in late 2023. Operating domestic gas CCGT/OCGT capacity totals approximately 8.0–8.5 GW, dominated by the Phú Mỹ complex (~3,900 MW) and the Cà Mau, Nhơn Trạch, and Ô Môn plants. LNG-fired CCGT capacity that has reached commercial operation as of early 2026 is limited to Nhon Trach 3 & 4 (1,620 MW). The pipeline of announced or pre-permit LNG projects totals approximately 30–35 GW, but as IEEFA noted in October 2025, Vietnam is likely to miss its PDP8 2030 LNG capacity target of 22.4 GW by a substantial margin due to turbine procurement backlogs, regulatory delays, and bankability challenges. Coal projects under construction total approximately 4.7 GW (Quang Trach 1, Long Phu 1, Vung Ang 2, Na Duong 2, An Khanh–Bac Giang). Cancelled or shelved coal projects represent approximately 10–12 GW of capacity that was at various stages of development before being abandoned under PDP8 and market/financial pressures.
