### Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

**Vung Ang 2 Thermal Power Plant**
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Summary:** Inaugurated April 2026, this $2.2bn BOT project is Vietnam’s first ultra-supercritical (USC) coal plant, developed by VAPCO (OneEnergy Ltd, REE, CLP, Mitsubishi). It is a key milestone in Vietnam’s coal fleet modernization, but faces scrutiny over its long-term role in the energy mix as Vietnam pivots to LNG and renewables.

**Vinh Tan 3 Thermal Power Plant**
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** As of January 2026, the status of Vĩnh Tân-3 remains unclear. The project was initially planned as a 1,500 MW supercritical coal plant, but faced financing challenges after HSBC withdrew as financial advisor in 2020. Mitsubishi continues to categorize it as “under development,” but no recent construction progress has been reported.

**Hai Phong 3 Thermal Power Plant**
- **Confidence:** LOW
- **Summary:** Originally planned as a 1,200 MW supercritical coal plant, Hải Phòng-3 was proposed for conversion to LNG in 2020. No progress has been reported since, and the project is presumed shelved. The PDP8 does not include new coal plants, making its future uncertain.

**Vung Ang 3 Thermal Power Plant**
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** Vũng Áng-3 was proposed for conversion from coal to gas in 2020, with capacity potentially increasing from 2.4 GW to 4.8 GW. Samsung requested a refund of its investment, and the project is now considered shelved under PDP8, though not formally cancelled.

**Ninh Thuan 1 & 2 Nuclear Power Plants**
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (Ninh Thuan 1), LOW (Ninh Thuan 2)
- **Summary:** After being cancelled in 2016, Vietnam’s nuclear program was revived in 2024. Ninh Thuan 1 is now permitted, with an intergovernmental agreement signed with Russia in March 2026 for two VVER-1200 reactors. Ninh Thuan 2 remains in the pre-feasibility stage, with negotiations ongoing until May 2026.

**Long Son LNG Power Plant**
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** Originally planned as a coal plant, Long Sơn was approved for conversion to LNG under PDP8. The project is now in the permitted stage, with a proposed capacity of 4,800 MW and COD targeted for 2025–2030.
