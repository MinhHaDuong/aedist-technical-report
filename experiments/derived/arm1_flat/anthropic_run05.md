## Per-Asset Explanatory Notes

### 1. Uông Bí TPP (Uong Bi)

**Confidence: LOW (existence HIGH; capacity LOW).** Vietnam commenced construction of Uong Bi thermal power plant in 1961 with Soviet assistance, making it the main power source for the North. The station "has passed a lifespan of 40 years already" per the Green FDC PDP8 analysis. The plant has gone through multiple renovation and expansion phases (the "Uông Bí mở rộng" phases), with different generations of units from the 1960s through the 2000s. Exact current operating capacity is uncertain because the oldest Soviet-era units have likely been derated or retired; the ~630 MW total is an estimate from parametric knowledge. No primary source for current operating capacity was located in this review. PDP8 envisions all coal plants converting to biomass/ammonia or shutting down by 2050.

### 2. Phả Lại Complex (Pha Lai 1 & 2)

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The UNDP transition report confirms Pha Lai TPP 1 has a capacity of 440 MW and has been operated for approximately 40 years. Pha Lai 2 (600 MW, 2×300 MW) was commissioned around 2001–2002 with Japanese-supplied turbines. The power units of the Pha Lai Power Complex started operations in the 1980s and will exceed 40 years of operation within this decade, making them priority candidates for early coal transition. The UNDP studied Pha Lai alongside Cao Ngan and Van Phong for transition pathways including biomass co-firing, LNG conversion, and BESS integration.

### 3. Na Dương 1 & 2 (Na Duong)

**Confidence: Na Duong 1 HIGH; Na Duong 2 MEDIUM.** Na Dương-1, a two-unit, 100-MW coal-fired power plant owned by Vinacomin, began construction in April 2002, and was completed in 2005. The mine-to-mouth plant uses coal from the nearby Na Dương coal mine. GEM Wiki confirms biomass co-firing experiments in October 2025. Na Duong 2 (~110 MW, also CFB) was listed among plants under construction per the EIC 2023 article, but GEM Wiki cites an October 2023 Vietnamese-language report noting "3 difficult questions remain" for the project, suggesting it may face delays or obstacles that could push its status closer to Shelved.

### 4. Vũng Áng 1 & 2 (Vung Ang)

**Confidence: HIGH.** Vung Ang 1 (1,200 MW, PV Power) has been operating since 2014–2015. GEM Wiki references confirm Unit 1 COD and subsequent commercial operations, with Ha Tinh provincial press reporting annual coal consumption of approximately 3 million tonnes. Vung Ang 2 (1,200 MW, Mitsubishi/KEPCO/Shikoku Electric BOT, $2.2 billion) is Vietnam's most technologically advanced coal plant, using USC technology. In September 2025, Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting the foreign owners of seven coal power plants from the requirement to pay a 15% global minimum corporate tax rate. Unit 1 entered commercial operation in July 2025 per Năng lượng Việt Nam Online. A storm in September 2025 collapsed the coal warehouse roof, requiring repairs through November 2025.

### 5. Mông Dương 2 (Mong Duong 2)

**Confidence: HIGH.** In 2015, AES commissioned the 1,240 MW Mong Duong 2 coal-fired power plant in the Quang Ninh province (northern Vietnam). This is a BOT project and one of the first large-scale foreign-invested coal IPPs in Vietnam. AES has since explored divestment. The plant uses supercritical technology and imported coal.

### 6. Vĩnh Tân Complex (Vinh Tan 1, 2, 3, 4, 4 Extension)

**Confidence: Operating units HIGH; VT3 MEDIUM (shelved).** Vinh Tan power station is an operating power station of at least 4,244 megawatts (MW) comprising VT1 (1,200 MW, Chinese BOT), VT2 (1,244 MW, EVN), VT4 (1,200 MW, EVN), and VT4 Extension (600 MW, EVN). In March 2019, Vietnam's State Audit Agency found that the 1,200 MW Vinh Tan 1 plant had violated environmental laws, including unapproved cooling water discharge and excess NOx emissions. In February 2021, Mitsubishi announced withdrawal from Vinh Tan-3, citing its climate targets. VT3 (planned ~1,980 MW, 3×660 MW) has been shelved since; Indian investor interest was reported in August 2024 per Vietnamese press, but no confirmed developer has been assigned. The capacity of VT3 is disputed: some sources cite 2×660 MW = 1,320 MW rather than 3×660 MW.

### 7. Duyên Hải Complex (Duyen Hai 1, 2, 3, 3 Extension)

**Confidence: HIGH for DH1/2/3; MEDIUM for DH3 Extension capacity.** Duyen Hai Power Generation Complex is an operating power station of at least 4,378 megawatts (MW) in Tra Vinh. The Duyen Hai Power Plant I & III is a 2,488 MW thermal power project commissioned in 2016. The Duyen Hai Power Plant II thermal project with a capacity of 1,320 MW came online in 2021, developed by Teknik Janakuasa (Malaysia) as a BOT. The DH3 Extension capacity is recorded here as ~660 MW to approximate the GEM total of 4,378 MW, though an arithmetic reconciliation with the individually cited units (1,244 + 1,244 + 1,320 + 660 = 4,468) shows a ~90 MW discrepancy, likely attributable to gross-versus-net differences or rounding.

### 8. Long Phú 1 (Long Phu 1)

**Confidence: MEDIUM (existence and capacity); status uncertain.** Long Phu is listed among 6.13 GW of coal power plants under construction. The 1,200 MW project in Sóc Trăng has been severely delayed, primarily because the original EPC contractor, Russia's Power Machines, was sanctioned by the United States. PV Power/PVN has been seeking an alternative contractor. The project was retained in PDP8 but faces an uncertain timeline. If the financial and contractor issues are not resolved, the project could move to Shelved or Cancelled status.

### 9. Nam Định 1 (Nam Dinh 1 / Hai Hau)

**Confidence: HIGH for existence and status; MEDIUM for capacity.** In 2013, Nam Định provincial authorities said they would support the construction of a 2,400-MW coal plant by the Korean Taekwang Vina Company. The first phase (1,200 MW, 2×600 MW) was being actively pursued with ACWA Power. In December 2019 ACWA Power and Taekwang Power signed a $1.89 billion Sinosure-backed loan with Chinese banks, but the project has not reached financial close. PDP8 required financial resolution by June 2024; the project appears to remain shelved as of late 2025.

### 10. Công Thanh (Cong Thanh) — Coal to LNG Conversion

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** In March 2011, land clearing began on the two-unit, 600-MW, $620 million Cong Thanh power station in the Nghi Son Economic Zone. The coal project stalled. In November 2022, the People's Committee of Thanh Hoa advocated for the project to be converted to natural gas. In August 2023, capital was reportedly arranged with the UK-based private equity firm Actis for the project to progress using combined-cycle gas turbine technology, now at 1,500 MW. However, no financial close has been confirmed.

### 11. Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Nhon Trach 3 & 4) — Vietnam's First LNG Plant

**Confidence: HIGH.** PetroVietnam Power (PV Power) started initial operation of the first of two 812-MW LNG-fired units at its Nhon Trach power plant, the first power plant in Vietnam to be built specifically to be powered by LNG. With a combined installed capacity of 1.6 GW, the project deploys GE Vernova's HA-class gas turbine technology, delivered by an EPC consortium of Samsung C&T and Lilama. Each plant is equipped with advanced 9HA.02 gas turbines achieving >63% combined-cycle efficiency. The capacity discrepancy (1,624 MW from POWER Mag vs. 1,760 MW from earlier Vietnamese Energy Magazine reporting vs. 1.6 GW rounded from Energy Institute) reflects design-stage revisions; the as-built figure of 2×812 MW = 1,624 MW is used here. LNG is sourced from the PV Gas Thi Vai terminal. International banks Citi and ING led a $521.5 million financing package.

### 12. Sơn Mỹ 2 (Son My 2) — AES LNG

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The Vietnamese government approved the investment policy of the 2,250 MW Son My 2 CCGT power plant project, developed by AES Vietnam, estimated to require an investment of US$1.8 bn. Commissioning was originally scheduled for 2025, but significant delays are expected. IEEFA notes Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for domestic gas and LNG-fired power capacity by a combined 25.2 GW, and that turbine backlogs add to regulatory and bankability challenges.

### 13. Quảng Ninh LNG (Quang Ninh LNG)

**Confidence: HIGH.** Tokyo Gas and Marubeni have formed a joint venture with PetroVietnam and Colavi to develop a 1.5 GW CCGT power plant and an onshore LNG import terminal in Cam Pha, Quang Ninh province. The JV aims to start commercial operation in the second half of 2027. This is one of the more advanced LNG projects in Vietnam, with a formalised JV and stated timeline. However, gas turbine supply constraints and regulatory issues may still cause delays.

### 14. Quỳnh Lập LNG (Quynh Lap LNG) — SK Innovation

**Confidence: HIGH.** The Quynh Lap LNG project, located in Nghe An province, will consist of a 1,500 MW gas combined cycle power plant and a 250,000 m³ LNG terminal. Construction will begin in 2027; the $2.3 billion project is slated for completion in 2030. The site was previously allocated to a cancelled coal project (Quỳnh Lập 1). This represents the first time SK Innovation is replicating its Korean LNG value-chain model abroad.

### 15. Bạc Liêu LNG (Bac Lieu LNG) — Delta Offshore Energy

**Confidence: LOW.** The 3,200 MW proposal by Singapore-based Delta Offshore Energy was reported in the Vietnam Energy Magazine in 2020 with a target COD of 2025–2026 and an electricity price of 7 US cents/kWh. IEEFA notes that turbine bottlenecks add to regulatory and bankability challenges delaying LNG-fired plants in Vietnam, and identifies this as one of the projects most unlikely to proceed on its stated timeline. As of mid-2026, there is no evidence the project has reached financial close, secured turbine orders, or begun construction.

### 16. Ô Môn Power Centre (O Mon 2, 3, 4) — Block B Gas

**Confidence: LOW (capacity and COD).** The O Mon Power Centre in Cần Thơ currently has only O Mon 1 (~660 MW CCGT) in operation. MOECO took FID on the Block B Project, comprising an upstream gas field and pipeline to fuel a gas-fired power plant, with first gas expected by the end of 2026. O Mon 2, 3, and 4 (combined estimated ~2,550 MW) are contingent on Block B gas delivery. Individual capacities are approximate (from parametric knowledge) and have not been confirmed from primary sources in this review. PDP8 lists these in its domestic gas project tables.

## Aggregate Fleet Summary

Vietnam's operating thermal fleet as inventoried above comprises approximately 25–27 GW of coal-fired capacity and approximately 7–8 GW of domestic-gas-fired capacity. As of January 2023, the Global Coal Plant Tracker estimated Vietnam had 24.7 GW in coal capacity, with a further 6.1 GW under construction. Coal-fired power capacity is capped at 31,055 MW through 2030 under the revised PDP8, with no new coal projects to be approved thereafter. LNG capacity is projected to reach 22,524 MW by 2030, accounting for 9.5–12.3% of the national power mix, though Vietnam is likely to miss its 2030 targets for gas and LNG-fired power by a combined 25.2 GW per IEEFA's October 2025 assessment. The only major new LNG capacity to come online in recent years is Nhon Trach 3 and 4, which provides ~1.6 GW and entered commercial operation in 2025. A further approximately 13 GW of cancelled coal capacity was removed from the pipeline under PDP8. The PDP8, sets a firmer roadmap for fuel conversion: all coal plants must convert to biomass/ammonia or shut down by 2050, with a total converted capacity of 25,798 MW.
