**Aggregate statistics (prose).** As of 2024, EVN reported Vietnam's total installed power capacity at 82,387 MW, of which coal-fired power totalled 26,757 MW (32.5 %) and gas- and oil-fired power 8,653 MW (10.5 %). The inventory above accounts for approximately 24,600 MW of identifiable operating coal capacity across 31 entries and roughly 8,500 MW of identifiable operating gas/oil capacity across 15 entries. The ~2,100 MW gap between inventoried operating coal and EVN's aggregate figure is attributed to (a) smaller Vinacomin/TKV plants whose individual capacities could not be independently verified, (b) industrial captive plants at steel, cement, and pulp/paper complexes not individually identified, and (c) rounding and gross/net differences in reported capacities. At the end of September 2022, Vietnam had 39 coal-fired power plants with a total capacity of 24,674 MW in operation, suggesting several additional smaller plants exist beyond those inventoried here.

Under PDP8, thermal power sources to be developed from 2021 to 2030 include domestic gas (14,930 MW), LNG (22,400 MW), and coal (30,127 MW) of total planned capacity. The PDP8 cancelled around 13 GW of planned coal capacity from the pipeline while retaining approximately 13 GW for completion. Under the April 2025 Amended PDP8, coal capacity shows a modest 3 % increase over the original plan, but coal's share of the power mix declines to 13–16.9 %, down from 20 % under the original PDP8.

For gas-fired generation, of the approximately 14 gas-fired power projects then underway, only Nhon Trach 3 and 4 were expected to start operations in Q2–Q3 2025. PV Power's Nhon Trach 3 and 4 plants have officially entered commercial operation with a combined 1.6 GW, representing Vietnam's first LNG-fired electricity generation.

## Per-asset explanatory notes

### Phả Lại 1 & 2 (Pha Lai 1 & 2)

**Existence/status: HIGH.** Pha Lại 1 is the oldest of Vietnam's major coal-fired thermal power plants, operated for approximately 40 years, with a capacity of 440 MW. Pha Lai 2, with a capacity of 600 MW and 23 years of operation, is also required to undergo transition. Both are operated by EVNGENCO2 (Power Generation Corporation 2). Pha Lai 1 is on the PDP8 decommissioning list (Decision 500/QD-TTg). UNDP has studied transition pathways including conversion to gas turbines with BESS, or biomass/SynCon. The 4×110 MW subcritical units of Pha Lai 1 were built with Soviet assistance and commissioned 1983–1986. Pha Lai 2 uses 2×300 MW subcritical units commissioned around 2001, making it roughly mid-life. Capacity: HIGH confidence (two concordant sources: UNDP 2024 and EVN 2023).

### Thái Bình 2 (Thai Binh 2)

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** Originally planned for commissioning circa 2017, Thai Binh 2 experienced severe cost overruns and schedule delays under PVN's management. The Thai Binh 2 project is a 1,200 MW coal-fired power plant being developed by PVN. Despite delays and financial difficulties, the project is nearing completion. Based on parametric knowledge, Unit 1 was synchronized to the grid in late 2022 and Unit 2 in 2023. The plant's troubled history became a focal point for PVN governance reform. Status is capped at MEDIUM because the most recent detailed source (the-shiv, Feb 2026) still described it as "nearing completion," potentially reflecting outdated information on that specific plant. Capacity (2×600 MW = 1,200 MW) is consistent across sources.

### Long Phú 1 (Long Phu 1)

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** Long Phu 1 in Sóc Trăng province is one of the most troubled coal projects in Vietnam. The 1,200 MW (2×600 MW) plant was contracted with Russian firm Power Machines (OMZ/Silovye Mashiny) as EPC contractor. International sanctions on Russian entities following 2022 severely disrupted equipment delivery and construction. The project has been effectively stalled for years. PDP8 retained it in the coal pipeline, but commercial operation date remains uncertain. The plant is listed as "Construction" because PDP8 recognises it as under construction, but physical progress is minimal. Confidence on COD: LOW.

### Vân Phong 1 (Van Phong 1)

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** The Van Phong 1 BOT Coal-fired Power Plant is described as "a new and largest coal-firing power plant." The 1,320 MW (2×660 MW) supercritical BOT plant in Khánh Hòa's Van Phong Economic Zone is developed by a consortium led by Sumitomo Corporation (Japan) through KPJB. The plant was commissioned approximately 2023–2024. Legal issues related to the BOT contract will arise with any proposed transition solution, requiring renegotiation. The supercritical technology makes it the most efficient coal plant in Vietnam's fleet. COD is estimated at 2023–2024 based on the UNDP's present-tense description in March 2024; no explicit COD announcement was found.

### Na Dương 2 (Na Duong 2)

**Existence/status: HIGH.** PDP8 listed Na Duong II as under construction and would be completed. However, the project has faced significant delays. Following two failed bidding periods, bidding for a 3,315 billion VND contract package was opened in 2023, with target commercial operation remaining 2026. The 110 MW CFB unit is a mine-mouth plant using local Na Dương lignite. Should Na Dương-2 be commissioned, Na Dương-1 reportedly risked being too close to the mine's edge due to necessary mine expansions. Construction progress updates from Vinacomin in 2024–2025 confirm the project is active.

### Quảng Trị coal (cancelled) → potential LNG redevelopment

**Existence/status: HIGH (cancellation confirmed).** In October 2022, the Thai Ministry of Energy announced that the project was cancelled following nearly a decade of delays, financial problems, and Vietnam's net zero emissions commitments. The original 1,320 MW (2×660 MW) supercritical coal project in Hải Lăng, Quảng Trị had been proposed under BOT by Thai developers. EVN has subsequently sought to invest in the 1,320 MW site, and Quảng Trị provincial authorities have actively engaged with proposals to redevelop the site, potentially as an LNG-to-power project. As of late 2025, the redevelopment path was unresolved. The coal version is recorded as "Cancelled"; any LNG successor would be a new project.

### Nhơn Trạch 3 & 4 (Nhon Trach 3 & 4)

**Existence/status: HIGH.** Vietnam's first purpose-built LNG power plants. Located in the southern province of Dong Nai, the two plants have combined investment capital of about $1.4 billion and total capacity of 1,624 MW. PV Power is the investor, with a consortium of Lilama and Samsung C&T as the EPC contractor. GE provides two blocks of more than 800 MW each including GE 9HA.02 gas turbines. Inaugurated in December 2025 and scheduled for commercial operations in early 2026. Fuel is supplied via the Thi Vai LNG terminal operated by PV GAS. A $521.5 million facility agreement was signed for Nhon Trach 3–4 on October 1, 2024, with additional earlier financing tranches. The project was the first power project in Vietnam to secure international loans exceeding $1 billion without a government guarantee. Capacity: HIGH confidence (concordant across GE Vernova, PV Power, and multiple press sources).

### Block B – Ô Môn gas chain

**Status: MEDIUM (for the chain as a programme); individual plant status: LOW.** The Block B gas field (blocks 48/95 and 52/97B, offshore Southwest Vietnam) is the cornerstone of Vietnam's domestic gas strategy. The Block B project entails an upstream gas field offshore Southwest Vietnam and a pipeline linking it to O Mon, a gas-fired thermal power plant complex 400 kilometers away. The downstream complex centres on the Ô Môn power centre in Cần Thơ: Ô Môn I (660 MW CCGT conversion, using the existing OCGT facility), plus new Ô Môn II, III, and IV plants (~750 MW each). PDP8 explicitly identifies O Mon I at 660 MW using Block B gas. The existing Ô Môn I OCGT (~330 MW, operational since 2009) currently runs on domestic pipeline gas. The Block B–O Mon and Hiep Phuoc 1 plants, with a combined capacity of 6,634 MW, are considered the only projects besides Nhon Trach 3&4 that could become commercially operational before 2030. Individual plant capacities for O Mon II–IV are estimated at ~750 MW based on parametric knowledge of typical CCGT configurations and PDP8 aggregate targets; exact figures have not been independently confirmed. Block B gas field development has faced repeated delays since the early 2010s; downstream plant CODs are contingent on upstream gas availability.

### Ca Voi Xanh (Blue Whale) – Dung Quất gas chain

**Status: LOW (individual plants not yet advanced).** The partners in the production sharing contract for blocks 117–119 holding the Ca Voi Xanh gas field are ExxonMobil, EMEPVL, and PVEP. The gas is expected to power the Dung Quat Power Complex, comprising three CCGT plants each of about 750 MW. Dung Quat I and III are developed by EVN, Dung Quat II by Sembcorp, with a total capacity of 2,250 MW. The original schedule called for first gas by 2023–2024, but this has been substantially delayed due to commercial negotiations and geopolitical sensitivities in the South China Sea. No final investment decision has been publicly confirmed for either the upstream or downstream components. Capacities are approximate.

### Sơn Mỹ LNG (Son My)

**Status: MEDIUM (for existence); LOW (for development timeline).** The Son My complex in Bình Thuận province is a large-scale integrated LNG import and power generation project. Son My involves AES Corporation, PetroVietnam, and local partners, consisting of an LNG import terminal and gas-fired power plants with a total planned capacity of over 4,500 MW. This is split into Son My I (~2,250 MW, AES-led) and Son My II (~2,250 MW, developer uncertain). The project has been in development for over a decade with limited physical progress. Development of the Son My LNG Terminal for Son My 1 and 2 LNG to Power projects will be continued as planned under PDP7 Revised. Exact per-plant capacities are not confirmed; the ~2,250 MW figure per plant is estimated from the total.

### Mông Dương 2 BOT (Mong Duong 2)

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** Korean Electric Power Company (KEPCO) purchased a 15 % share in the 2.0 GW Jawa-9 and Jawa-10 power stations, and purchased a 40 % share in Vietnam's 1.2 GW Vung Ang-2 power station. Note: the Boom and Bust 2021 reference mentions Vung Ang 2, not Mong Duong 2. Mong Duong 2 (1,240 MW, 2×620 MW) was developed by AES Corporation in BOT arrangement with POSCO Energy and China Investment Corporation. AES subsequently divested its stake. The plant has been operating since 2015. Capacity of 1,240 MW is from GEM-GCPT and parametric knowledge; gross/net distinction not specified. The BOT concession period is typically 25 years. Ownership changes have occurred; current operator details may differ from original consortium.

### Sông Hậu 1 (Song Hau 1)

**Existence/status: MEDIUM.** Song Hau 1 (1,200 MW, 2×600 MW subcritical) in Hậu Giang province had one of the most troubled construction histories among Vietnamese coal plants. The project was managed by PVN with Lilama (Vietnam Machinery Erection Corporation) as the main EPC contractor. It experienced severe cost overruns (reportedly exceeding the original budget by more than 40 %) and schedule delays of several years. Unit 1 was commissioned approximately 2020 and Unit 2 in 2022. The plant's ownership/operator may have been transferred from PVN to another entity. EVN's 2023 overview implicitly includes it in the operating fleet. Capacity: MEDIUM confidence.

### Bạc Liêu LNG (Bac Lieu LNG)

**Existence/status: LOW.** The approximately 3,200 MW LNG-to-power project proposed by Delta Offshore Energy (a Singapore-registered entity) in Bạc Liêu province has been one of Vietnam's most controversial energy proposals. While included in PDP7 revised and retained in PDP8's LNG pipeline, the project has faced persistent doubts about the developer's financial capacity, the absence of a signed PPA, and questions about economic viability. No significant construction has occurred. The project is listed as "Announced" rather than "Pre-permit" because the regulatory pathway remains unclear. Capacity figures (~3,200 MW) come from project announcements; no independent verification is available.

### Nghi Sơn 2 BOT (Nghi Son 2)

**Capacity note: MEDIUM.** Nghi Son coal power station is described as an operating station of at least 1,920 MW. With Nghi Son 1 at 600 MW (2×300 MW), the implied capacity of Nghi Son 2 is ~1,320 MW. This is consistent with 2×660 MW supercritical units typical of Japanese-Korean BOT coal plants in Southeast Asia. The Nghi Son 2 BOT is led by Marubeni Corporation and KEPCO. Some sources may cite the station total as 1,800 MW (if NS2 = 1,200 MW), but GEM's "at least 1,920 MW" suggests the 1,320 MW figure for NS2 is more accurate. The discrepancy is unresolved and flagged. In September 2025, Vietnam's Ministry of Finance proposed exempting the foreign owners of seven coal power plants from the requirement to pay a 15 % global minimum corporate tax rate.
